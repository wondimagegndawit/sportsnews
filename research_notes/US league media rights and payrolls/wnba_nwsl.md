## WNBA Key Question 1: Current national media rights deals and combined league-wide AAV (2026)

### Takeaway
The WNBA's 11-year rights cycle that began with the 2026 season is now reported (Front Office Sports, May 11, 2026, citing a source familiar with the deals) to total about $3.1 billion, or roughly $281 million per year, across seven partners (Disney/ESPN-ABC, NBCUniversal/NBC-Peacock, Amazon Prime Video, Paramount/CBS, Scripps/ION, USA Sports/USA Network and NBA TV). The foundational July 2024 deal with Disney, NBCU and Amazon was ~$2.2 billion over 11 years (~$200 million/yr); the CBS and ION renewals plus the new USA Sports deal lift the total to the $281 million AAV figure. No per-partner values for the renewals/additions have been disclosed, and I found no evidence of any Netflix deal.

### Cited Findings
**Foundational deal (Disney, NBCU, Amazon; signed July 2024; starts 2026 season)**
- The WNBA's 11-year deal with Disney, Amazon and Comcast-owned NBCUniversal is "valued at about $200 million per year," as CNBC previously reported — [CNBC via NBC 7 San Diego, June 13, 2025](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/wnba-renews-media-rights-deal-with-scripps/3847464/)
- Front Office Sports describes the prior deal as "a $2.2 billion, 11-year deal signed in July 2024 with Disney, NBCUniversal, and Amazon" — [Front Office Sports newsletter, May 11, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/wnba-media-deals-top-3b/)

### Inferences
- Best-sourced total AAV for 2026: ~$281 million/yr (FOS, single sourced; $3.1B / 11 years = $281.8M, my arithmetic), versus the ~$200M/yr for the three-partner foundational deal. The ~$81M/yr difference covers CBS, ION, USA Sports and NBA TV combined, but no outlet has published how it splits. If ION's renewal is in the neighborhood of its prior $13M/yr, CBS plus USA Sports plus NBA TV would account for the balance (~$68M/yr) — this is my inference, not reported.
- The widely circulated "could reach $260M/yr with added partners" projection from 2024 has effectively been superseded by the May 2026 FOS $281M AAV figure.
- The re-set provision after 2028 means the AAV is not fixed for the full 11 years.

### Gaps
- No per-partner dollar values for Disney, NBCU, Amazon, Paramount/CBS, Scripps/ION, USA Sports or NBA TV have been reported; the $3.1B/$281M figure is from a single FOS source and is not confirmed by the league.
- No reliable source found for any Netflix–WNBA deal; the only Netflix references in results were unrelated.
- ION renewal length is undisclosed; whether its annual value changed from ~$13M is unknown.
- Could not fetch the Scripps 10-Q filings to check for disclosed WNBA rights-fee commitments.

## WNBA Key Question 3: Salary cap structure (2025 and 2026) and CBA status

### Takeaway
2025 (final year under the 2020 CBA): hard cap of $1,507,100, minimum team salary ~$1,261,400 (83.7% of cap), supermax $249,244, minimums $66,079 (0–2 yrs) / $78,831 (3+ yrs). The WNBPA opted out in October 2024; the old CBA expired after the 2025 season (extended to January 9, 2026); a tentative 7-year agreement (2026–2032, opt-out after 2031) was announced March 20, 2026 and ratified by players (unanimously) and the Board of Governors in the week of March 23–24, 2026. The new CBA sets a revenue-based cap of $7.0M for 2026 (projected >$11M by 2032), a $1.4M maximum/supermax (20% of cap), tiered minimums of $270,000–$300,000, a reported 85%-of-cap floor ($5.95M in 2026), and a formula of roughly 30.25–30.5% of league revenue plus 12–13% of team revenue (for salary cap plus benefits) from 2027, with annual movement limited to ±13% (2027) and ±10% thereafter.

### Cited Findings
**2025 structure (2020 CBA)**
- 2025: minimum salary $66,079, supermax $249,244, salary cap $1,507,100, with the cap increasing at a fixed 3% per year under the 2020 agreement (snippet, ESPN-sourced) — [TSN/ESPN report](https://www.tsn.ca/wnba/article/report-wnbas-proposal-ups-max-salary-base-to-1m-n1-47169591/)
- Veteran minimum $78,831 alongside the $249,244 supermax (snippet) — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/wnba/why-the-wnba-s-proposal-to-triple-salaries-still-isn-t-enough)
- Spotrac: 2025 cap $1,507,100 and a minimum team-spending floor of $1,261,400 (snippet; page returned 402 on fetch) — [Spotrac news](https://spotrac.com/news/_/id/2492/MIN)
- Her Hoop Stats (2020 CBA explainer): the cap is "a hard cap with very little wiggle room"; the team minimum is 83.7% of the cap; teams may be under the minimum during the season but must meet it by season's end or pay the shortfall to rostered players; the only in-season exception is the hardship/emergency hardship exception, and contracts signed under it count against the cap but the team may exceed the cap; 2020 league guarantee was $1,209,000 per team / $14,508,000 league-wide — [Her Hoop Stats, July 23, 2020](https://herhoopstats.substack.com/p/wnba-cba-and-salary-cap-explained-a92)
  - 83.7% × $1,507,100 = $1,261,443 (my arithmetic), consistent with Spotrac's $1,261,400 and a secondary table's $1,261,440 — [SportsDunia (secondary)](https://www.sportsdunia.com/wnba-records/wnba-salary-cap-breakdown)
- 2025 comparison figures per CBS Sports: cap $1.5M; minimum $66,079; average salary $120,000; supermax $249,244; champion bonus $22,908; MVP $15,450 — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/wnba/news/wnba-cba-news-deal-details-salaries-explainer/)
- Supermax eligibility under the old deal required 8+ years of service plus an MVP, All-WNBA First/Second Team or DPOY honor (snippet) — [Front Office Sports newsletter](https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/pm-2-25/)

**New CBA economics (official key-elements document)**
- "Creates a revenue-based salary cap directly tied to league and team revenue growth. The team salary cap will be set at $7.0 million in 2026 and is projected to be over $11 million by 2032"; Maximum $1.4M in 2026, projected $2.4M by 2032; Average $583,000 in 2026, projected over $1M by 2032; Minimum $270,000–$300,000 in 2026 (by years of service), $340,000–$380,000 by 2032; all 38 existing rookie deals adjusted upward; teams must carry 12 players; two developmental roster spots per team that do not count against the cap; new season-ending injury exception; pregnancy and childbirth salary cap exception; starting 2027 players with 7+ years of service cannot be designated Core Players; 2026 champion bonus $60,000 per player (up from $22,908); MVP $60,000 (up from $15,450); bonuses grow with cap from 2027; league-provided housing for all players 2026–2028 and for players earning ≤$500,000 in 2029–2030; charter travel projected >$300M over the agreement — [WNBA/NBA "Key Elements of the Tentative CBA" PDF, March 2026](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/46/2026/03/KEY-ELEMENTS-OF-THE-TENTATIVE-COLLECTIVE-BARGAINING-AGREEMENT-Final-.pdf)

**Minimum salary tiers, max/supermax, exceptions (reported)**
- 2026 minimums: rookie $270,000; 1–3 YOS $277,500; 4–6 YOS $285,000; 7–9 YOS $292,500; 10+ YOS $300,000; about 20% of the $7M cap ($1.4M) designated for supermax; cap expected to exceed $10M by 2031; revenue share "roughly 20% on average over the deal's life"; minimum-salary cap exception (veteran signed at the fourth-tier minimum or higher counts only $277,500 against the cap); "Exceptional Performance on Initial Contract" rule lets All-WNBA 1st/2nd team players renegotiate year four of rookie deals to the standard max, or to the supermax if MVP — [Front Office Sports, March 20, 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/wnba-wnbpa-sign-term-sheet-for-7-year-cba/)
- Teams may have up to seven protected contracts (up from six); previously teams could carry 11 players for cap reasons, now must carry 12 — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/wnba/news/wnba-cba-news-deal-details-salaries-explainer/)
- Cap annual change limited to 10% in either direction, except 13% after the first season; players wanted gross revenue, league pushed for net — [AP via NY1, March 19, 2026](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/03/20/wnba-players-briefed-on-a-transformational-7-year-cba-with-a-7m-2026-salary-cap)
- A'ja Wilson signed a three-year, $5 million supermax with the Aces under the new CBA — [RTG Pod explainer](https://rtgpod.com/wnba-cba-2026-explained/)

**Cap formula and floor (long-form CBA, reported)**
- ESPN's reading of the long-form CBA (snippet; page could not be fetched): cap equals a designated 20% of "Shared Basketball Revenue" (league plus team revenue from the prior year); 2026 cap fixed at $7M; for 2027 the formula uses 30.25% of league revenue plus 12% of team revenue, rising to 30.5% of league revenue plus 13% of team revenue for 2031 and 2032; cap cannot move more than 10% year over year (13% for 2027); cap is calculated after subtracting benefits and adjusted for prior-year reconciliation; excess revenue is paid to players proportionally as determined by the union — [ESPN, "Long-form CBA outlines revenue share, later free agency period"](https://www.espn.com/wnba/story/_/id/48898370/wnba-new-cba-shows-salary-cap-set-20-player-revenue-share)
- The IX Basketball (June 4, 2026): salary cap plus benefits = 30.25%–30.5% of league revenue plus 12%–13% of team revenue; cap may move up to 13% from 2026 to 2027 and up to 10% per season thereafter, with revenue beyond the limit not carrying over; **minimum team salary is 85% of the cap, i.e., $5,950,000 for 2026**; player minimums fixed through 2035 rising 4% annually (2029 10+ YOS minimum $337,500); non-gate team revenue defined up front and grown 12.5%/yr on average; gate revenue = ticket revenue minus taxes and fees; a 25% one-year rise in non-gate revenue triggers good-faith talks; prior 2020 CBA gave players 50% of revenue above a threshold minus 30% for expenses — [The IX Basketball, June 4, 2026](https://www.theixsports.com/features/unpacking-the-new-wnba-collective-bargaining-agreement/)

**Hard vs. soft cap**
- The 2020 CBA cap was a hard cap (Her Hoop Stats, above). For the 2026 CBA, none of the official documents or fetched articles use the terms "hard" or "soft"; DIRECTV/Her Hoop Stats describe it as a "hard cap of approximately $7 million" while simultaneously listing the Liberty at $7,348,510, above the cap — [DIRECTV Insider, May 15, 2026](https://www.directv.com/insider/wnba-payrolls/)

**Expansion**
- 2026 season: 330 games, 44 per team, across the newly expanded 15-team league (Toronto Tempo and Portland Fire debut) — [Sports Media Watch, January 2026](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2026/01/wnba-announces-schedule-cba-negotiations-new-media-rights-deal/); double expansion draft for Portland and Toronto — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/wnba/news/wnba-cba-news-deal-details-salaries-explainer/)
- WNBA.com navigation lists a Cleveland Sirens team slated for 2028 — [WNBA.com](https://www.wnba.com/news/wnba-wnbpa-tentative-cba-deal-2026)

### Inferences
- Structure: the 2026 cap is best described as a revenue-indexed cap with a fixed 2026 figure, a hard-ish ceiling in practice but with enumerated exceptions (season-ending injury, pregnancy/childbirth, minimum-salary cap charge of $277,500, two off-cap developmental spots) that allow payroll to exceed $7.0M — which is why 2026 payrolls above $7M appear in the Her Hoop Stats data. No luxury tax has been reported.
- Floor: $5.95M (85% of cap) per The IX versus 83.7% under the old CBA; total 2026 floor across 15 teams = $89.25M (my arithmetic).
- Max salary: $1.4M is simultaneously described as the "maximum" (official release) and as the "supermax" (FOS/CBS). A separate "standard max" is referenced by FOS but its dollar value was not reported; sources in effect treat $1.4M (20% of cap) as the top salary.
- Expansion to 18 teams by 2030: per the task brief (Cleveland 2028, Detroit 2029, Philadelphia 2030) — only the Cleveland 2028 reference was confirmed in fetched sources; treat the 18-by-2030 figure as unverified here.

### Gaps
- The long-form CBA text itself was not accessible; the 30.25%/12% formula, 85% floor and "Shared Basketball Revenue" definition rest on ESPN (snippet) and The IX reporting. The ESPN snippet's "20% of Shared Basketball Revenue" wording and The IX's "30.25–30.5% league + 12–13% team" framing are different descriptions; The IX explains the final deal split the base rather than using a flat 20%.
- No explicit official statement that the new cap is "hard" or "soft."
- The dollar value of the non-super "standard maximum" under the new CBA was not found. The 2025 standard (non-super) maximum (~$214,466 under the 2020 CBA schedule) was not confirmed by a fetched source and is therefore omitted.
- Exact ratification dates: sources give "Monday" (players) and "Tuesday" (BOG) in the week after March 20, 2026; one secondary source says BOG March 24, 2026; one says March 22. Not reconciled against an official release.

## NWSL Key Question 1: Current national media rights deals and combined AAV

### Takeaway
The NWSL's domestic rights package (2024–2027 seasons) with CBS Sports/Paramount+, ESPN/Disney, Amazon Prime Video and Scripps/ION is reported at about $240 million over four years (~$60 million/yr; Fortune citing a person familiar; SBJ sources put it at ~$60M/yr including rights fees plus production and marketing commitments). Per-partner shares have never been disclosed. On September 18, 2025, the league announced a mid-cycle expansion for 2026–2027 (ESPN from 20 to 36 games; CBS to 33–38 games; new fifth partner Victory+ with 57 games) with no financial terms disclosed, so the best-supported league-wide AAV remains ~$60M/yr plus an undisclosed increment.

## NWSL Key Question 3: Salary cap structure and CBA status (2024 CBA through 2030)

### Takeaway
The NWSL–NWSLPA CBA runs July 30, 2024 – December 31, 2030. It sets a Team Salary Cap floor schedule of $3.3M (2025), $3.5M (2026), $4.4M (2027), $4.7M (2028), $4.9M (2029), $5.1M (2030), each increased by a Team Revenue Share equal to 10% of (net national media revenue + sponsorship revenue less 25%) divided by the number of teams; actual caps were $3.5M in 2025 and $3.7M in 2026. Minimum salaries: $48,500 (2025), $50,500 (2026), $65,000 (2027), $67,600 (2028), $70,500 (2029), $82,500 (2030). There is no maximum salary, allocation money was phased out in 2024, and the cap is effectively hard except for enumerated exemptions (up to four minimum-salary cap-exempt contracts, roster-relief contracts) and the league-created High Impact Player rule (up to $1M over the cap), which the NWSLPA challenged via a grievance filed January 14, 2026 (outcome not found).

### Cited Findings
**CBA term and cap schedule (official text)**
- Term: July 30, 2024 – December 31, 2030, continuing year to year unless either party gives 60 days' notice — [NWSL CBA 2024–2030 (NWSLPA PDF)](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §8.16 Team Salary Cap: 2024 not less than $2,750,000; thereafter not less than 2025 $3,300,000; 2026 $3,500,000; 2027 $4,400,000; 2028 $4,700,000; 2029 $4,900,000; 2030 $5,100,000; "The Team Salary Cap shall be increased by the Team Revenue Share from the prior League Season"; NWSL may in its discretion, after consultation with the NWSLPA, increase the cap in any year and may reduce or eliminate the cap charge "for certain roster classifications" — [NWSL CBA, §8.16](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §8.14 Team Revenue Share: 10% of the sum of NWSL Media Revenue (net national media revenue = gross less promotional value and production expenses) and Sponsorship Revenue (gross national sponsorship less an assumed 25% cost, excluding value-in-kind), divided by the number of teams; added to both the Team Minimum Spend and the Team Salary Cap — [NWSL CBA, §8.14](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §8.1 Minimum Salary (base, excl. bonuses): 2024 $37,856; 2025 $48,500; 2026 $50,500; 2027 $65,000 (auto stipend eliminated, locality COLA and supplemental stipend implemented); 2028 $67,600; 2029 $70,500; 2030 $82,500 — [NWSL CBA, §8.1](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §8.13 Team Minimum Spend: none in 2024; from 2025, minimum salary × cap-counting players (22–26 in 2025–26; 23–26 from 2027) plus Team Revenue Share — [NWSL CBA, §8.13](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §8.17: cap counts base salary and bonuses (prior season's performance bonuses used) — [NWSL CBA, §8.17](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- §7A.1: Roster Relief Contracts (for 45-day/season-ending injury, goalkeeper injury, pregnancy/parental leave, mental-health leave, national-team call-up) and up to four Salary Cap Exempt Contracts per team at minimum salary without bonuses that do not count against the cap — [NWSL CBA, §7A.1](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)
- NWSLPA summary: cap tied to revenue sharing for the first time, on top of a guaranteed minimum base; prior-year shareable media and sponsorship revenues "add at least $200,000 to the base cap each year"; NWSLPA audit rights — [NWSLPA CBA page](https://www.nwslplayers.com/cba)

**Actual cap figures by season**
- 2025: base $3.3M; total maximum roster spend $3.5M with revenue sharing; minimum salary $48,500; roster 22–26; exceptions for injuries, parental leave and national-team call-ups — [Just Women's Sports, Feb. 19, 2025](https://justwomenssports.com/reads/nwsl-raises-player-salary-cap-pay-ahead-of-2025-season-kick-off/)
- 2026: $3.7M team salary cap on a $3.5M base after revenue-sharing adjustments (announced March 11, 2026); teams can exceed the cap by up to $1M for High Impact Players (rule announced in 2025) — [The Equalizer, March 11, 2026](https://equalizersoccer.com/quick-update/nwsl-announces-3-7m-team-salary-cap-3-5m-base/)
- Historical cap and allocation money table: allocation money $300K (2020), $400K (2021), $500K (2022), $600K (2023), "phased out" in 2024; caps 2024 $2.75M through 2030 $5.1M "+ revenue sharing" — [Soccer America, Aug. 22, 2024](https://www.socceramerica.com/by-the-numbers-nwsl-salary-cap/)
- "The league has no maximum salary" (snippet) — [Just Women's Sports, Feb. 19, 2025](https://justwomenssports.com/reads/nwsl-raises-player-salary-cap-pay-ahead-of-2025-season-kick-off/)
- Allocation money: teams must spend or withdraw existing allocation money by December 31, 2026 (snippet, secondary explainer) — [SportsOrca](https://sportsorca.com/soccer/nwsl/nwsl-salary-cap-rules/)

**High Impact Player (HIP / "Rodman rule") and grievance**
- The HIP rule allows each team to spend an additional $1 million on player wages above the salary cap, "which will clock in at $3.7 million for the 2026 season"; eligibility tied to USWNT playing-time thresholds, year-end award lists and a media-generated top-players list (snippet) — [Front Office Sports, "NWSL union files grievance over league's Rodman rule"](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nwsl-union-files-grievance-over-leagues-rodman-rule-for-star-players/)
- NWSLPA filed a grievance on January 14, 2026 calling for immediate rescission of the HIP rule, arguing it violates the CBA and federal labor law; it was the union's second grievance in under two months (the first concerned the league's rejection of Trinity Rodman's agreed contract with Washington) — [The Equalizer, Jan. 14, 2026](https://equalizersoccer.com/2026/01/14/nwslpa-files-grievance-over-new-high-impact-player-rule-calls-for-immediate-rescission/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nwsl-union-files-grievance-over-leagues-rodman-rule-for-star-players/)
- League position: the CBA allows it discretion to establish the High Impact Player roster classification; Commissioner Jessica Berman defended the rule citing a "responsibility" to support the "commercial business" — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/soccer/news/nwsl-jessica-berman-high-impact-player-rule-nwslpa-grievance/)
- Secondary explainer: HIP effective July 1, 2026; the cap charge for such contracts must be at least 12% of the base cap ($420,000 in 2026); NWSLPA grievance pending (snippet) — [SportsOrca](https://sportsorca.com/soccer/nwsl/nwsl-salary-cap-rules/)
- Rodman re-signed with the Spirit, with club officials noting the HIP rule helped enable her return; she is described as the highest-paid women's soccer player in the world (snippet) — [Front Office Sports, "Trinity Rodman NWSL Spirit"](https://frontofficesports.com/article/trinity-rodman-nwsl-spirit/)

### Inferences
- Hard vs. soft: the CBA never uses "hard cap," but the cap is a binding ceiling on cap-counted compensation with no luxury tax; flexibility comes only from (a) cap-exempt minimum contracts, (b) roster-relief contracts, (c) the league's §8.16 discretion to reduce cap charges for "certain roster classifications," which is the hook the league used for the HIP rule. With HIP, the effective 2026 ceiling for a team is up to $4.7M.
- The revenue-share add-on was ~$200,000 per team in both 2025 ($3.3M→$3.5M) and 2026 ($3.5M→$3.7M), implying shareable media + sponsorship revenue of roughly $28M (2024, /14 teams) and ~$32M (2025, /16 teams) under the 10% formula — my back-calculation, not reported.
- Scheduled 2027 cap: $4.4M base plus revenue share (likely ≥$4.6M), the largest single-year step in the schedule.

### Gaps
- Outcome of the NWSLPA's HIP grievance (arbitration ruling or settlement) as of October 2026 was not found; search-result dates were unreliable.
- The exact 2025 and 2026 Team Revenue Share dollar amounts were not published by the league in fetched sources; the ~$200,000 figure is implied by the announced totals.
- The SportsOrca details (HIP effective date, 12% minimum cap charge, Dec. 31, 2026 allocation-money deadline) come from a secondary explainer and were not confirmed against a league release.
