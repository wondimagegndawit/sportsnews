# NFL and NBA: media rights AAV, total player payroll, and salary cap structure (as of October 21, 2026)

Scope note: United States men's leagues only. Every figure is labelled with its season/year and whether it is an official/league figure, a figure reported by a named outlet (usually from anonymous sources, since neither league discloses rights fees), or an estimate/aggregator sum. ## NFL Key Question 1: Current national media rights deals and combined league-wide AAV

### Takeaway
The NFL's current national package (2023 through the 2033 season for the five core partners, with a league opt-out after the 2029 season, 2030 for ESPN) is almost universally reported at ~$110B/11 years, i.e. ~$10B per year across CBS, Fox, NBC, ESPN/ABC and Amazon; adding YouTube's Sunday Ticket (~$2B/yr reported) and Netflix (~$150M/yr reported for the original 2-game deal; fee for the May 2026 five-game extension undisclosed) brings best-sourced combined national media AAV to roughly $12.0-12.3B per year. None of these fees is officially disclosed; all are press-reported estimates (Bloomberg, CNBC, WSJ, SBJ). As of October 2026 the NFL has NOT exercised its opt-out and no partner has publicly agreed to an early fee increase: Fox explicitly refused in August 2026, CBS/Paramount talks (NFL reportedly seeking ~$1B/yr more) were reported as ongoing/paused, and Goodell said in September 2026 the league is "evaluating" repackaging.

**Cross-check against league national revenue**

### Inferences
- Summing the most commonly cited per-partner figures: ESPN $2.7B + Fox $2.2-2.25B + CBS $2.1B + NBC $2.0B + Amazon $1.0B = ~$10.0-10.05B/yr, consistent with $110B / 11 = $10.0B/yr. Adding YouTube (~$2.0B) gives ~$12.0B/yr; adding Netflix at the original ~$150M/yr gives ~$12.2B/yr (the 2026 five-game extension is presumably higher but undisclosed). So the best-sourced combined league-wide national media AAV is approximately $10B (core five) to ~$12B+ (all national deals). This summation is my calculation from press-reported figures.
- The $105B/$110B/$113B spread reflects different 2021 outlets' estimates (Bloomberg/CNBC/NPR); $110B has become the consensus number used by SportsPro, S&P Kagan and most 2026 coverage.
- No renegotiated or early-opt-out fee is in effect for the 2026 season; the current reported fees remain the operative figures.

### Gaps
- No official fee disclosure exists for any partner; all per-partner AAVs are press estimates. Exact escalators (fees typically step up over the term) are not public, so "AAV" is an average over the term, not the 2026 payment.
- The fee for Netflix's May 2026 four-year extension was not disclosed in any source found.
- Could not retrieve the Sportico Fox article (paywall/redirect) or the FOS opt-out newsletter text directly; relied on SMW, CNBC and SportsPro.

## NFL Key Question 2: Total player salary / payroll, most recent season

### Takeaway
For the 2025 season, Spotrac's team cash tracker totals $9,652,450,676 in cash across all 32 teams (1,686 contracts; ~$7.31B active, ~$623M dead money), i.e. ~$302M per club against a $279.2M cap; a cap-based ceiling is 32 x $279.2M = $8.93B. The league's official figure for 2026 player costs is $378.8M per club (cap $301.2M + $77.6M benefits) = ~$12.1B league-wide including benefits.

### Cited Findings
- Spotrac 2025 NFL Team Salary Cash Tracker totals row: $9,652,450,676 total cash, 1,686 players; active cash ~$7.31B; dead money ~$623M; top teams Jacksonville $351,286,256, Minnesota ~$345.8M, Buffalo ~$342.5M — [Spotrac 2025 cash tracker](https://www.spotrac.com/nfl/cash/_/year/2025/sort/cash_total). NOTE: figures taken from the search-engine snapshot of the page; the page itself returned HTTP 402/403 to my fetches, so the total could not be re-verified directly and may have moved slightly as contracts are updated.
- Over The Cap cash spending tables (year selector 2026-2030; 2025 no longer selectable). For the table shown first (appears to be the in-progress 2026 season), the 32 team "Total Cash Spending" values sum to $10,584,348,651 (my sum; average $330.8M per club), led by Cowboys $391,383,971, Packers $383,760,985, Seahawks $373,840,520, with Dolphins $246,372,940 lowest — [Over The Cap cash spending](https://overthecap.com/cash-spending). OTC shows no league total row.
- 2025 salary cap $279.2M per club; 2026 cap $301.2M per club plus $77.6M benefits = $378.8M total player costs per club (announced Feb 27, 2026, per NFL spokesman Brian McCarthy) — [NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-2026-salary-cap-set-at-301-2-million-per-team); [Raiders.com summary](https://www.raiders.com/news/2026-nfl-salary-cap-set-at-301-2-million-free-agency-transactions-draft).
- Packers FY2026: player costs rose $131.7M year-over-year, driving operating income from +$83.7M to -$1.1M (shows cash payroll can swing far above the cap in a given year due to restructures) — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/packers-earnings-nfl-revenue-media/).
- Minimum team cash spending: 90% of the salary caps over the 2024-2026 three-league-year period and over 2027-2030 (CBA Art. 12 Sec. 9) — [Over The Cap CBA text, Art. 12 Sec. 9](https://overthecap.com/collective-bargaining-agreement/article/12/section/9).

### Inferences
- Methodology comparison for 2025: (a) Spotrac cash sum ~$9.65B (cash actually paid in the 2025 league year incl. signing bonuses and dead money); (b) cap x 32 = $8.93B (cap accounting, not cash); (c) league "player costs" incl. benefits would be higher (for 2026 the league itself puts it at ~$12.1B = 32 x $378.8M; a 2025 equivalent was not found). Cash exceeding cap x 32 is expected because the cap counts prorated bonuses while cash counts them when paid.
- OTC's 2026 running total ($10.58B) is for the current, incomplete 2026 season and will change; it is not a final figure.

### Gaps
- No official NFL/NFLPA publication of total league-wide player compensation for 2025 was found (the league does not publish audited player-cost totals; the Packers' statement is the only public team financial).
- Could not independently verify the Spotrac 2025 total due to access restrictions (HTTP 402/403); OTC's 2025 cash table is no longer offered in its year selector.

## NFL Key Question 3: Salary cap structure, revenue share, CBA expiry

### Takeaway
The NFL operates a hard cap: $301.2M per club for 2026 (first time above $300M; $279.2M in 2025; $255.4M in 2024), with a minimum cash spending floor of 90% of the caps over multi-year periods; players receive a base 48% of All Revenue from 2021 with a "media kicker" up to 48.8%; the CBA runs through the 2030 season/league year ending March 2031.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 cap: $301.2M per club (low end of the January projection of $301.2M-$305.7M), +$22M over 2025's $279.2M; 2024 was $255.4M; 2022 $208.2M; benefits $77.6M for total player costs $378.8M per club — [NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-2026-salary-cap-set-at-301-2-million-per-team); projection range per [Tom Pelissero on X](https://x.com/TomPelissero/status/2017325334840930672) and [theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nfl/news/3467984).
- Hard cap / minimum cash spending mechanism: CBA Article 12 Section 9 sets guaranteed Minimum Team Cash Spending of 89% of the caps for 2017-2020 and 90% for the 2021-2023, 2024-2026 and 2027-2030 periods — [Over The Cap CBA text](https://overthecap.com/collective-bargaining-agreement/article/12/section/9/(b)/). (Teams failing the floor must pay the shortfall to players.)
- Revenue share: players received 47% of All Revenue in 2020; from 2021-2030 the base share is 48% whether 16 or 17 games are played; the "media kicker" raises the share to 48.5% if TV revenue rises 60% and to 48.8% if it rises 120% or more — [NFL.com CBA guide](https://www.nfl.com/news/guide-to-understanding-proposed-cba-ahead-of-voting-deadline-0ap3000001105649); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/28901832).
- CBA term: the 2020 CBA "ensures labor peace until March of 2031," covering the 2020 season plus 10 new years (through the 2030 season) — [NFL.com CBA guide](https://www.nfl.com/news/guide-to-understanding-proposed-cba-ahead-of-voting-deadline-0ap3000001105649); [SI](https://www.si.com/nfl/browns/news/collective-bargaining-agreement-passes-ensures-labor-peace-until-2030).
- Funding rule: teams must escrow guaranteed money minus a deductible that rose to $15M and then $17M for 2029-30 — [NFL.com CBA guide](https://www.nfl.com/news/guide-to-understanding-proposed-cba-ahead-of-voting-deadline-0ap3000001105649).

### Inferences
- 32 x $301.2M = $9.64B is the 2026 aggregate cap; 32 x $378.8M = $12.12B is the league's own 2026 aggregate "player costs" (cap + benefits).
- The large 2025-to-2026 cap jump ($22M) is consistent with the media-kicker structure and the Packers' 4.8% national-revenue growth plus cap-smoothing carryovers.

### Gaps
- The exact media-kicker percentage currently in effect (48%, 48.5% or 48.8%) is not published by the league; no 2026 source stated which tier applies.
- The CBA's formal expiry is best described as "after the 2030 season / March 2031 league year"; I did not retrieve the CBA's own termination article text.

## NBA Key Question 1: Current national media rights deals and combined AAV

### Takeaway
The NBA's 11-year deals (2025-26 through 2035-36) with Disney/ESPN-ABC, NBCUniversal/Peacock and Amazon Prime Video are reported at ~$76-77B in total, roughly $6.9-7.0B per year; the WSJ-reported split is Disney ~$2.6B, NBC ~$2.5B, Amazon ~$1.8-1.9B per year (the ~$77B figure includes ~$2.2B over the term for the WNBA). No official financial terms were released, and the deals have no NBA opt-out.

### Cited Findings
- July 2024: NBA agreed to 11-year deals worth a reported ~$76B with Disney, NBC and Amazon; Disney ~$2.6B/yr (up from $1.4B), NBC ~$2.5B/yr, Amazon ~$1.8B/yr; official terms not released — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/40535771/reports-nba-agrees-terms-76-billion-media-rights-deal); [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-signs-new-tv-deal-details-on-11-year-76-billion-deal-with-espn-nbc-amazon-as-tnt-gets-left-out/amp); [AFP via Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/nba-signs-11-year-media-deals-reportedly-worth-76-billion-1.1721885748069).
- Sports Media Watch, citing the Wall Street Journal: Disney $2.6B/yr, Comcast/NBC $2.5B/yr, Amazon $1.9B/yr (Puck's John Ourand reported Amazon at $1.8B); total $77B across three 11-year deals (2025-2036) = ~$75B NBA + ~$2.2B WNBA; the NBA deals have no opt-out, while the WNBA can seek a rights-fee revaluation after three years — [Sports Media Watch](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2024/07/nba-media-rights-breakdown-who-gets-what-espn-nbc-amazon/).
- Sportcal analysis: $76.0B over 11 years = ~$6.9B per year, a ~165% increase on the prior cycle — [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/analyst-comment/nba-nets-more-media-value-with-new-domestic-rights/).
- Package contents (per SMW): Disney/ESPN-ABC gets the Finals every year, a conference final most years, early-round playoffs, up to ~112 games/season, Draft/Lottery; NBC/Peacock ~128 games incl. 100 regular season + 28 playoff, MLK Day, All-Star, Sunday nights post-NFL, ~50 on Peacock; Amazon ~92 games incl. NBA Cup knockout rounds, the entire Play-In, Thursday (post-NFL) and Friday games, plus League Pass partnership — [Sports Media Watch](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2024/07/nba-media-rights-breakdown-who-gets-what-espn-nbc-amazon/).
- SportsPro (Mar 2026) uses "US$76 billion" as the comparator for the NBA's rights — [SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/broadcast-ott/nfl-cbs-paramount-media-rights-price-march-2026/).

### Inferences
- WSJ per-partner figures sum to $7.0B/yr ($77B/11); ESPN's figures sum to $6.9B/yr ($76B/11). The difference is almost entirely the Amazon figure ($1.8B vs $1.9B) and whether WNBA value is included. Best statement: combined national AAV ~$6.9-7.0B/yr for 2025-26 through 2035-36 (reported, WSJ/ESPN; not official), of which roughly $6.7-6.8B/yr is attributable to the NBA proper if the ~$2.2B WNBA allocation is stripped out.
- Deals are front-loaded lower and escalate over the term (typical structure), so the 2025-26 payment is likely below the $6.9B average; no source gave year-one amounts.

### Gaps
- No official league or partner disclosure of fees; no public year-by-year escalator schedule.
- Local/regional rights are out of scope and not included; international rights are also excluded.

## NBA Key Question 2: Total league-wide player salary, most recent season

### Takeaway
The best "published" figures come from the league's audited BRI accounting as reported by Sportico: for 2025-26, BRI was $11.68B and total player salaries and benefits were $6.27B (above the players' 51% designated share, triggering a 5.5% escrow haircut); for 2024-25, BRI was $10.25B (implying a ~$5.2B player share at 51%, with players forfeiting >$480M of escrow). Sum-of-payroll aggregators (Spotrac/HoopsHype) were inaccessible to me (402/403), so I could not confirm a roster-sum figure; the structural bounds for 2025-26 are 30 x $139.182M floor = $4.18B and 30 x $154.647M cap = $4.64B, with actual payrolls above the cap for most teams.

### Cited Findings
- 2025-26 final accounting (reported by Sportico, 2026): BRI $11.68B, up from $10.25B in 2024-25; player salaries and benefits totaled $6.27B, exceeding the 51% threshold; the ~$580M escrow pool split 54.8% to owners / 45.2% to players, a 5.5% haircut on negotiated salaries (e.g., Stephen Curry, top earner at $59.8M, forgoes $3.3M) — [Sportico, "NBA Players, Owners to Split $580M in Escrow"](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2026/nba-escrow-players-salaries-owners-million-1234937798/) (figures taken from search snapshot; article is paywalled/redirected).
- 2024-25: BRI was $10.25B according to a source familiar with the league's accounting; players forfeited more than $480M of escrow, retaining 90.9% of salaries (10% escrow split ~91% to teams, 9% to players) — [Sportico, 2025, "NBA player salaries cut, escrow system"](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-player-salaries-cut-escrow-system-1234858905/) (snippet; paywalled).
- Players' share: "NBA players are slated to receive about 51% of league revenues" under the 2023 CBA; ~$160M/yr of licensing revenue added to BRI, split 50-50 — [AP via Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2023/04/27/sports/nba-players-finalize-new-collective-bargaining-agreement/amp/); [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/37929751/nba-players-officially-sign-new-cba-which-starts-july-1).

## NBA Key Question 3: Salary cap structure (2025-26), tax, aprons, revenue share, CBA expiry

### Takeaway
The NBA uses a soft cap with exceptions: for 2025-26 the cap is $154.647M, tax level $187.895M, first apron $195.945M, second apron $207.824M, minimum team salary $139.182M (90% of cap), with tax rates of $1.00/$1.25/$3.50/$4.75 per dollar in successive ~$5.685M brackets (repeater: $3.00/$3.25/$5.50/$6.75), plus $0.50 per bracket beyond; players receive ~51% of BRI (within the 49-51% band) and the 2023 CBA runs through June 30, 2030 with a mutual opt-out after 2028-29 (notice by Oct 15, 2028).

### Cited Findings
- Official 2025-26 figures (NBA announcement June 30, 2025, effective 12:01 a.m. ET July 1): Salary Cap $154.647M; Tax Level $187.895M; First Apron $195.945M; Second Apron $207.824M; Minimum Team Salary $139.182M; Non-Taxpayer Mid-Level $14.104M; Taxpayer Mid-Level $5.685M; Room Mid-Level $8.781M — [NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-salary-cap-set-2025-26-season). Also [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-salary-cap-2025-26-season-million-1234858605/) and [Hoops Rumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/06/salary-cap-tax-line-set-for-2025-26-nba-season.html).
- The 10% increase was the maximum permitted under the CBA; 2024-25 cap was $140.588M — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/44404893/sources-nba-salary-cap-increase-10-2025-26); [NBA PR 2024-25](https://pr.nba.com/2024-25-nba-season-salary-cap). 2024-25 tax line $170.814M, first apron $178.132M, second apron $188.931M — [Hoops Rumors](https://hoopsrumors.com/2024/06/salary-cap-tax-line-set-for-2024-25-nba-season.html).
- Tax line = 121.5% of cap; minimum team salary = 90% of cap, and a team below the floor pays the shortfall to the league — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-salary-cap-to-rise-by-10-for-2025-26-season-what-reported-increase-means-for-aprons-luxury-tax-more/).
- Luxury tax rates from 2025-26: Bracket 1 $1.00 (repeater $3.00); Bracket 2 $1.25 ($3.25); Bracket 3 $3.50 ($5.50); Bracket 4 $4.75 ($6.75); +$0.50 per bracket thereafter; brackets scale with the cap (2024-25 width $5.168M); repeater = taxpayer in at least three of the previous four seasons; proceeds split 50% to league purposes and 50% equally to non-taxpaying teams — [Hoops Rumors glossary](https://hoopsrumors.com/2024/11/hoops-rumors-glossary-luxury-tax-penalties-4.html). 2025-26 bracket width $5.685M and full rate ladder (to 7.75x / 9.75x in bracket 10) — [SalarySwish](https://www.salaryswish.com/luxury-tax/2026).
- Apron mechanics: teams above the second apron cannot aggregate salaries in trades or use the taxpayer mid-level, and using certain exceptions hard-caps a team at the first or second apron — [NBC Sports Boston explainer](https://www.nbcsportsboston.com/nba/second-apron-nba-cba-explained/716143/); [CBS Sports deadline explainer](https://picks-s1.cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-trade-deadline-luxury-tax-apron-explainer-2026/); [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25223729-nba-salary-cap-2025-26-and-celtics-cavaliers-among-teams-facing-hard-cap).
- Revenue share: players receive ~51% of BRI under the 2023 CBA; the 2025-26 escrow result (salaries+benefits $6.27B vs. 51% of $11.68B BRI) shows the mechanism in practice — [AP via Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2023/04/27/sports/nba-players-finalize-new-collective-bargaining-agreement/amp/); [Sportico 2026](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2026/nba-escrow-players-salaries-owners-million-1234937798/).
- CBA term: effective July 1, 2023, runs through the 2029-30 season (June 30, 2030); either side may opt out after 2028-29 by written notice on or before October 15, 2028, in which case it terminates June 30, 2029 — [NBA.com tentative deal release](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-and-nbpa-reach-tentative-deal-on-new-collective-bargaining-agreement); [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/37929751/nba-players-officially-sign-new-cba-which-starts-july-1); [Wikipedia, NBA CBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_collective_bargaining_agreement).
- NBPA remains opposed to the second apron and the next negotiation is expected to involve NBA Europe revenue and BRI definitions — [Front Office Sports, Oct 2026](https://frontofficesports.com/article/wnba-players-not-reaping-leagues-revenue-gains/) (context piece; treat as reporting, not policy).

### Inferences
- Aggregate structural figures for 2025-26: 30 x cap = $4.64B; 30 x tax line = $5.64B; 30 x floor = $4.18B. The league-accounted $6.27B salaries+benefits for 2025-26 sits above 30 x tax line, consistent with most teams spending above the cap via Bird rights and exceptions (soft-cap behaviour).
- The 2026-27 cap of $164,961,000 shown on Basketball-Reference indicates a further ~6.7% rise, below the 10% maximum.
