## MLB Key Question 1: Current national media rights deals and combined league-wide AAV (plus local/RSN estimate)

### Takeaway
MLB's national package for 2026-2028 consists of five deals: Fox (~$729M/yr, through 2028), Turner/TBS (~$470M-$535M/yr depending on source, through 2028), and the three new three-year deals announced November 19, 2025 with ESPN (~$550M/yr, now built around MLB.TV distribution), NBCUniversal/Peacock (~$200M/yr) and Netflix (~$50M/yr; Forbes reported ~$35M). Best-sourced combined national AAV is roughly $2.0B-$2.1B per year (reported/estimated), and Ampere Analysis independently pegs national rights at ~$2.1B for 2026 with local/regional rights at a further ~$1.6B.

### Cited Findings
- On November 19-20, 2025 MLB announced three-year (2026-2028) national rights agreements with ESPN, NBCUniversal and Netflix; AP reported "The three deals will average nearly $800 million per year" and that "ESPN will still pay $550 million while the NBC deal is worth $200 million and Netflix $50 million." (REPORTED, AP) — [AP via World Baseball Network, Nov 20, 2025](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/)
- Same AP story on the legacy deals: "MLB receives an average of $729 million from Fox and $470 million from Turner Sports per year," both expiring after the 2028 season. (REPORTED, AP) — [AP via World Baseball Network](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/)
- ESPN package (2026-28): rights to license and distribute MLB.TV (the out-of-market package, now on the ESPN app), in-market streaming rights for six clubs (San Diego, Colorado, Arizona, Cleveland, Minnesota, Seattle), and 30 exclusive national games (mostly summer weeknights); ESPN loses Sunday Night Baseball, its postseason games and the Home Run Derby. — [AP via World Baseball Network](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/); [ESPN press story](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/47026249/espn-mlb-reach-new-3-year-media-agreement)
- NBCUniversal package (2026-28): Sunday Night Baseball (25 games, mostly NBC, rest on NBC Sports Network, all streaming on Peacock), the Wild Card Series, Sunday early-afternoon games on Peacock, the Futures Game, first-round draft coverage and a Labor Day prime-time game. — [AP via World Baseball Network](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/)
- Netflix package (2026-28): the Home Run Derby, two additional games (including the March 25 season opener and the Aug. 13 Field of Dreams game) and one MLB special-event game per year. — [AP via World Baseball Network](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/)
- CNBC (Nov 19, 2025, from search excerpt; full page returned 403) also reported ESPN at ~$550M/yr, NBC at ~$200M/yr and Netflix at ~$50M/yr for the Derby, a total of ~$800M/yr "according to a source familiar with the figures," and noted ESPN's old deal had also been $550M/yr, so MLB "had to take a haircut of about $300 million per year relative to what ESPN had been paying" for the Sunday night/wild-card/Derby package. (REPORTED, CNBC) — [CNBC, Nov 19, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/19/mlb-media-rights-deals-nbc-espn-netflix.html); earlier CNBC scoop Aug 22, 2025 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/22/mlbrrights-espn-nbc-netflix.html)
- Forbes (Maury Brown, Nov 19, 2025; search excerpt, page returned 403) put the Netflix deal "in the $35 million a year range," lower than the AP/CNBC $50M figure. Deadline reported the Wall Street Journal pegged the three-deal total at $800M/yr. (REPORTED, conflicting on Netflix) — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maurybrown/2025/11/19/mlb-announces-3-year-media-rights-deals-with-espn-nbcuniversal-and-netflix/); [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/11/major-league-baseball-rights-deals-espn-netflix-nbcuniversal-1236623833/)
- Fox deal: a seven-year, $5.1 billion extension covering 2022-2028, "according to Eric Fisher of SportsBusiness Journal" (≈$728.6M/yr). Note AP's $470M/yr average for Turner conflicts with SBJ's $535M. (REPORTED, conflicting) — [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/media/2020/tbs-extends-mlb-deal-for-3-75-billion-1234613684/); [SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/mlb-finalizes-seven-year-rights-extension-with-turner/); [Bloomberg via BQ](https://www.bloombergquint.com/onweb/at-t-s-turner-to-pay-3-75-billion-in-new-baseball-rights-deal)

(ESTIMATED, Forbes) — [Forbes, Mar 2023](https://www.forbes.com/sites/mikeozanian/2023/03/23/baseballs-most-valuable-teams-2023-price-tags-are-up-12-despite-regional-tv-woes/)
- Labor-linked contract note: The Athletic reported (Sept 28, 2026) that a lockout costing at least 60 games in 2027 could extend most national media contracts through 2029. (REPORTED) — [CBS Sports timeline](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/); [Yahoo/SB Nation](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/looming-mlb-owners-lockout-140000077.html)

### Inferences
- Summed national AAV for 2026-2028 (reported figures): Fox $729M + Turner $470M (AP) or $535M (SBJ) + ESPN $550M + NBC $200M + Netflix $50M (or $35M Forbes) = approximately $1.98B-$2.06B per year. This bracket is consistent with Ampere's independent ~$2.1B national estimate for 2026.
- Total national + local rights to MLB clubs in 2026 ≈ $3.7B (Ampere), i.e., national ~57% / local ~43%.
- The ESPN deal's $550M/yr headline is not like-for-like with the old $550M/yr: the new package is anchored on MLB.TV distribution (previously a direct-to-consumer revenue line for MLB), so the true incremental rights fee for the games alone is lower than the headline (CNBC's "$300M haircut" framing).

### Gaps
- No official (league or network) confirmation of any of the per-deal dollar values; all are reported by AP/CNBC/WSJ/SBJ from unnamed sources. The Turner AAV discrepancy ($470M AP vs. $535M SBJ) and Netflix ($50M AP/CNBC vs. $35M Forbes) could not be resolved.
- Ampere's methodology (how it values MLB.tv-held local rights for 14 teams) was not available in the summaries read.

## MLB Key Question 2: Total player payroll, most recent season

### Takeaway
No source read publishes a single official league-wide MLB payroll total for 2026; the AP's April 9, 2026 Opening Day study gives a record average of $5,335,966 across 942 rostered players (implying ≈$5.03B, researcher calculation), while The Baseball Cube's 2026 compilation shows ≈$6.13B under a broader methodology. For the completed 2025 season, the commissioner's office's final CBT accounting showed a record $402.6M in luxury tax paid by nine clubs (AP), with the Dodgers at $345.3M payroll plus $169.4M tax.

### Cited Findings

## MLB Key Question 3: Salary cap structure — no cap, Competitive Balance Tax; 2026 thresholds and rates; CBA expiry and October 2026 labor status

### Takeaway
MLB has no salary cap; it uses the Competitive Balance Tax with a 2026 base threshold of $244M and base rates of 20%/30%/50% for first/second/third-plus consecutive years over, plus surcharges at $20M/$40M/$60M over (effective top rate 110%). The 2022-26 CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, 2026; owners formally proposed a $245.3M salary cap and $171.2M floor for 2027 (May 28, 2026), the MLBPA has rejected any cap and countered with a "competitive integrity tax" and a $300M CBT threshold, and as of October 7-20, 2026 a December 1 lockout is widely expected but not yet announced.

### Cited Findings
- MLB.com glossary (via search excerpt; direct fetch returned HTTP 406): CBT threshold for 2026 under the 2022-26 CBA is $244 million; tax rate 20% for a first-year payor, 30% for a second consecutive year, 50% for a third or more consecutive year; surcharges for clubs $20M-$40M over the threshold (12%), $40M-$60M over (42.5% first year / 45% subsequent), and $60M+ over (60%), i.e., breakpoints at $264M, $284M and $304M; a club that falls below the threshold resets its penalty level. (OFFICIAL, MLB.com) — [MLB.com Competitive Balance Tax glossary](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/transactions/competitive-balance-tax)
- Effective stacked rates for a third-year-plus payor (Dodgers 2025 breakdown): 50% on the first $20M over, 62% on the next $20M, 95% on the next $20M, 110% above $60M over. (REPORTED, TrueBlueLA) — [TrueBlueLA](https://www.truebluela.com/dodgers-payroll/108038/dodgers-payroll-competitive-balance-tax-2025)
- Spotrac's 2026 team tax pages apply the $244M threshold with 12%/45%/60% surcharges at $264M/$284M/$304M, a 50% base rate for the Yankees and Dodgers (third-year-plus) and 30% for Boston (second year). (ESTIMATED, Spotrac) — [Spotrac Dodgers tax](https://www.spotrac.com/mlb/los-angeles-dodgers/tax); [Spotrac Yankees tax](https://www.spotrac.com/mlb/new-york-yankees/tax); [Spotrac Red Sox tax](https://www.spotrac.com/mlb/boston-red-sox/tax/_/year/2026)
- Dodgers projected to pay about $180M in luxury tax for 2026. (ESTIMATED, CBS Sports) — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-proposes-salary-cap-floor-cba-negotiations-mlbpa/)
- CBA expiry: "The 2022-26 Basic Agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET" on Dec. 1, 2026; formal negotiations began May 12, 2026. (OFFICIAL date, reported by CBS) — [CBS Sports timeline, Oct 8, 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/); [MLB.com fan FAQ](https://www.mlb.com/dbacks/news/cba-fan-faq)
- Owners' proposal (May 28, 2026): salary cap of $245.3M for 2027 (including benefits), salary floor of $171.2M (including benefits), 50-50 revenue split, centralized/equally shared local revenues, and an NBA/NHL-style escrow system; "No club would be permitted to exceed the cap, and all 30 clubs would be required to meet the floor." Based on luxury-tax payroll estimates, nine teams would be above the cap and 12 below the floor. (REPORTED, CBS/Bleacher Report) — [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-proposes-salary-cap-floor-cba-negotiations-mlbpa/); [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25431848-mlb-proposes-salary-cap-part-cba-negotiations-players-full-details-revealed); [CBS timeline](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/)
- MLBPA's first proposal (May 27, 2026): a "competitive integrity tax" on clubs that fail to spend a minimum on payroll, minimum salary raised from $780,000 to $1.5M, CBT threshold raised from "$244 million to $300 million," a guaranteed "minimum of $240m in revenue every season" for small-market clubs, and free agency after five years for players 30 or older; MLB's Glen Caplin said the union plan would give the Dodgers "$70 million to spend on payroll." (REPORTED, CBS) — [CBS Sports timeline](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/); [CBS Sports on MLBPA proposal](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlbpa-mlb-proposal-collective-bargaining-agreement-lockout/)
- Subsequent 2026 timeline: June 1 MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer called a cap "a form of institutionalized collusion"; June 18 MLB proposed a 12-round draft with $200M hard bonus pool and an international draft; June 25 MLB proposed cap-tied contract maximums ($202M/5 years for players changing teams, $265M/6 years for "Cornerstone" players) and minimum salaries of $1M (2+ years service) / $900K; Aug. 27 roster-rule proposals contingent on a cap-and-floor; Oct. 8 MLB proposed a 154-game season and best-of-seven Division Series, also contingent on the cap-and-floor system. (REPORTED, CBS) — [CBS Sports timeline](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/)
- Lockout preparation (Oct 7, 2026): MLB sent clubs a memo on conduct during an expected lockout; deputy commissioner Dan Halem wrote "clubs need to be prepared for the possibility that an agreement will not be reached by then" while "Our commitment has been and remains to exhaust every realistic option"; MLBTR: "There's virtually no chance there'll be a new CBA in place by then" and the league will "immediately implement a lockout to freeze MLB offseason activity." (REPORTED, MLBTR citing The Athletic's Evan Drellich) — [MLB Trade Rumors, Oct 7, 2026](https://www.mlbtraderumors.com/2026/10/mlb-sends-memo-to-teams-outlining-lockout-procedures.html)
- Status as of Oct 20, 2026: Axios framed the World Series under a looming lockout; Manfred's last widely quoted statement (August) was that the sides were "meeting regularly" and "We want to play baseball next year." No lockout had been formally announced as of the pages read. (REPORTED) — [Axios, Oct 20, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/20/world-series-mlb-lockout-labor); [Yahoo/SB Nation, Oct 7, 2026](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/looming-mlb-owners-lockout-140000077.html); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/inside-mlb-cba-negotiations-manfred-players-union-exec-discuss-issues-sides-attempt-save-2027-season)
- Precedent: the previous lockout lasted 99 days, ending March 10, 2022. — [Nolo explainer](https://www.nolo.com/news/the-mlb-lockout-explained-a-legal-guide-to-the-labor-law-behind-the-fight.html)

### Inferences
- Structure to document: "No salary cap / Competitive Balance Tax (luxury tax)" with 2026 tiers $244M / $264M / $284M / $304M; a first-time payor's marginal rates are 20% / 32% / 62.5% / 80%; a third-time-plus payor's are 50% / 62% / 95% / 110%.
- The owners' $245.3M cap figure for 2027 is essentially the 2026 CBT threshold plus a small bump (and includes benefits), which is why the union characterizes it as a hard freeze on top-end spending.

### Gaps
- The MLB.com glossary could not be fetched directly (HTTP 406), so the 2022-2025 thresholds ($230M/$233M/$237M/$241M under the 2022-26 CBA) are from the researcher's prior knowledge of the agreement and should be verified at the glossary URL above; only the 2026 $244M figure and the rate schedule were confirmed via search excerpts.
- No report of a specific bargaining session or offer between Oct 8 and Oct 21, 2026 was found; the "far apart" characterization is CBS's as of Oct 8.

## NHL Key Question 1: National media rights deals and combined AAV (US-only and all-national incl. Canada)

### Takeaway
The NHL's US national deals (2021-22 through 2027-28) are reported at ~$400M/yr (ESPN/ABC/Hulu, ~$2.8B/7 yrs) and ~$225M/yr (Turner/TNT, $1.575B/7 yrs per SBJ), ≈$625M/yr combined (Sportico: "more than $600 million per year"). The new 12-year Rogers Canadian deal (2026-27 through 2037-38) is C$11B (~US$7.7B), ≈C$917M/yr or ≈US$640M/yr on a straight average (escalating, so lower early), bringing all-national AAV from 2026-27 to roughly US$1.27B/yr.

### Cited Findings
- US deals: "Per Sports Business Journal, Turner will pay $225 million per year for the rights. The previously agreed-upon ESPN deal is for approximately $400 million per year"; SBJ "tabbed it at $1.575 billion over the seven years"; combined "more than $625 million per year," up from ~$300M under NBC/Disney streaming. Seven years each, starting 2021-22; Turner carries up to 72 regular-season games, half the playoffs and the Stanley Cup Final in 2023, 2025, 2027. (REPORTED, SBJ via Daily Faceoff/Awful Announcing) — [Daily Faceoff, Apr 2021](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/nhl-inks-seven-year-broadcast-deal-with-turner-sports); [Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/nhl/turners-nhl-deal-will-include-up-to-72-exclusive-national-games-each-season-half-the-stanley-cup-playoffs-hbo-max-streaming.html); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/warnermedia-turner-nhl-tv-rights-nbc-out); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/04/27/sports/turner-sports-nhl-announce-seven-year-broadcast-rights-deal)
- Sportico (via Daily Faceoff): the two US agreements with ESPN and TNT are worth roughly "$600 million per year" combined and "run through the 2027-28 season." (REPORTED, Sportico) — [Daily Faceoff, Mar 31, 2025](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/report-nhl-rogers-reach-12-year-canadian-television-deal)

- US-only national AAV (2025-26 season): ≈$625M/yr (ESPN ~$400M + Turner ~$225M), SBJ-sourced; Sportico rounds to ">$600M."

### Gaps

## NHL Key Question 2: Total NHL player payroll, most recent season

### Takeaway
No source read publishes an official league-wide sum of NHL player salaries for 2025-26; the structural answer is that players receive 50% of hockey-related revenue (HRR), which the league put at ~US$6.5B for 2024-25 and projected at $6.8B (Dec 2025) then $7.5B-$8B in mixed US/Canadian currency (June 2026) for 2025-26, implying a players' share on the order of $3.25B-$4B depending on currency basis. A cap-arithmetic bound for 2025-26 is 32 teams × $95.5M upper limit = $3.06B maximum, with the 32-team midpoint sum ≈ $2.66B.

### Cited Findings
- 2024-25 HRR: "NHL revenues rose around three per cent last season to US$6.5 billion." (REPORTED, SportsPro) — [SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/insights/nhl-ice-hockey-business-revenue-sponsorship-media-rights-data/); NHL's own 2024 business review had projected "more than $6.6 billion" — [NHL 2024 Year in Review PDF](https://media.nhl.com/site/asset/public/ext/2024-25/2024YearInReview_BusinessNHL.pdf)

(OFFICIAL remarks, FOS) — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nhl-projects-record-8b-in-revenue-sees-bigger-growth-ahead); [FOS on X](https://x.com/FOS/status/2066309963970908594)

### Inferences
- Players' share of HRR (50%) for 2024-25 ≈ US$3.25B on the ~$6.5B figure; for 2025-26, between ≈$3.4B (on $6.8B) and ≈$3.75B-$4.0B (on the $7.5B-$8B mixed-currency figure). Mixed-currency figures overstate USD totals because Canadian revenue is counted at par.
- Cap-basis bound for 2025-26: 32 × $95.5M = $3.056B is the theoretical maximum of regular-season cap hits (most clubs operate at or just under the ceiling, using LTIR), and 32 × $83.05M midpoint = $2.66B; actual summed cap hits almost certainly fall between ~$2.9B and $3.06B. Cash payroll differs from cap hits because of signing-bonus timing and escrow.

## NHL Key Question 3: Hard cap figures 2025-26 / 2026-27 / 2027-28 and CBA status

### Takeaway
The NHL and NHLPA jointly announced (Jan 31, 2025) a three-season hard-cap schedule: $95.5M upper / $70.6M lower for 2025-26, $104M / $76.9M for 2026-27, and $113.5M / $83.9M for 2027-28, and the NHL and NHLPA ratified (July 8, 2025) a four-year CBA extension that runs from 2026-27 through the 2029-30 season (the prior CBA expired September 2026), reportedly including an 84-game schedule and a playoff salary cap.

### Cited Findings
- NHL/NHLPA joint announcement, January 31, 2025: team payroll ranges — 2025-26 upper limit $95.5M, lower limit $70.6M; 2026-27 upper $104M, lower $76.9M; 2027-28 upper $113.5M, lower $83.9M; covering "a minimum of the next three years (through 2027-28)," with the 2026-27 and 2027-28 increases "subject to the Collective Bargaining Agreement being in effect beyond the 2025-26 season." (OFFICIAL) — [NHL.com](https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-nhlpa-announce-team-payroll-ranges-for-next-3-seasons-through-2027-28)
- The January 2025 memo allowed "potential minor increases up or down" for 2026-27 and 2027-28; the $104M figure for 2026-27 was subsequently confirmed, making the maximum individual salary (20% of the upper limit) $20.8M. (OFFICIAL/REPORTED) — [Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/nhl-salary-cap-estimates-released-for-next-three-seasons-expected-to-surpass-100-million-in-2026-27); [theScore](https://beta.thescore.com/news/3536553); [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nhl-salary-cap-hits-104-234313010.html); [ESPN, Jan 2025](https://africa.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/43640473/nhl-growth-prompts-significant-salary-cap-spike)

### Inferences
- Structure to document: "Hard cap with floor (50/50 HRR split, escrow)." Midpoints (researcher arithmetic): 2025-26 $83.05M; 2026-27 $90.45M; 2027-28 $98.7M.
