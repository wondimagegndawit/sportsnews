# Power Four sports sponsorship: how many varsity sports, roster sizes, NCAA minimums, per-sport economics, and 2024-26 cuts/additions

Scope note: "Power Four" here = post-2024 membership, 68 schools: ACC 18 (Boston College, California, Clemson, Duke, Florida State, Georgia Tech, Louisville, Miami, North Carolina, NC State, Pittsburgh, SMU, Stanford, Syracuse, Virginia, Virginia Tech, Wake Forest, plus Notre Dame as an ACC member in sports other than football); Big Ten 18 (Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Maryland, Michigan, Michigan State, Minnesota, Nebraska, Northwestern, Ohio State, Oregon, Penn State, Purdue, Rutgers, UCLA, USC, Washington, Wisconsin); Big 12 16 (Arizona, Arizona State, Baylor, BYU, UCF, Cincinnati, Colorado, Houston, Iowa State, Kansas, Kansas State, Oklahoma State, TCU, Texas Tech, Utah, West Virginia); SEC 16 (Alabama, Arkansas, Auburn, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, LSU, Mississippi, Mississippi State, Missouri, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Texas A&M, Vanderbilt). All 68 were matched in the EADA 2024-25 and 2023-24 institution files (no school missing).

Two different "counts" exist and they are not interchangeable:
1. NCAA "sports sponsored" (what schools and the NCAA Manual mean by "16 varsity sports"): men's cross country, indoor track and outdoor track are three separate sports; swimming and diving is one sport; coed sailing counts once.
2. EADA "varsity teams" (US Dept. of Education, ope.ed.gov/athletics): schools may report cross country + indoor + outdoor track as ONE line item ("All Track Combined", EADA sport code 3). 67 of the 68 Power Four schools used "All Track Combined" for 2024-25 (all but Stanford), so EADA team counts run roughly 2-4 below each school's own "varsity sports" count. Example: Georgia Tech says 17 varsity sports; EADA shows 13 teams. Mississippi State says 16; EADA shows 13. Ohio State says 36; EADA shows 35. Use EADA for cross-school comparisons (uniform source, uniform year), and the school's own figure when quoting "X sponsors N sports."

## Key question 1: What does the NCAA Sports Sponsorship and Participation Rates Report (2024-25 edition) say about the average number of sports per school?

### Takeaway
The NCAA's September 2025 report gives averages by division only, not by FBS/autonomy: the average Division I school sponsored 19.2 teams (8.5 men's, 10.7 women's) and had 576.9 student-athletes in 2024-25. It does not publish an FBS or Power Four average; the closest proxy is the EADA computation in Key question 2 (Power Four mean 18.1 EADA teams; all FBS 16.6).

### Inferences
- Since D-I averages 19.2 teams and the EADA-based Power Four mean is 18.1 EADA teams (which undercounts track by 2-4 per school), the typical Power Four school sponsors roughly 20-22 NCAA-countable sports, i.e., in line with or slightly above the D-I average, while the Big 12 and SEC medians sit near the FBS 16-sport floor.
- GAO's 587 student-athletes per Power school (2023-24, NCAA financial data) matches the EADA 2024-25 Power Four mean of 589 unduplicated athletes computed below, so ~590 athletes per department is a defensible benchmark.

### Gaps
- No NCAA-published FBS, autonomy or Power Four average number of sports; the NCAA report only goes to the division level. The 2025-26 edition of the report (covering 2025-26) is not yet published as of Oct. 27, 2026 (the latest file is the Sept. 2025 edition covering 2024-25).

## Key question 3: NCAA Division I sport-sponsorship minimums (FBS, FCS, Division I-wide)

### Takeaway
Bylaw 20.9.9.1 (2026-27 Division I Manual, numbering unchanged from the 2020-era citations) requires FBS members to sponsor at least 16 varsity sports including football: at least six all-male or mixed-team sports and at least eight all-female sports (up to two emerging sports countable). The Division I-wide floor (20.9.6) is 14 (7 men's/mixed + 7 women's, or 6 + 8, each with at least two team sports); FCS (20.9.10.1) is 14 including football (7+7 or 6+8).

### Cited Findings
- Bylaw 20.9.9.1 Sports Sponsorship [FBS], full text: "The institution shall sponsor a minimum of 16 varsity intercollegiate sports, including football, based on the minimum sports sponsorship and scheduling requirements set forth in Bylaws 20.9.6 and 20.9.6.3, including a minimum of six sports involving all-male teams or mixed teams of males and females, and a minimum of eight varsity intercollegiate sports (of which a maximum of two emerging sports per Bylaw 20.02.7 may be used) based on the minimum sports sponsorship and scheduling requirements set forth in Bylaws 20.9.6 and 20.9.6.3 and involving all-female teams, subject to the waiver provision in Bylaw 20.9.6.2. (Revised: 1/11/94 effective 9/1/94, 4/25/02 effective 8/1/04)" — [NCAA 2026-27 Division I Manual (LSDBi report 90008, generated 10/27/26), Bylaw 20.9.9.1, p. 353](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Bylaw 20.9.9.5 Waivers [FBS]: "There shall be no waivers of the Football Bowl Subdivision membership requirements." Bylaw 20.9.9.4: an FBS school that fails any requirement gets notice; a second failure within 10 years leads to restricted membership (ineligible for postseason football) — [same Manual, Bylaws 20.9.9.4-20.9.9.5](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Bylaw 20.9.9.3 Financial Aid Requirements [FBS]: (a) award an average of at least 90% of the permissible maximum grants-in-aid (based on 2024-25 limits) over a rolling two-year period in all sports used to meet the sponsorship requirement; and (b) annually award at least 210 athletics grants-in-aid and spend at least $6 million on grants-in-aid (revised 10/4/23 effective 8/1/27 for current FBS members; revised 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25) — [same Manual, Bylaw 20.9.9.3](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Bylaw 20.9.6 Sports Sponsorship [Division I-wide]: a member must sponsor teams in a minimum of (a) seven varsity sports including at least two team sports for all-male/mixed teams and seven (max two emerging sports) including at least two team sports for all-female teams; or (b) six men's/mixed and eight women's, each with at least two team sports = 14 sports — [same Manual, Bylaw 20.9.6, p. 349](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Bylaw 20.9.10.1 Sports Sponsorship [FCS]: minimum of (a) seven varsity sports including football for men's/mixed teams and seven for women (max two emerging), or (b) six including football and eight women's = 14 sports including football — [same Manual, Bylaw 20.9.10.1](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Legislative history: the 16-sport FBS (then Division I-A) requirement with "at least six men's and eight women's sports" was adopted in 2002 effective Aug. 1, 2004, with the intent "to eliminate all waivers of the additional Division I-A membership requirements" — [NCAA LSDBi proposal record](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=223); 2001 NCAA News preview of the proposal — [NCAA News archive, Aug. 13, 2001](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ncaanewsarchive/2001/Division-I/grid-panel-weighs-in-on-membership-criteria-issue---8-13-01.html)
- The rule binds in practice: Central Michigan fell to five men's sports after cutting men's track in 2020 and needed an NCAA waiver for 2020-21 and 2021-22 to stay FBS-compliant under Bylaw 20.9.9.1 — [Central Michigan Life, June 2020](https://www.cm-life.com/article/2020/06/central-michigan-chippewas-fbs-compliant-michael-alford-story); the Division I Council in 2020 rejected a COVID-era request for a blanket waiver of the 16-sport FBS minimum — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/node/23760)

### Inferences
- Because the eight-women's-sports floor is higher than the six-men's floor, Power Four cuts since 2024 have fallen on women's sports only where a replacement women's sport was added (Iowa State) or where the school was well above the floor (Utah), and on non-sport "events" (Virginia diving, Washington State field events) that do not reduce the sponsored-sport count.

### Gaps
- NCAA LSDBi proposal 2023-41 references the FBS requirements as "Bylaw 20.10.9" with sponsorship at 20.10.9.1 — [NCAA LSDBi proposal 107256](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=107256) — but the 2026-27 Manual text retrieved today numbers them 20.9.9/20.9.9.1; cite 20.9.9.1 and note the proposal-era numbering discrepancy.

## Key question 5: Per-sport economics — only football and men's basketball have positive median net revenue

### Takeaway
The NCAA's most recent sport-level medians (FY2016, Division I Revenues and Expenses Report 2004-2016) show that among FBS autonomy schools only football (median net generated revenue +$19.0M) and men's basketball (+$2.8M) were in the black; every other sport had a negative median, from -$0.2M (men's fencing, women's sand volleyball) to -$3.2M (women's basketball). The NCAA stopped publishing sport-level tables after that edition; its December 2025 report gives only department-level medians (FBS autonomy median net generated revenue -$15M in FY2024).

## Key question 6: Recent sport cuts or additions at Power Four schools (2024-2026), including post-House roster-limit effects

### Takeaway
Only a handful of Power Four sport-sponsorship changes occurred in 2024-26: Utah dropped women's beach volleyball (announced Apr. 29, 2025, effective end of 2024-25); Virginia "divested" from diving (Mar. 2025, not a separate NCAA sport); Iowa State discontinued women's gymnastics (Mar. 3, 2026) and added women's wrestling (Apr. 16, 2026, competing 2027-28); Nebraska added women's flag football (Jan. 16, 2026, first season spring 2028). None of the four schools cited the House settlement as the reason; the settlement-driven cuts (Cal Poly swimming/diving, UTEP and ULM women's tennis, Grand Canyon men's volleyball, Radford tennis, Washington State track consolidation) were outside the Power Four.
