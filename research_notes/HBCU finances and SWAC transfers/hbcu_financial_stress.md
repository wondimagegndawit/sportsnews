Scope note: debt levels and athletics budgets are excluded (covered by other researchers). Every figure below carries its data year and source. Source-quality flags: **[primary]** = government/agency/original report read directly; **[secondary]** = reputable press or think tank relaying a primary source; **[weak]** = trade blog, advocacy explainer, or a figure I saw only in a search-result summary and could not open. ## 1. State underfunding of land-grant HBCUs (2023 federal letters) and follow-up through 2026

### Takeaway
On Sept. 18, 2023 the US Secretaries of Education and Agriculture told 16 governors that their 1890 land-grant HBCUs had been underfunded by a combined $12-13 billion in state per-student appropriations over 1987-2020; six SWAC members account for roughly $5.3 billion of that. As of late 2026 no state has paid the federal figure: Tennessee re-labelled existing capital money, Mississippi's repayment bill died, and the only live litigation is the Florida A&M student suit revived by the 11th Circuit in June 2026.

### Cited Findings

**The letters and methodology**
- Letters were sent Sept. 18, 2023 by Education Secretary Miguel Cardona and Agriculture Secretary Tom Vilsack to 16 governors; the headline gap was "more than $12 billion." HBCU land-grants exist in 18 states, but Delaware and Ohio were found to have funded theirs equitably. [primary, via search summary of the USDA release] — [USDA press release](https://www.usda.gov/node/54759); [Higher Ed Dive, Sept 2023](https://highereddive.com/news/16-states-underfunded-land-grant-hbcus-by-over-12b-biden-admin-says/694020)
- Method: NCES/IPEDS data for 1987-2020; each 1890 institution's state appropriation per student was compared with the same state's 1862 (originally white) land-grant, and the gap is what the HBCU would have received at the 1862 school's per-student level. [secondary] — [The 74 / States Newsroom, Sept 2023](https://www.the74million.org/article/states-urged-by-biden-administration-to-rectify-underfunding-of-land-grant-hbcus/)
- The letters asked governors to close the gap over several budget years or, failing that, to make "a substantial state allocation toward the 1890 deficit" plus a forward-looking 2-to-1 state match of federal land-grant dollars, without cutting other institutions, and warned of "costly litigation." [secondary] — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/states-urged-by-biden-administration-to-rectify-underfunding-of-land-grant-hbcus/)
- Total is reported three ways: "more than $12 billion" (USDA/ED release), "$13 billion" (Inside Higher Ed, NPR), and "$12.6 billion" (Brookings, Oct 2026). All derive from the same letters. — [Inside Higher Ed](https://insidehighered.com/node/126596); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/09/18/1200230948/land-grant-hbcus-funding); [Brookings, Oct 28, 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)

**Follow-up by state, 2023-2026**
- **Tennessee (TSU, $2.1B):** The state has not accepted the federal figure as a debt; a Comptroller spokesperson said historic underfunding "isn't what's at issue." A separate state legislative analysis had put TSU's land-grant match underfunding at up to $544 million, and in April 2022 Tennessee appropriated $250 million to TSU for capital/infrastructure. In June 2025 TSU and the state signed an agreement letting TSU redirect $96 million of that $250 million to operations over three years ($99M stays in deferred maintenance; $55M remains for a $33M electrical-grid upgrade and $22M in agriculture buildings). In other words, the "remedy" money was partly converted into a cash-flow bridge. — [Tennessee Lookout, June 17, 2025](https://tennesseelookout.com/2025/06/17/tennessee-state-signs-financial-operating-agreement-with-state/); [Higher Ed Dive, June 18, 2025](https://www.highereddive.com/news/tennessee-state-operations-96m-state-funds-dwayne-tucker-jason-mumpower/751125/); [NewsChannel 5](https://newschannel5.com/news/tsu-is-owed-over-2-billion-lawmaker-calls-on-state-to-fully-fund-tennessee-state-university)
- **Mississippi (Alcorn, $257.8M):** HB 1896 (2024 session) would have appropriated exactly $257,807,216 to the IHL board for Alcorn; it died in committee March 27, 2024. — [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/MS/text/HB1896/2024)
- **Florida (FAMU, $1.97B):** A class action by FAMU students (filed 2022) alleging discriminatory underfunding and program duplication was dismissed by US District Judge Robert Hinkle in January 2024 for lack of proof of intentional discrimination/de jure segregation. The 11th Circuit revived it in June 2026 (reported as June 9, 2026), holding the dismissal came too early for plaintiffs to develop evidence. It is not a merits win. — [Higher Ed Dive, Jan 2024](https://www.highereddive.com/news/judge-dismisses-discrimination-lawsuit-famu-florida/705933/); [Bloomberg Law, June 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/florida-a-m-university-underfunding-suit-revived-on-appeal); [JBHE, June 2026](https://jbhe.com/2026/06/previously-dismissed-florida-am-university-underfunding-lawsuit-moves-forward/)
- **Maryland (precedent, pre-letter):** A 2006 federal lawsuit over underfunding and program duplication at Maryland's four public HBCUs was settled for $577 million over 10 years; Gov. Hogan signed the bill in March 2021, a federal judge approved it in May 2021, and payments began in FY2023 (first year: $24M Morgan State, $16.8M Bowie State, $9.7M UMES, $9M Coppin State). Sources differ on whether $22M in legal fees is inside the $577M ($555M + $22M) or on top. Brookings (Oct 2026) describes it as a "15-year commitment," which conflicts with the 10-year description in contemporaneous reporting. — [Maryland Matters, Apr 28, 2021](https://marylandmatters.org/2021/04/28/maryland-settles-hbcu-federal-lawsuit-for-577-million/); [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/education/maryland-gov-larry-hogan-to-sign-577m-hbcu-settlement-bill); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/maryland-governor-signs-bill-giving-577m-to-state-hbcus-to-end-long-runnin/597292/); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)
- **Georgia:** The Legislature gave Savannah State (not the 1890 school) a $42.8 million capital investment in 2025, its largest ever. — [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/)
- **Federal follow-through lapsed:** The letters were a Biden-administration initiative. The April 23, 2025 Trump executive order rescinded the Biden HBCU orders and moved the White House Initiative on HBCUs into the Executive Office of the President; about 16 months later the initiative's site showed no advisory-board meeting and no annual report. — [Brookings, Sept 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-actions-on-higher-education-arent-impacting-hbcus-yet/); [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/)

### Inferences
- The six SWAC land-grant members named in the letters (FAMU $1.97B, Southern $1.1B, Prairie View $1.1B, Alabama A&M $527.3M, UAPB $330.9M, Alcorn $257.8M) sum to roughly **$5.3 billion**, about 40% of the national total. This is my arithmetic on the table, not a published figure. The other SWAC members (e.g., Jackson State, Grambling, Alabama State, Texas Southern, Mississippi Valley State, Bethune-Cookman) are not 1890 land-grants, so the letters produced no figure for them; their absence is not evidence they were equitably funded.
- The 15 table figures sum to about $10.9B; adding NC A&T's ~$2B gives ~$12.9B, which explains the "$12B" vs "$13B" headlines.
- The figures are a per-student comparison against one flagship per state, not a legal judgment or an audited debt. An advocacy brief should call them a "federal estimate," and note that states (Tennessee explicitly) dispute them.
- Three years on, the record supports the claim that the letters produced no state repayment plan matching the federal estimate; the strongest dollar remedy in the record (Maryland, $577M) came from 15 years of litigation, not from the letters.

### Gaps
- I did not open each of the 16 individual letters; the table comes from States Newsroom's compilation. The exact NC A&T figure was given only as "$2 billion" in text.
- No reliable 2025-2026 update found for Alabama, Arkansas, Louisiana or Texas action on their figures (no source found either way).
- I found no confirmation of any new state-level settlement or of a DOJ/ED enforcement action following the letters.

## 2. Endowment and philanthropy gap

### Takeaway
HBCU endowments are a rounding error in US higher-education wealth: HBCUs were 1.4% of institutions reporting to NACUBO in FY2025 but held 0.3% of $953.7 billion, and per-student endowments run from about one-half (public) to one-fifth or less (private) of non-HBCU peers depending on the source and year. The practical consequence is no cushion against a bad enrollment or appropriations year.

### Cited Findings
- **NACUBO FY2025:** HBCUs comprised 1.4% of the 678 institutions reporting but held 0.3% of the $953.7 billion in reported endowment assets; the 32 institutions with endowments above $5 billion held 57.4%. The 10 largest HBCU endowments total $2.4 billion vs $340 billion for the 10 largest predominantly white institutions (more than 140:1). [secondary, citing NACUBO-Commonfund] — [Brookings, Oct 28, 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)
- Same Brookings piece: public HBCUs hold "roughly one-third" the endowment per student of comparable publics, and private HBCUs "roughly one-eighth" that of private non-HBCUs (no source or data year given in the text; treat as approximate). — [Brookings, Oct 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)
- **CEA (2021 data):** per full-time student, public HBCU endowments were about 50% of public non-HBCU endowments, and private HBCU endowments about 21% of private non-HBCU endowments. [primary] — [CEA, May 2024](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/cea/written-materials/2024/05/16/the-economics-of-hbcus)
- **Century Foundation (2021):** search summary reports non-HBCU endowment per FTE at 3x (public) and 4x (private) HBCU levels; the AJC (Aug 2026) cites the same foundation for a 7x private gap. The ratios conflict across summaries of the same work; I did not open the TCF report. [secondary] — [TCF commentary](https://tcf.org/content/commentary/its-time-to-create-a-federal-hbcu-endowment-fund/); [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/)
- **ACE/UNCF (Jan 2019; IPEDS data through ~2015):** within both sectors HBCU endowments "lag behind those of non-HBCUs by at least 70 percent"; per FTE student, public HBCU endowment was about 20% of public non-HBCU, and private HBCU about 27% of private non-HBCU. [primary] — [ACE/UNCF issue brief (Williams & Davis)](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/bitstream/handle/10919/89184/PublicPrivateHbcus.pdf)
- **GAO via UNCF (2022 report):** HBCUs average $15,000 in endowment per student vs $410,000 at comparable non-HBCUs. [secondary; UNCF attributes this to a GAO study I did not open] — [UNCF, "Greater Funding, Greater Needs," July 2022](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Patterson-HBCUs-funding-report_V6.pdf)
- **Public vs private HBCUs (Nov 2025):** average public HBCU endowment is $41 million vs $133 million at private HBCUs, though public HBCUs serve about three times as many undergraduates. — [TMCF / Payne Center, "The American Dividend," Nov 7, 2025](https://tmcf.org/?p=7288)
- **Land-grant comparison:** non-HBCU land-grants collectively hold about $45 billion in endowment assets vs about $1 billion for land-grant HBCUs. [weak: seen only in a search summary attributed to ACE's Higher Ed Today, 2021] — [Higher Ed Today](https://www.higheredtoday.org/2021/10/06/exposing-the-gap-addressing-funding-and-endowment-disparities-for-hbcus-and-its-impact/)
- **Individual endowments:** Howard is the only HBCU above $1 billion, while more than 100 predominantly white institutions exceed it (Brookings/HBCU Money say 169); Spelman about $600 million; Morehouse about $340 million; North Carolina A&T $202 million, the largest of any public HBCU (2025). — [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/); [NBC News, Apr 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/nbcblk/many-hbcus-need-government-funding-are-preparing-future-rcna201380); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)
- **Foundation giving (Candid/ABFE, May 2023):** over 2015-2019 the eight Ivy League schools received $5.5 billion from large US foundations vs $303 million for 99 HBCUs; the average Ivy received 178 times the average HBCU (about $110 million/yr vs $618,588/yr). Foundations also gave HBCUs proportionately less general operating support. Some outlets (e.g., CBS) mis-state this as a single-year 2019 figure. Giving jumped to $249 million in 2020. — [Candid press release](https://candid.org/press/philanthropy-and-hbcus-report-reveals-legacy-of-chronic-underfunding-to-historically-black-colleges-and-universities/); [Fortune, May 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/02/ivy-league-hbcu-philanthropy-foundations-donation-178-times-less-funding-study/)
- **Annual donations:** HBCUs average about $2.5 million a year in donations vs about $230 million at the ~100 billion-dollar-endowment institutions and about $4 million at the ~3,300 other non-HBCUs. — [Brookings, Sept 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-actions-on-higher-education-arent-impacting-hbcus-yet/)
- **Private gifts as revenue:** private gifts, grants and contracts are 17% of revenue at private HBCUs vs 25% at private non-HBCUs. — [UNCF, 2022](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Patterson-HBCUs-funding-report_V6.pdf)
- **Mitigating development:** Lilly Endowment's $100 million unrestricted grant to UNCF (2024) anchors a $370 million pooled endowment across 37 member institutions. — [Brookings, Oct 2026](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/hbcu-campus-and-community-development/)

### Inferences
- The per-student ratio depends heavily on year, sector and whether Howard is included (ACE excludes Howard). A defensible range for a brief: public HBCUs hold roughly 20-50% of peer endowment per student; private HBCUs roughly 12-27%. Cite one source and its year rather than blending.
- The $41M average for public HBCUs (2025) is small relative to operating shortfalls in this file (TSU's projected $46M deficit; Bowie State's $18M), i.e. a single bad year can equal or exceed the entire average public HBCU endowment.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain a NACUBO-published HBCU median endowment or a total-HBCU-endowment figure for FY2025; the Brookings relay is the best available.
- Federal Reserve analysis of HBCU endowments (named in the brief) was not located.
- No HBCU-specific figure for endowment spending as a share of operating budget was found.

## 3. Deferred maintenance and capital needs

### Takeaway
GAO's 2018 survey found HBCUs reporting that 46% of their building space needs repair or replacement, and the public-HBCU deferred maintenance backlog has grown from an average $67 million (GAO, 2018) to $81 million (2021) to $96 million (May 2024) per TMCF surveys. Federal relief legislation (IGNITE HBCU Excellence Act) has been reintroduced repeatedly and has never received a vote.

### Cited Findings
- **GAO-18-455 (published June 2018):** GAO surveyed all 101 accredited HBCUs; 79 responded. Respondents reported that 46% of their building space, on average, needs repair or replacement. Fewer than half of HBCUs had ever used the federal HBCU Capital Financing Program; two had defaulted on program loans and 29% of loan payments were delinquent in 2017. Public HBCUs in four states reported state-law barriers to using the program. [primary] — [GAO-18-455](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-18-455)
- **Backlog averages from GAO-18-455:** public HBCUs reported an average deferred maintenance backlog of $67 million and private HBCUs $17 million. [secondary: TMCF cites GAO p. 14 for the $67M; AJC gives both; I did not open the GAO table itself] — [TMCF Infrastructure Needs Fact Sheet, 2024](https://tmcf.org/wp-content/uploads/HBCU-Infrastructure-Needs-Fact-Sheet-2024-Updated-Finals-2.pdf); [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/how-funding-altered-the-hbcu-experience/)
- Inside Higher Ed and a Substack summary cite a $46 million all-HBCU mean from 48 respondents; this appears to be a different cut of the same survey, not a contradiction. [weak, search-summary only] — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/144191)
- 22% of the 35 public HBCUs surveyed by GAO reported that nearly three-quarters of their building space needed repair or replacement; a majority of HBCUs said historic buildings make up more than 10% of their space. — [TMCF fact sheet, 2024](https://tmcf.org/wp-content/uploads/HBCU-Infrastructure-Needs-Fact-Sheet-2024-Updated-Finals-2.pdf)
- **TMCF follow-up surveys of 4-year public HBCUs:** average deferred maintenance $81 million in 2021 (+23% vs GAO) and $96 million in May 2024 (+19% vs 2021, +43% vs GAO 2018). Average need for new infrastructure rose from $120 million (2021) to $236 million (2024). Research-II public HBCUs reported an average $89 million need for R&D infrastructure. Historically Black community colleges reported an average total infrastructure need above $45 million (2024). [primary for TMCF's own survey; advocacy source; sample size not stated] — [TMCF fact sheet, 2024](https://tmcf.org/wp-content/uploads/HBCU-Infrastructure-Needs-Fact-Sheet-2024-Updated-Finals-2.pdf)
- **UNCF member survey (Jan-Feb 2022, 31 private HBCUs):** nearly two-thirds reported more than $5 million in deferred maintenance; facilities ranked at the bottom of priorities for pandemic relief funds. — [UNCF, July 2022](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Patterson-HBCUs-funding-report_V6.pdf)
- **Borrowing costs more:** a 2018/2019 Journal of Financial Economics study found an HBCU would pay underwriters about $35,000 more on a $30 million bond than a comparable non-HBCU, with the effect about three times larger in the Deep South. — [UNCF, 2022 (citing Dougal et al.)](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Patterson-HBCUs-funding-report_V6.pdf)
- **Legislation:** The IGNITE HBCU Excellence Act was first introduced May 2021 and never received a vote; the current version (H.R. 8791, 119th Congress) has nearly 40 House cosponsors and no action since May 2026. A substitute HBCU/TCU/MSI R&D infrastructure program was funded at $50 million in FY23 and FY24 against budget requests of $450 million and $350 million. — [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/); [TMCF fact sheet](https://tmcf.org/wp-content/uploads/HBCU-Infrastructure-Needs-Fact-Sheet-2024-Updated-Finals-2.pdf); [H.R. 8791 text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr8791ih/html/BILLS-119hr8791ih.htm)
- **Capital money diverted to operations:** at Tennessee State, $96 million of a $250 million 2022 state capital appropriation was redirected to operating cash flow in June 2025, leaving $99 million for deferred maintenance. — [Higher Ed Dive, June 2025](https://www.highereddive.com/news/tennessee-state-operations-96m-state-funds-dwayne-tucker-jason-mumpower/751125/)
- Bowie State's finance chief on HBCU practice: repairs are often delayed to meet more pressing financial needs. — [AJC, Aug 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/08/hbcus-say-they-need-funding-boost-to-deliver-full-potential/)

### Inferences
- The backlog is compounding faster than inflation in TMCF's series (+43% in six years), and the TSU case shows the mechanism: when cash runs short, capital money is the first thing converted to payroll.
- TMCF's averages are self-reported by member institutions to an advocacy body; they are the only post-2018 series available and should be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- No GAO update to GAO-18-455 after 2018 was found; GAO's second recommendation (analyzing loan modifications) was still open with a target of end-2025.
- No sector-wide dollar total for HBCU deferred maintenance from a neutral source; no 2025-2026 survey found.

## 4. Revenue dependence, enrollment, and 2025-2026 federal policy shocks

### Takeaway
HBCUs depend on government money and on low-income students' federal aid to a degree other colleges do not: public HBCUs drew 54% of revenue from federal/state/local sources (vs 38% at public non-HBCUs), roughly 7 in 10 students receive Pell, and nearly a quarter of families use Parent PLUS (vs under 10% nationally). 2025-2026 brought a one-time $495 million federal boost alongside research-grant cancellations, a restructured Education Department, and Parent PLUS caps effective July 1, 2026 whose closest historical analogue cut HBCU enrollment about 10% more than elsewhere.

### Cited Findings

**Revenue mix**
- Public HBCUs received 54% of overall revenue from federal, state and local sources vs 38% at public non-HBCUs; private HBCUs received 45% of revenue from tuition and fees vs 37% at private non-HBCUs, and 12% from public sources vs 9% (Howard excluded). Between 2003 and 2015 HBCUs had the steepest declines in federal funding per FTE student of any sector; private HBCUs fell 42%. [primary; data through ~2015, published Jan 2019] — [ACE/UNCF issue brief](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/bitstream/handle/10919/89184/PublicPrivateHbcus.pdf)
- NBC News (Apr 2025) restates this as "54% of Black colleges' total revenue ... compared with just one-third for other colleges," attributed to a 2019 UNCF report. The underlying brief says 54% vs 38% and applies to public HBCUs; use the brief's wording. NBC also reports that 18 HBCUs, including Howard, were among the 30 institutions most reliant on federal funds for operating revenue (2023 federal data). — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/nbcblk/many-hbcus-need-government-funding-are-preparing-future-rcna201380)
- HBCUs spend 73.8 cents of every dollar on Black students (UNCF framing, 2022). — [UNCF, 2022](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Patterson-HBCUs-funding-report_V6.pdf)

**Student aid dependence** (figures differ by definition; do not mix)
- 2019-20 NPSAS: 81.4% of HBCU students received federal Title IV aid vs 54.9% of all students; 67.9% received Pell vs 40.2%; 64.3% took federal student loans vs 34.5%. — [PNPI fact sheet, updated Jan 2026](https://pnpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HBCU_FactSheet_Jan-26.pdf)
- 71% of HBCU students are Pell-eligible vs a 36% national average. — [Brookings, Sept 2025](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-actions-on-higher-education-arent-impacting-hbcus-yet/)
- 73% of undergraduates at UNCF member (private) HBCUs received Pell (UNCF 2025 fact sheet). — [UNCF Fact Sheet 2025](https://uncf.org/wp-content/uploads/UNCF-Fact-Sheet-2025-final.pdf)
- CEA says 75% of HBCU students "rely on" Pell (year unspecified); a PNPI figure cited by AJC gives 56% vs 32% for 2021. The spread (56-75%) reflects different denominators. — [CEA](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/cea/written-materials/2024/05/16/the-economics-of-hbcus); [AJC, Mar 2026](https://www.ajc.com/education/2026/03/tuition-costs-remain-a-hurdle-for-many-hbcu-students)
- About 24% of students at the average HBCU come from the bottom income quintile, more than twice the four-year average. — [CEA](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/cea/written-materials/2024/05/16/the-economics-of-hbcus)
- Six-year (150%) graduation rate at HBCUs was 38% vs 61% at all Title IV institutions (2023). — [PNPI](https://pnpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HBCU_FactSheet_Jan-26.pdf)

**Enrollment**
- 100 HBCUs in 2025 (40 public 4-year, 11 public 2-year, 48 private nonprofit 4-year, 1 private 2-year; 22 land-grant). Fall 2024 enrollment: 267,736 undergraduates and 36,375 graduate students (304,111 total), up 3.7% from 293,257 in 2014; non-HBCU enrollment rose 2.1% over the same decade. HBCUs enrolled 9% of Black students in 2024 (9.4% in 2014). [IPEDS via PNPI] — [PNPI, Jan 2026](https://pnpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HBCU_FactSheet_Jan-26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The combination (majority-public revenue at public HBCUs, 45% tuition dependence at private HBCUs, two-thirds-plus Pell, about a quarter on Parent PLUS) means nearly every HBCU revenue line runs through a federal or state decision. That is the core structural argument for "stability."
- The $495M in FY2025 was explicitly one-time and came with offsetting risk (grant cancellations of at least $140M at HBCUs, Parent PLUS caps, program administration moved to Labor). It should not be presented as evidence that federal support is secure, and equally the administration's increase should not be omitted; both facts are true.
- Fall 2026 headcount announcements are positive at many HBCUs, so there is, as yet, no data showing the Parent PLUS cap reduced HBCU enrollment. Legacy-borrower grandfathering means the cap binds mainly on new families, so effects would phase in over several entering classes. Claims of a 2026 enrollment hit would be premature.

## 5. Financial distress signals: accreditation, monitoring, state rescues, layoffs, closures (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
The clearest case is Saint Augustine's University: probation (Dec 2022), accreditation removal vote (Dec 2024), Chapter 11 (April 2026, $50-100M owed), all fall 2026 classes cancelled, and a bankruptcy judge calling it essentially closed. Around it are a public flagship that needed $43 million in emergency state cash to make payroll (Tennessee State, Nov 2024), a private HBCU in its second year of probation over finances (Johnson C.
