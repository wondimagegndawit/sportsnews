# HBCU / SWAC Athletics Finances (research notes, compiled Nov. 27, 2026)

**How to read the numbers in these notes**

- **EADA** = U.S. Dept. of Education Equity in Athletics Data Analysis survey. Figures below were pulled directly from the official EADA institution-level data files (`instLevel.xlsx`) for survey years 2024-25 (≈ FY2025, file dated April 2026) and 2023-24 (≈ FY2024). EADA "revenue" includes institutional support and student fees and is not broken out by source, so subsidized departments usually report revenue exactly equal to expenses. EADA cannot show a subsidy rate or an operating deficit.
- **NCAA MFRS / "NCAA report"** = the NCAA Membership Financial Reporting System filing that public schools submit; it itemizes student fees, direct institutional support, guarantees, etc. USA Today, Sportico, Knight-Newhouse and the Louisiana Legislative Auditor all work from these filings. Private schools (Bethune-Cookman, Howard, Hampton) do not release them.
- EADA and NCAA-report totals for the same school and year often differ by millions (see Southern, Jackson State, Grambling below). Do not mix them in one comparison.
- **Access limits:** the Knight-Newhouse database (knightnewhousedata.org) returned HTTP 403 to every request, and Sportico's database covers FBS public schools only. The most recent school-by-school subsidy table I could retrieve directly is USA Today's, which stops at FY2022.

## 1. School-by-school finances: revenue, expenses, subsidy rate, deficits and debt (SWAC first, then MEAC)

### Takeaway
Every SWAC athletics department reported between $5.3M and $17.9M in total expenses for 2024-25 (EADA), with a conference median of about $13.0M, and the last full NCAA-report dataset (FY2022) shows roughly two-thirds of SWAC public-school athletics revenue coming from student fees and institutional/state support. State audits and NCAA filings for FY2025 show operating deficits at Grambling ($5.08M), Southern (~$1.5M) and Jackson State ($0.8M), and Florida A&M needed a decade-long repayment plan, a 2026 debt-forgiveness vote and a $250,000 foundation transfer to close its books.

### Inferences
- The SWAC median department (about $13.0M, EADA 2024-25) is roughly half the size of the median FCS department by any definition (EADA FCS median $23.7M; NCAA-report FCS median $25.5M in FY2024). See section 2.
- Where both exist, NCAA-report numbers run higher than EADA for the same school-year (Southern $18.3M vs. $13.1M; Jackson State $16.4M vs. $12.4M). EADA therefore probably understates true SWAC spending, and deficits invisible in EADA show up once revenue is itemized.
- Grambling's audited revenue of $9.2M against EADA "revenue" of $14.8M for the same year suggests the roughly $5M gap was absorbed by the university; the reporting I read does not say whether the audit's revenue figure already includes direct institutional support.
- FAMU's $6.214M is most likely the remaining balance of the athletics debt the university had been repaying since 2016, given that the Board of Governors vote and the trustees' "Athletics Debt Forgiveness" item concern the same matter. No document I retrieved states that directly.
- Jackson State is the clearest "thin margin" example: the FCS attendance leader (section 4) runs a department in the bottom five of FCS by EADA spending, relies on student fees for about 40% of revenue (FY2025 secondary figures), and has posted three straight deficits.

## 2. How SWAC/HBCU budgets compare with FCS, FBS and Power-conference budgets

### Takeaway
A typical SWAC department (about $13M) is about half the FCS median, about one-eighth of the FBS median and about one-thirteenth of the Power-conference median; Ohio State alone reported spending nearly twice what all 12 SWAC schools spent combined. The House revenue-sharing cap for a single school ($20.5M in 2025-26) is larger than the entire athletics budget of any SWAC member.

### Cited Findings
- NCAA medians for FY2024 (NCAA-report definition, published Dec. 2025): **FBS** generated revenue $82.19M, total revenue $106.93M, total expenses $109.99M; **FCS** generated revenue $6.08M, total revenue $25.15M, total expenses $25.48M; **Division I without football** generated revenue $3.95M, total expenses $22.82M. — [NCAA, Division I Athletics Finances: 10-Year Trends 2015-2024](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- FBS **autonomy (Power)** medians FY2024: generated revenue $133.8M, total revenue $149.3M, total expenses $166.8M. FBS **non-autonomy** medians: generated revenue $17.2M, total expenses $48.7M. The autonomy/non-autonomy median expense gap grew from $60M in 2015 to $118M in 2024. — [NCAA report, Dec. 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- FCS expenses ranged from about $5.8M to $77.1M in FY2024. The **bottom expense quartile of FCS** had median total expenses of $15.69M, median generated revenue of $4.21M and a median shortfall of $10.98M. — [NCAA report, Dec. 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- Expenses exceeded generated revenue at all but one FCS school in FY2024; the median FCS shortfall was $16.8M (up from $11.9M in 2015); the median FCS department was 25% self-sufficient; FCS median expenses rose 9.3% from 2023 to 2024 while generated revenue rose 4.4%. — [NCAA report, Dec. 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- FCS revenue mix, FY2024: institution and government support 58%, student fees 12%, donor contributions and endowments 13%, media rights/NCAA and conference distributions/bowl revenue 5%, ticket sales 4%, guarantees 3%, royalties/licensing 2%. Generated revenue was 89% of total revenue at autonomy schools and 30% at FCS schools. — [NCAA report, Dec. 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- EADA medians, 2024-25 (EADA definition; computed from the data file): FBS (128 schools) total expenses $111.8M; FCS (131 schools) $23.7M; Division I without football (99) $22.8M. In 2023-24 the FCS median was $22.5M and the FBS median $99.5M. — [EADA 2024-25](https://ope.ed.gov/athletics/api/dataFiles/file?fileName=EADA_2024-2025.zip); [EADA 2023-24](https://ope.ed.gov/athletics/api/dataFiles/file?fileName=EADA_2023-2024.zip)

- SWAC Commissioner Charles McClelland (Aug. 2024): "We have the same problems. There might just be differences in zeroes at the end of those problems." — [AP via WVTM, Aug. 20, 2024](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/alabama-hbcu-nil-football/61931473)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude ratios, EADA 2024-25 against the SWAC median of $13.04M: FCS median is 1.8x; FBS median is 8.6x; Ohio State is 22.6x. Against NCAA FY2024 medians (different definition, one year earlier): FCS 2.0x, FBS 8.4x, Power/autonomy 12.8x.
- Ohio State's reported 2024-25 expenses ($295.3M) are 1.9 times the combined expenses of all 12 SWAC schools ($153.6M).
- The 2025-26 revenue-sharing cap ($20.5M) is 1.57 times the SWAC median total athletics budget and exceeds the largest SWAC budget (Alabama State, $17.9M, EADA).
- The SWAC median ($13.0M) sits below even the median of the NCAA's bottom FCS expense quartile ($15.69M, FY2024), though the two figures use different definitions.
- HBCU departments are not unusual in running generated-revenue shortfalls (94% of Division I does). What distinguishes them is scale and the smaller institutional budgets absorbing the gap.

### Gaps
- NCAA does not publish medians for HBCUs or by conference; no SWAC-specific NCAA-report median for FY2024 or FY2025 was found.
- No Power-conference median from EADA (EADA has no conference field); the Power median above is NCAA-report data for FY2024.
- EADA's FCS group reflects each school's self-classification that year (James Madison and Delaware still appear as FCS), which slightly inflates the FCS top end.

## 3. House v. NCAA: what SWAC/MEAC schools pay, who opted in, and what leaders say

### Takeaway
The SWAC, which was not a defendant, is being charged about $34M over 10 years through reduced NCAA distributions ($4.14M withheld in the most recent year), and it also paid $1.06M toward a separate volunteer-coach settlement. All 12 SWAC schools opted in to revenue sharing for 2025-26, but leaders openly say they have little or nothing to share: "You can't revenue share something that you don't have."

### Cited Findings

**Back damages: structure**
- Damages total about $2.78B over 10 years; NCAA reserves and insurance cover about $1.1B and the remaining $1.6B "will be withheld from future distributions to DI members." Of that $1.6B, 40% comes from the five defendant conferences and 60% from non-defendant conferences, while "more than 95 percent of the damages will be paid to football and men's and women's basketball athletes who played at institutions in the Defendant Conferences." The plan was an internal NCAA decision that "could be altered." — [Knight Commission brief, Feb. 12, 2025](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KnightCommissionBrief_HousevNCAA_182025.pdf)
- Power conferences contribute about $664M and the 27 non-power conferences about $990M over 10 years; the budget hit was estimated at 1% to 1.68% for non-FBS conferences vs. an average 0.61% for power conferences. Big Sky Commissioner Tom Wistrcill: "It's both ironic and a gut punch… This is clearly unfair." — [Yahoo Sports (Ross Dellenger), May 21, 2024](https://sports.yahoo.com/with-28b-settlement-looming-ncaas-smaller-conferences-feeling-squeezed-what-other-options-are-there-210917099.html)
- Share of total damages by group as reported in 2024-25 coverage: NCAA about 40-41%, Power Five 24%, Group of Five 10%, FCS 14%, non-football Division I 12%. (Taken from search-result summaries of Fisher Phillips and AP pieces, not read in full.) — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/plan-after-final-approval-granted-in-house-v-ncaa-authorizing-pay-for-student-athletes); [AP via AOL](https://www.aol.com/proposed-2-77-billion-settlement-170748141.html)

**Back damages: SWAC and HBCU amounts**

There's going to be some challenging times from a financial standpoint." — [HBCU Sports, June 16, 2025](https://hbcusports.com/2025/06/16/the-swac-is-all-in-on-house-settlement-heres-how-schools-will-be-impacted/)
- Prairie View AD Anton Goff: the SWAC's $30M over 10 years works out to about $2.5M per school, or $250,000 a year, deducted from each school's annual NCAA distributions. — [Defender Network, June 19, 2025](https://defendernetwork.com/sports/college-sports/prairie-view-texas-southern-swac-revenue/)

**Opt-in status (2025-26)**
- The SWAC announced June 16, 2025 that all 12 members would opt in, and that it would place no scholarship caps on any sport it sponsors. — [HBCU Sports, June 16, 2025](https://hbcusports.com/2025/06/16/the-swac-is-all-in-on-house-settlement-heres-how-schools-will-be-impacted/)

## 4. Revenue sources at risk: guarantees, NCAA distributions, classics and bowls, media deals, attendance

### Takeaway
The SWAC's conference-level revenue is at a record ($25.44M, with $18.01M distributed to members, about $1.5M per school), and it draws the largest crowds in FCS, but school budgets still lean on six-figure guarantee checks and NCAA distributions that are now being reduced. HBCU-versus-FBS guarantee games were projected to pay at least $6.9M in 2026, and AP reporting says playoff and scheduling changes could shrink that market.

### Cited Findings

**Conference revenue and distributions**
- Latest SWAC audit (presented Nov. 27, 2026): total revenue $25,444,570, which McClelland says is first among FCS conferences; distributions to the 12 members of $18,010,000 (about 71% of revenue), up from $15,956,000 (62%) reported in Nov. 2025; no audit findings. McClelland: "we distributed more to our institutions than other conferences have as their overall budget." — [HBCU Legends on SI, Nov. 27, 2026](https://www.si.com/college/hbcu/football/state-of-the-swac-2026); [HBCU Sports, Nov. 27, 2026](https://hbcusports.com/2026/11/27/swac-sets-another-conference-revenue-record/)

- Scholarship expansion under House can also cut NCAA money: "As the number of total athletics scholarships grows larger, the overall unit distribution value will be reduced" in the NCAA's $150M-plus Grant-in-Aid Fund. — [Knight Commission brief](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KnightCommissionBrief_HousevNCAA_182025.pdf)

### Inferences
- $18.01M across 12 members averages about $1.5M per school, roughly 11-12% of the SWAC median athletics budget (EADA $13.0M). Conference money is growing but remains a small share next to student fees and institutional support.

## 5. Reported cuts to sports, staff or scholarships at HBCUs for budget reasons, 2023-2026

Scholarships are honored for athletes who stay. — [HBCU Sports, Aug. 4, 2025](https://hbcusports.com/2025/08/04/hbcu-cuts-tennis-program-as-part-of-tough-cost-cutting-measures/)
