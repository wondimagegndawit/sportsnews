# HBCU Sector-Wide Institutional Debt (as of Nov. 27, 2026)

Scope note: every figure below is INSTITUTIONAL debt (owed by the colleges) unless it sits in the section explicitly labeled "student borrower debt." Items marked **[researcher computation]** are my own calculations from NCES IPEDS raw data files, not published statistics; items marked **[snippet-only]** come from a search-engine excerpt of a page I could not open directly (ed.gov program pages, Bloomberg, and The EDU Ledger returned HTTP 403) and should be re-verified before publication.

## 1. What is the total and average/median long-term debt of HBCUs?

### Takeaway
No government agency, rating agency, or major research organization publishes an "average debt of HBCUs" figure; I found none from NCES, GAO, UNCF, TMCF, Urban, Brookings, TCF, Moody's, S&P, or Fitch. The best available building block is IPEDS: HBCUs reported about $3.65 billion in long-term debt in fiscal year 2024 across 102 institutions (mean about $36 million, median about $8.5 million; median about $27 million among the 71 that reported any debt), with Howard University alone holding 23% of the total.

### Cited Findings

**Published research (no average exists)**
- The only published sector-level analysis of HBCU institutional debt I found is Ithaka S+R's "Borrowing During a Time of Crisis" (Ward, Haynes, Gusakova; Aug. 31, 2022; IPEDS-based). It reports charts, not a headline average: "While HBCUs do not appear to deviate from their non-HBCU peers significantly in terms of debt per FTE outcomes, there is a large gap in total debt between the two groups that grows over time and a marked increase in the leverage ratio at HBCUs following 2009." — [Ithaka S+R full report PDF](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Ithaka-SR_-BorrowingDuringCrisis_Aug312022.pdf)
- Same report: HBCUs were "roughly seven to 11 percentage points more likely to rapidly increase debt than their non-HBCU counterparts" during the Great Recession (a "rapid increase" = 50%+ growth in total long-term debt, 2009–2011), controlling for other characteristics. — [Ithaka S+R](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Ithaka-SR_-BorrowingDuringCrisis_Aug312022.pdf)
- Same report: high-borrowing HBCUs cut the share of spending going to education-related expenses by "roughly six percentage points" relative to non-high-borrowing institutions, which the authors read as evidence that HBCUs "may have been forced to borrow during the Great Recession to maintain operations." The authors caution these are not causal estimates. — [Ithaka S+R](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Ithaka-SR_-BorrowingDuringCrisis_Aug312022.pdf)
- A Winter 2022 AAUP *Academe* article on HBCU institutional debt gives no sector totals or averages; it points readers to IPEDS and tax filings to build debt profiles institution by institution. — [AAUP, "Battling Institutional Debt at HBCUs"](https://www.aaup.org/article/battling-institutional-debt-hbcus)
- For scale, an EY analysis put all U.S. higher-education interest-bearing debt at "nearly $294 billion" and industry-average debt per FTE student at $21,000 (not HBCU-specific; date of analysis not captured — snippet-only). — [EY, "The other looming educational debt crisis"](https://assets.ey.com/content/dam/ey-sites/ey-com/en_gl/topics/strategy/pdf/ey-the-other-looming-educational-debt-crisis-institutional-debt.pdf?download)

**IPEDS building blocks, fiscal year 2024 [researcher computation]**
Method: NCES IPEDS Finance survey files for FY2023-24 (files dated Dec. 30, 2025; I did not verify whether this is the provisional or final release), joined to the HD2024 directory file's HBCU flag. Debt definition differs by accounting standard: public (GASB) institutions = "Long-term debt, current portion" (F1A07) + "Long-term debt" (F1A10); private nonprofit (FASB) institutions = "Debt related to property, plant, and equipment" (F2A03A), which IPEDS defines as all long-term debt obligations including bonds, mortgages, capital leases and long-term notes. FTE = 12-month FTE from EFIA2024. Sources: [F2324_F1A (public)](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip), [F2324_F2 (private nonprofit)](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip), [HD2024](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/HD2024.zip), [EFIA2024](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/EFIA2024.zip).
- IPEDS flags 102 HBCUs in 2024: 51 public and 51 private nonprofit; 92 four-year and 10 two-year. All 102 have a FY2024 finance record. — [NCES IPEDS HD2024](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/HD2024.zip)
- Total long-term debt reported by all 102 HBCUs, FY2024: $3.650 billion ($2.258 billion at 50 GASB-reporting institutions; $1.391 billion at 52 FASB-reporting institutions). — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)
- Mean debt per HBCU (total ÷ 102): $35.8 million. Median: $8.5 million. 31 of 102 report zero debt; among the 71 reporting any debt, mean is $51.4 million and median is $27.3 million. — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip)
- Concentration: Howard University reports $838.6 million, 23.0% of all HBCU debt. The top five hold 41.9% and the top ten hold 54.9%. Excluding Howard: total $2.811 billion, mean $27.8 million, median $7.5 million. — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip)
- Four-year public HBCUs (n=42): total $2.184 billion, mean $52.0 million, median $38.4 million; 37 of 42 report debt. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F1A](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)
- Four-year private nonprofit HBCUs (n=50): total $1.364 billion, mean $27.3 million, median $0.2 million; only 27 of 50 report any debt, and their median is $10.2 million. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip)
- Largest reported debt, FY2024: Howard $838.6M; Southern University and A&M $228.8M; St. Philip's College (TX; a community college in the Alamo Colleges District, so this is likely a district allocation) $168.4M; North Carolina A&T $155.2M; Texas Southern $137.9M; North Carolina Central $100.9M; Kentucky State $97.5M; Bethune-Cookman $92.6M; Delaware State $92.0M; Tennessee State $90.5M. — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)
- Other sector balance-sheet totals, all 102 HBCUs, FY2024: total assets $26.48 billion; total liabilities (a broader measure that includes pensions, payables, etc.) $9.81 billion; endowment assets $5.53 billion; interest expense $132.7 million; 257,949 FTE students; aggregate debt per FTE $14,149. — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)
- Pre-forgiveness comparison, FY2020 (revised file; 100 HBCUs matched): total long-term debt $4.236 billion, mean $42.4 million, median $21.9 million, 86 reporting any debt; Howard $431.8 million. Interest expense $154.6 million. — [NCES IPEDS F1920_F1A](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/datacenter/data/F1920_F1A.zip); [F1920_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/datacenter/data/F1920_F2.zip)

**Caveats that must travel with the IPEDS numbers [researcher computation]**
- The IPEDS debt field under-reports for some private HBCUs: seven report $0 debt while reporting more than $250,000 of interest expense in FY2024 — Hampton ($4.23M interest; $185.6M total liabilities), Paine ($1.39M interest), Saint Augustine's ($1.14M interest; $45.4M total liabilities), Tougaloo, Bennett, Le Moyne-Owen, Dillard. Substituting IPEDS's separate "plant-related debt" field where it is larger raises the sector total only to $3.865 billion. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip)
- Several public HBCUs show little or no debt because bonds are carried by the state or university system rather than on the campus's books (e.g., Florida A&M reports $2.4 million, Grambling State $0, Prairie View A&M $10.8 million). Southern University and A&M reports debt equal to 92% of its assets, which suggests system-level debt is booked there. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F1A](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)

### Inferences
- A responsible phrasing is: "HBCUs reported roughly $3.7 billion in long-term institutional debt in fiscal 2024 — about $36 million per institution on average, though the typical (median) HBCU reports under $10 million and one university, Howard, accounts for nearly a quarter of the total (author's analysis of NCES IPEDS data)." The mean is not a "typical" figure and should not be presented as one.
- The true figure is somewhat higher than $3.65 billion because of the reporting gaps above; "at least $3.7 billion" is defensible, and $9.8 billion is the ceiling if one uses total liabilities (which is not the same thing as debt).
- Reported HBCU debt fell about $0.6 billion (14%) from FY2020 to FY2024 despite $1.6 billion of federal forgiveness, because Howard's debt roughly doubled (+$407 million) and other schools borrowed anew. Excluding Howard, HBCU debt fell from about $3.80 billion to $2.81 billion (−26%).
- The collapse of the private-HBCU median (from $17.2 million in FY2020 to $0.2 million in FY2024) is consistent with the 2021 forgiveness wiping out the only long-term debt many small private HBCUs had.

### Gaps
- No published average or median HBCU institutional debt figure exists from any authoritative source that I could find; the numbers above are original calculations and should be labeled as such.
- I could not read the Diverse/EDU Ledger article "Report Reveals a Differing Picture of Debt at HBCUs" (403); it appears to be coverage of the Ithaka report.
- Ithaka's Figures 3 and 4 (total debt, debt per FTE, leverage by HBCU status) are charts; the underlying values are not printed in the text.
- IPEDS FY2025 finance data were not available at the NCES URLs I tried.
- IPEDS does not capture off-balance-sheet debt (foundation-held housing bonds, public-private partnership dorm deals, state-issued bonds), which is material for public HBCUs.

## 2. HBCU Capital Financing Program (U.S. Department of Education)

### Takeaway
The Department discharged about $1.6 billion owed by 45 HBCUs (13 public, 32 private) in 2021 — about $36 million per institution on a simple average — and lending has since rebuilt. Education's FY2027 budget justification shows $830.3 million outstanding on 25 loans in 2025, while the Department's own web page cites about $1.72–$1.74 billion; the two figures conflict and I could not reconcile them.

### Cited Findings

**The 2020–21 forgiveness**
- The Department announced in April 2021 that it had discharged "over $1.6 billion" of HBCU Capital Financing debt for 45 HBCUs — 13 public and 32 private nonprofit — under authority in the Coronavirus Response and Relief Supplemental Appropriations Act (signed December 2020). — [U.S. Department of Education press release](https://www.ed.gov/news/press-releases/department-education-discharges-over-16-billion-hbcu-capital-finance-debt) (snippet-only; page returned 403); corroborated by [Ithaka S+R](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Ithaka-SR_-BorrowingDuringCrisis_Aug312022.pdf) and [AAUP](https://www.aaup.org/article/battling-institutional-debt-hbcus)
- The forgiveness covered program loans "outstanding as of December 27, 2020." — [GAO-18-455 recommendation status notes](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-18-455)
- Early reporting (January 2021) put the forgiveness at $1.3 billion; the Department's April 2021 figure was $1.6 billion. — [Inside Higher Ed, Jan. 19, 2021](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2021/01/19/hbcus-receive-boost-capital-loan-forgiveness) (snippet-only)
- One institution's example: Morehouse College had about $56 million of capital debt cancelled, eliminating about $5 million a year in debt service, against an annual budget of about $100 million and a recurring deficit of about $5 million. — [AAUP *Academe*, Winter 2022](https://www.aaup.org/article/battling-institutional-debt-hbcus)
- Moody's noted capital-finance debt relief was 14% of the $11.4 billion in federal funds awarded directly to HBCUs in fiscal 2021–2024. — [Moody's Ratings, Oct. 2, 2024](https://ma.moodys.com/rs/961-KCJ-308/images/Sector_In-Depth-Higher-Education-US-Favorable.pdf?version=0)

**Program size before forgiveness (GAO, 2018)**
- As of November 2017, Education had lent "over $2 billion in total with $1.8 billion outstanding." — [GAO-18-455, p. 8](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- 46 of the 99 eligible HBCUs had used the program; as of February 2018 private HBCUs were 33 of 46 participants and accounted for about $1.39 billion of funding since 1996. — [GAO-18-455, pp. 26–27](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- The statute caps total outstanding loans at $1.1 billion, but Congress has passed appropriations language every year since fiscal 2012 letting Education lend above that amount. — [GAO-18-455, pp. 7–8](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- Borrowers must place 5% of loan proceeds in a pooled escrow account that covers any participant's delinquency or default. — [GAO-18-455](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- Distress before forgiveness: 29% of loan payments were delinquent in 2017; two HBCUs defaulted in the prior two years (plus one in 2004); as of April 2018 four colleges were delinquent and three remained in default, all seven private; escrow payouts for defaults totaled $15.1 million as of December 2017. — [GAO-18-455](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- Congress added $10 million in fiscal 2018 "for outstanding loan deferments." — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 6](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)

**Current portfolio and new borrowing**
- Education's FY2027 budget justification, 2025 column: 25 total loans (16 private, 9 public); total loan awards $1.6305 billion; "total outstanding loan awards" $830.3 million ($489.9 million private, $340.4 million public). — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, pp. 9–10](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- Same document, new lending in 2025: three loans totaling $187 million (one private for $96 million; two public for $91 million). — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 9](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- Those three 2025 loans match the Department's loan list: Xavier University of Louisiana $96 million (June 2025), Lincoln University (PA) $56 million (March 2025), Morgan State $35 million (July 2025). — [ED program loans page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/historically-black-colleges-and-universities-hbcus-and-predominantly-black-institutions-pbis/historically-black-college-and) [snippet-only]
- Same document, 2027 projection: 35 loans, $2.3565 billion in total awards, $1.4387 billion outstanding ($739.6 million private, $699.0 million public), with four new loans totaling $302 million. — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, pp. 9–10](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- FY2027 request: $20.15 million in loan subsidy to support up to $389.3 million of new loans, "an increase of nearly $45 million over 2025." — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, pp. 1–2](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- Education's program web pages give different, larger figures, and three versions surfaced in search: an outstanding balance of "about $1.72 billion ($739 million attributed to public HBCUs and $983 million attributed to private HBCUs)"; "about $1.74 billion ($808 million public, $935 million private)"; and "about $1.70 billion ($739 million public, $958 million private)." — [ED program page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program); [ED loans page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/historically-black-colleges-and-universities-hbcus-and-predominantly-black-institutions-pbis/historically-black-college-and) [snippet-only; pages undated]; conflicts with the $830.3 million in the [FY2027 Congressional Justification](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- The loans page says "35 loans outstanding to 20 colleges and universities with loan amounts ranging from $6 million to $226.5 million," and that the program has originated 141 loans to 52 HBCUs (16 public, 36 private) since inception. — [ED loans page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/historically-black-colleges-and-universities-hbcus-and-predominantly-black-institutions-pbis/historically-black-college-and) [snippet-only]
- Other post-forgiveness loans: Benedict College $10 million (November 2023, energy upgrades, 30-year fixed) — [ED loans page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/historically-black-colleges-and-universities-hbcus-and-predominantly-black-institutions-pbis/historically-black-college-and) [snippet-only]; Edward Waters University $25 million (closed Sept. 10, 2026, part of a $33 million campus plan including its first new residence hall since 1982) — [News4Jax, Oct. 8, 2026](https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/10/08/edward-waters-announces-33m-campus-investment-including-first-new-residence-hall-since-1982/). JBHE describes the Edward Waters money as a grant; Jacksonville Today and the program structure indicate it is a loan. — [JBHE](https://jbhe.com/2026/10/edward-waters-university-announces-33-million-investment-in-campus-infrastructure/)

**Repayment risk today**
- Education's FY2027 justification: the program had no delinquencies or defaults in its first 10 years, but "increases in financial instability at certain HBCUs have affected Federal liability in the program, which are reflected in delinquency rate fluctuations and Department estimates." — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 8](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- Upward reestimates of existing loan subsidy costs: $4.955 million in 2025 and $24.747 million in 2026. — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 4](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- Share of program borrowers whose revenue rose year over year: 13% in 2022, 74% in 2023, 68% in 2024 (target 70%). — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 10](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)
- The justification says "in most cases these institutions cannot access traditional funding sources at reasonable interest rates" and calls borrowers "some of the Nation's most vulnerable institutions of higher education." — [ED FY2027 Congressional Justification, p. 7](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/fy-2027-congressional-justification-historically-black-college-and-university-capital-financing-program-account-113548.pdf)

### Inferences
- $1.6 billion ÷ 45 institutions = about $35.6 million forgiven per institution on a simple average. This is my arithmetic, not a published figure; actual amounts varied widely (Morehouse's was about $56 million).
- Using the budget justification, the 2025 average outstanding balance is about $33 million per loan ($830.3 million ÷ 25 loans). Using the web page, it is about $50 million per loan or $87 million per borrowing institution ($1.74 billion ÷ 35 loans or ÷ 20 institutions). The brief should pick one source and name it.
- The likeliest explanation for the conflict is that the web page reports loan commitments/face amounts (its total is close to the justification's $1.63 billion "total loan awards") while the justification's "outstanding" figure reflects drawn, unpaid principal. This is inference; neither source says so.
- Post-forgiveness lending has been brisk: the portfolio went from effectively zero in early 2021 to $830 million–$1.7 billion by 2025, and Education projects $1.44 billion outstanding by 2027.
- The $24.7 million upward reestimate in 2026 suggests Education's expected losses on existing loans rose; the document does not explain the cause.

### Gaps
- I found no current count of delinquent, deferred, or defaulted loans since the 2021 forgiveness. The FY2027 justification gives no delinquency rate.
- I could not open the ed.gov program pages or the FY2025/FY2026 budget justifications (403), so the per-loan list for 2021–2026 is incomplete and the web-page balances are unverified and undated.
- The Department's April 2021 release as surfaced did not include a per-institution list of forgiven amounts.
- The number of distinct borrowing institutions in 2025 is not stated in the budget justification (only the number of loans).

## 3. How does HBCU debt compare with non-HBCU peers (leverage, cost of borrowing, ratings)?

### Takeaway
On reported balance sheets HBCUs carry less debt than non-HBCUs, not more; the fragility argument rests on thin endowments and assets behind that debt, costlier and narrower access to credit, and weak or absent credit ratings. Public HBCUs are the exception on leverage: their median debt is larger than their endowment (1.17x versus 0.77x at non-HBCU publics) and their liabilities equal half their assets.

**Thin cushions behind the debt**
- GAO (IPEDS, 2015-16): median HBCU endowment was $12 million versus $23 million at matched non-HBCUs; HBCUs drew a median 70% of revenue from government sources versus 63%. — [GAO-18-455, Table 3](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- GAO survey (2017): responding HBCUs said 46% of their building space, on average, needed repair or replacement; deferred maintenance backlogs averaged $46 million (median $19 million; range $450,000 to $269 million) across 48 reporting HBCUs — $67 million average at public HBCUs and $17 million at private HBCUs. — [GAO-18-455, p. 18](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- TMCF (Nov. 7, 2025): average public HBCU endowment is $41 million versus $133 million at private HBCUs. — [TMCF, "The American Dividend"](https://tmcf.org/landmark-report-public-hbcus-power-u-s-competitiveness-and-economic-mobility-yet-face-severe-funding-disparities/)
- Hunt Institute (January 2026): non-HBCU land-grant institutions hold more than $45 billion in endowment assets versus about $1 billion at HBCU land-grants. — [Hunt Institute issue brief](https://hunt-institute.org/resources/2026/01/issue-brief-the-underfunding-of-historically-black-colleges-and-universities/) [snippet-only]
- Federal officials estimated in September 2023 that 16 states underfunded their land-grant HBCUs by more than $12 billion from 1987 to 2020. — [NPR via WFAE, Oct. 9, 2023](https://www.wfae.org/united-states-world/2023-10-09/hbcus-have-been-underfunded-by-12-billion-federal-officials-reveal)

**Cost of borrowing**
- "Bond penalty" study (Dougal, Gao, Mayew, Parsons, *Journal of Financial Economics*, 2019): in 4,145 tax-exempt higher-education bond issues totaling $150 billion from 1988 to 2010 (102 by HBCUs), HBCUs paid underwriting fees averaging 92 cents per $100 raised versus 81 cents for non-HBCUs — about 14% more. In Alabama, Mississippi and Louisiana the HBCU cost averaged $106,000 on a $10 million issue. HBCU bonds also cost more to trade and sat longer in dealer inventory. — [JBHE summary, Oct. 14, 2019](https://jbhe.com/2019/10/study-finds-that-hbcus-pay-a-premium-to-underwrite-tax-exempt-bonds/); [paper (NBER conference draft)](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f82453/f82453.pdf)
- The underwriting premium is a one-time issuance cost (11 basis points of principal), not an interest-rate gap; the data end in 2010. — [JBHE summary](https://jbhe.com/2019/10/study-finds-that-hbcus-pay-a-premium-to-underwrite-tax-exempt-bonds/)
- GAO: program loans used for refinancing in 2012–2016 had a median true interest cost of 3.15%, compared with 1.49% for AAA-rated, 2.60% for AA-rated and 3.29% for A-rated higher-education refinancing bonds in the same states — i.e., the federal program gets HBCUs roughly A-rated pricing. Refinancing through the program saved a median 14% of loan cost. — [GAO-18-455, pp. 28–29](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)
- Extreme example of market-rate borrowing by a distressed HBCU: Saint Augustine's University took a $7 million loan at a 24% rate from Gothic Ventures. — [The Bond Buyer, Dec. 12, 2024](https://www.bondbuyer.com/articles/struggling-hbcu-st-augustines-takes-a-high-rate-loan)

**Credit ratings**
- Moody's rated eight HBCUs with enrollment data as of October 2024, out of roughly 100. Named ratings then: Spelman A1 stable; Morgan State A1 stable; Elizabeth City State Baa1 stable; Howard Ba1 positive; Clark Atlanta Ba1 stable (Ba1 is one notch below investment grade). — [Moody's Ratings, Oct. 2, 2024](https://ma.moodys.com/rs/961-KCJ-308/images/Sector_In-Depth-Higher-Education-US-Favorable.pdf?version=0)
- Moody's called HBCU enrollment gains "credit positive" in that report but warned that "sustained credit improvement" depends on retention, governance, and the ability to "refresh aging infrastructure." — [Moody's Ratings, Oct. 2, 2024](https://ma.moodys.com/rs/961-KCJ-308/images/Sector_In-Depth-Higher-Education-US-Favorable.pdf?version=0)
- Howard University: Fitch revised its outlook to negative in the week of Feb. 4, 2026, citing financial pressure after fall 2025 enrollment fell 5%. — [Bloomberg, Feb. 4, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-04/howard-university-s-sharp-growth-reverses-in-5-enrollment-drop) [snippet-only; rating level not confirmed]
- Morgan State says S&P affirmed its A+ (stable) and Moody's its A1 (stable). — [Morgan State University release](https://www.morgan.edu/news/sandp-and-moodys-affirm-strong-credit-ratings) (institution's own release; date not confirmed)
- Sector backdrop: Moody's kept a negative outlook on U.S. higher education for 2026. — [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/moodys-negative-outlook-higher-ed-2026/806097/)
- GAO reported that bonding-authority officials said HBCUs "may lack high credit ratings," which is why the federal program exists. — [GAO-18-455, p. 29](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-18-455.pdf)

### Inferences
- The accurate advocacy framing is "under-capitalized," not "over-indebted": HBCUs owe less than peers in absolute and per-asset terms, but public HBCUs' debt exceeds their endowments and their total liabilities are half of assets, and HBCUs of both types have far smaller reserves to absorb a shock. Claiming HBCUs carry heavier debt loads than peers would be contradicted by IPEDS and by Ithaka.
- Low debt at private HBCUs partly reflects the 2021 forgiveness and partly constrained access to credit rather than financial strength; Ithaka and GAO both describe access barriers. This is an interpretation, not a measured result.
- The lower interest-to-expense share at HBCUs (0.8–0.9% vs. 1.0–2.0% at peers) likewise reflects less debt, and for program borrowers, federally subsidized rates.
- Fewer than one in ten HBCUs carries a public Moody's rating, so rating actions describe only the strongest, largest schools; the most fragile HBCUs borrow unrated, from the federal program, banks, CDFIs, or private lenders.

### Gaps
- I found no complete, current list of S&P- or Fitch-rated HBCUs, and no agency count of HBCU downgrades for 2024–2026. Searches for 2025–2026 rating actions on Clark Atlanta, Morehouse, Dillard, Tuskegee, Bethune-Cookman and Hampton returned nothing.
- Howard's current rating levels at all three agencies are unconfirmed for 2026. Older search results describing Moody's cutting Howard from A3 to Baa1 and then Baa3 refer to 2013–2014 actions and must not be cited as recent.
- No study updates the bond-penalty finding with post-2010 issuance data.
- GAO's Table 3 prints median endowment per student as $15,000 for HBCUs versus $410,000 for matched non-HBCUs; the non-HBCU value looks implausible against the total-endowment medians, so I recommend not using the per-student comparison.
- Debt service coverage ratios (a standard rating metric) cannot be computed from IPEDS; principal payments are not reported.

## 4. Reporting on specific HBCU debt and financial crises, 2024–2026

### Takeaway
The clearest debt-driven failure is Saint Augustine's University, which filed Chapter 11 in April 2026 listing $50–$100 million in liabilities, including $14.4 million owed to the IRS, after borrowing at 24%. Talladega College and Tennessee State show the more common pattern: operating deficits and missed payroll rather than large bonded debt.

### Cited Findings
- **Saint Augustine's University (NC, private):** filed voluntary Chapter 11; court documents obtained April 28, 2026 list estimated debts of $50–$100 million, 200–999 creditors, and $14.4 million owed to the IRS. Accreditation was set to end May 15, 2026, with spring 2026 graduates the last to receive accredited degrees. — [ABC11, Apr. 29, 2026](https://abc11.com/post/saint-augustines-university-files-chapter-11-bankruptcy/18986410/)
- Saint Augustine's borrowed $7 million from Gothic Ventures at a 24% rate in late 2024, while missing two years of audited financial statements. — [The Bond Buyer, Dec. 12, 2024](https://www.bondbuyer.com/articles/struggling-hbcu-st-augustines-takes-a-high-rate-loan)
- Its primary lender is Self-Help Ventures Fund, which offered to assume some debt conditional on two board members leaving. — [JBHE, January 2026](https://jbhe.com/2026/01/lender-agrees-to-assume-st-augustines-universitys-debt-if-certain-board-members-are-let-go/) (specialty outlet; headline-level only)
- IPEDS FY2024 for Saint Augustine's: $45.4 million total liabilities against $81.9 million in assets; $1.14 million interest expense; long-term-debt field reported as $0. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip) [researcher extraction]
- **Talladega College (AL, private):** failed to make payroll in spring 2024; president and CFO resigned; secured a $15 million working-capital loan from Hope Credit Union in April 2025 to refinance debt and pay vendors; cut four sports; fall headcount fell nearly 31% from 2018 to 2023, to 837. — [Higher Ed Dive, Apr. 10, 2025](https://www.highereddive.com/news/talladega-college-15M-loan-cut-sports-distress/745088/)
- IPEDS FY2024 for Talladega: $30.5 million long-term debt against $115.5 million in assets and a $2.6 million endowment. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip) [researcher extraction]
- **Tennessee State University (public):** administrators projected a $46 million deficit by June 30, 2025 and needed state help to make November 2024 payroll. — [HBCU News, Dec. 20, 2024](https://hbcunews.com/2024/12/20/tennessee-state-university-requests-financial-intervention-to-avoid-46-million-deficit/) (weaker source; snippet-only). By December 2025 the university reported cutting the deficit by $13.2 million; the state reportedly still owes TSU $294 million. — [JBHE, January 2026](https://jbhe.com/2026/01/a-major-financial-turnaround-at-tennessee-state-university/) [snippet-only]
- Federal officials said in 2023 that Tennessee underfunded TSU by about $2.1 billion (1987–2020). — [NPR via WFAE, Oct. 9, 2023](https://www.wfae.org/united-states-world/2023-10-09/hbcus-have-been-underfunded-by-12-billion-federal-officials-reveal) [snippet-only for the TSU figure]
- IPEDS FY2024 for Tennessee State: $90.5 million long-term debt; $359.6 million assets. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F1A](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip) [researcher extraction]
- **Grambling State (LA, public):** the Louisiana Legislative Auditor found a fiscal 2025 athletics deficit of about $5.1 million ($14.3 million in expenses against $9.2 million in revenue). — [KSLA, May 11, 2026](https://www.ksla.com/2026/05/11/deficit-more-than-5-million-discovered-audit-grambling-athletics-dept/)
- Grambling took a $100 million HBCU Capital Financing loan in November 2016, which would have been covered by the 2021 forgiveness. — [ED loans page](https://www.ed.gov/grants-and-programs/grants-higher-education/historically-black-colleges-and-universities-hbcus-and-predominantly-black-institutions-pbis/historically-black-college-and) [snippet-only; forgiveness coverage is inferred from the cutoff date]
- **Jackson State (MS, public):** eight positions eliminated at year-end 2026 as federal funding lapsed; the university expects to lose "millions" from the Department of Education. No debt figure reported. — [Mississippi Today, Oct. 30, 2026](https://mississippitoday.org/2026/10/30/jackson-state-layoffs-federal-funding/) [snippet-only]
- **Howard University (DC, private):** largest HBCU debtor by far at $838.6 million in FY2024, up from $431.8 million in FY2020, with $34.0 million in annual interest expense. — [NCES IPEDS F2324_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F2.zip); [F1920_F2](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/datacenter/data/F1920_F2.zip) [researcher extraction]
- **IPEDS FY2024 long-term debt for other schools named in the assignment [researcher extraction]:** Texas Southern $137.9M (assets $718.3M; interest $11.4M); Jackson State $85.4M (assets $420.3M); Fisk $45.2M (assets $232.4M; endowment $41.3M); Florida A&M $2.4M on campus books (assets $860.3M); Bennett College $0 reported debt but $8.0M total liabilities and $0.45M interest expense on 183 FTE students. — [NCES IPEDS F2324 finance files](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/complete-data-files/F2324_F1A.zip)

### Inferences
- Recent HBCU crises have been triggered by enrollment-driven operating deficits, unpaid payroll taxes, and vendor arrears more than by bonded debt; the debt that did the damage was small, short-term, and expensive (a $7 million loan at 24%, IRS liabilities).
- Small private HBCUs with endowments under about $15 million (Saint Augustine's $13.6M, Talladega $2.6M, Bennett $16.5M per IPEDS FY2024) have little capacity to absorb an enrollment shock, which is the fragility point the brief can make with documented cases.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 reporting with institutional debt dollar figures for Bennett College, Fisk University, Texas Southern, or Florida A&M; search results for Bennett and Fisk were from 2016–2019.
- The Saint Augustine's full schedule of creditors and the final terms of the Self-Help financing (WRAL reportedly cited up to $30 million) were not confirmed from a primary source.
- Tennessee State's $46 million deficit figure comes from a weaker outlet and should be re-sourced to the Tennessean, WPLN, or state comptroller documents before use.
- The EDU Ledger feature "The Debt Still Standing" (on state obligations to HBCUs) returned 403 and could not be read.

## 5. Separately labeled: HBCU STUDENT borrower debt (not institutional debt)

### Takeaway
Student-borrower figures are a different measure and should not be combined with any number above. The available headline figures are older (2012–2022 data) and I could verify them only at the snippet level.

### Cited Findings
- STUDENT DEBT: A 2016 UNCF Frederick D. Patterson Research Institute report found that, in 2012, a quarter of HBCU bachelor's recipients borrowed $40,000 or more — four times the rate of non-HBCU peers — and put average debt at $26,266 for HBCU graduates versus $14,881 at other institutions. — [UNCF report hosted by ED](https://safesupportivelearning.ed.gov/sites/default/files/FINAL_HBCU_Loan_Debt_Burden_Report.pdf); [JBHE, Dec. 2016](https://jbhe.com/2016/12/student-loan-debt-is-a-major-problem-for-large-numbers-of-hbcu-students) [snippet-only]
- STUDENT DEBT: A 2022 Center for Responsible Lending study found HBCU graduates' debt load was 19% higher than non-HBCU peers', with average debt at graduation of $32,373. — [CRL, "Paying from the Grave," Nov. 2022](https://www.responsiblelending.org/sites/default/files/nodes/files/research-publication/crl-hbcu-paying-from-grave-nov2022.pdf) [snippet-only; I did not open the PDF]
- STUDENT DEBT: Bankrate reported that 56 of the 94 HBCUs tracked by the Department of Education had student-loan nonpayment rates of 30% or higher. — [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/student-loans/more-than-half-of-hbcus-risk-losing-federal-financial-aid-access) [snippet-only; date not captured]

### Inferences
- High student nonpayment rates are an institutional risk too, because they can threaten a college's access to federal aid, on which HBCUs rely more heavily than peers (GAO: 70% of revenue from government sources at the median).

### Gaps
- A search summary attributed a "$40 billion" total owed by HBCU attendees and alumni to the Center for Responsible Lending, but I could not trace it to a document; do not use it without verification.
- I found no 2024–2026 authoritative total for outstanding federal student debt attributable to HBCU attendees.
