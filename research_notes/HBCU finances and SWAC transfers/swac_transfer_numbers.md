# SWAC transfer numbers: how many athletes left (and joined) SWAC programs, 2024-25 into 2025-26 and 2025-26 into 2026-27

Research date: November 27, 2026. Scope: the 12 SWAC members (Alabama A&M, Alabama State, Alcorn State, Arkansas-Pine Bluff, Bethune-Cookman, Florida A&M, Grambling State, Jackson State, Mississippi Valley State, Prairie View A&M, Southern, Texas Southern), with national NCAA figures for context.

How to read these notes:

- **[PUBLISHED]** = a count someone else published. **[OWN TALLY]** = my own count from a named tracker or dataset, with the method and per-school numbers given so it can be checked. **[SNIPPET ONLY]** = I saw the figure in a search-result summary but could not open the page itself; verify before printing.
- "Entered the portal" and "actually transferred" are different things. No source I could open gives a complete count of SWAC portal entrants by school in any sport. What exists is (a) lists of players who landed at a new Division I school and (b) year-over-year roster comparisons. Both are labelled.
- No source used here separates scholarship players from walk-ons for SWAC schools, and none separates graduate transfers from undergraduates. Treat every SWAC count as "all players, any aid status, graduate transfers included" unless stated.
- Cycle labels: "2025 cycle" = players leaving after the 2024 football season or the 2024-25 basketball season (football windows December 2024 and April 2025). "2026 cycle" = leaving after the 2025 football season (single window January 2-16, 2026) or the 2025-26 basketball season (window April 7-21, 2026).
- Player-level detail for the basketball tallies is in the companion file `swac_transfer_numbers_appendix_basketball_roster_tally.md` in this folder.

## Football: how many players left each SWAC school in the 2025 and 2026 portal cycles, how many moved up to FBS / Power 4, and how many transfers each roster took in?

### Takeaway
No complete count of SWAC football portal entrants exists in any source I could open; the best available numbers are lists of players who landed somewhere. Those lists show at least 33 SWAC players moving up to FBS in the 2025 cycle and at least 39 in the 2026 cycle (9 to Power 4 schools each year), plus at least 40 lateral moves to other FCS schools in 2026, while SWAC rosters were rebuilt with 19 to 60+ incoming transfers per school.

### Inferences
- The floor for SWAC players moving up to FBS rose from 33 (2025 cycle, HERO Sports list) to 39 (2026 cycle, my union of two lists), with 9 Power 4 destinations each year. The two years were compiled by different sources with different methods, so the increase is suggestive, not proven. Using the SI tracker alone, 2026 would read as 24, a decrease; the difference shows how incomplete any single tracker is.
- SWAC schools account for 24 of about 329 players (roughly 7%) on SI's national 2026 FCS-to-FBS list.
- Roughly half of the SWAC players who landed at a Division I school in 2026 moved up to FBS, a third moved sideways out of the conference, and about one in six moved to a conference rival. Moves down (Division II, NAIA, junior college) and players left without a school are invisible in these lists.
- The loss of honored players is concentrated: Jackson State, Bethune-Cookman, Alabama State and Prairie View A&M supplied all 6 first-team All-SWAC players who left for FBS in 2026.
- SWAC programs are net importers as well as exporters. Prairie View's 70+ newcomers, Alcorn's 19 transfers in a class of 28 and Grambling's 21 of 30 show rosters being rebuilt through the portal each year, which matters for the one-year-turnover argument below.

I found no HBCU Gameday, HBCU Sports, Andscape or SWAC-published conference-wide entrant count for football.
- No 247Sports page was found for Alabama A&M, Florida A&M, Mississippi Valley State or Prairie View A&M (several URL forms returned 404).
- SI's 2026 FBS-to-FCS tracker and all three of its 2025 trackers returned HTTP 410, and HERO Sports' 2025 lists now redirect to a sportsbook blog, so I could not tally 2025 lateral moves or 2025-26 FBS drop-downs into the SWAC. The 2025 "33" comes from the Defender's reprint of the HERO list.
- I could not compare football rosters year over year. ESPN's football data covers only players with statistics, and several school roster sites blocked automated reads. A hand comparison of official rosters remains possible.
- All-SWAC departures for the 2025 cycle (2024 all-conference team) were not tallied; I did not retrieve the 2024 all-conference list.
- Scholarship versus walk-on status and graduate-transfer status are not given in any list used here.

## Men's basketball: how many players left each SWAC program after 2024-25 and after 2025-26, what share of minutes was lost, how many all-conference players left, and which teams returned almost nobody? (Women's basketball included as a sub-section.)

## National context: NCAA portal totals, share finding a new school, FCS outcomes, and growth since the 2021 and 2024 rule changes

### Takeaway
Division I portal entries rose from about 17,800 in 2021 to about 23,000 in 2023, and football entries reached records in 2024-25 (more than 4,900 FBS and 3,200 FCS, per ESPN) before FBS entries fell about 23% in the single January 2026 window. Roughly a third of Division I entrants do not land at another NCAA school, and FCS players fare worst: an unverified report put 64% of January 2026 FCS entrants unsigned at mid-month.

### Cited Findings

**Official NCAA figures**

## Academic consequences: graduation and APR data for transfers, and SWAC schools' APR record

### Takeaway
NCAA data released on November 11, 2026 show athletes who transfer more than once graduate at rates 15 to 20 points below those who never transfer, with the largest gap among Black athletes (82% versus 62%). SWAC programs already carry a disproportionate share of APR penalties, but no source I found attributes those penalties to transfer departures.

### Cited Findings

**Transfers and graduation (NCAA data released November 11, 2026)**

- The NCAA analyzed six-year graduation for about 36,000 Division I athletes who enrolled in 2018-19. Across race, gender and sport, athletes who transferred more than once typically graduated at rates 10 points below one-time transfers and 15 to 20 points below athletes who never transferred. — [AP via Spectrum News NY1, November 11, 2026](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/11/11/ncaa-warns-multiple-transfers-tied-to-sharply-lower-graduation-rates-in-college-football)
- Black athletes, all sports: 82% graduated with no transfers, 74% with one, 62% with more than one. The gap between non-transfers and multiple transfers was 20 points for Black athletes and 14 for white athletes. — [AP via Spectrum News NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/11/11/ncaa-warns-multiple-transfers-tied-to-sharply-lower-graduation-rates-in-college-football)
- FCS football: a 21-point drop between non-transfers and multiple transfers. FBS: a 27- or 28-point drop (the AP text gives both). — [AP via Spectrum News NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/11/11/ncaa-warns-multiple-transfers-tied-to-sharply-lower-graduation-rates-in-college-football)
- Men's basketball: 74% for non-transfers, 80% for one-time transfers, 67% for multiple transfers. Women's basketball: 90% for non-transfers, 77% for multiple transfers. — [AP via Spectrum News NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/11/11/ncaa-warns-multiple-transfers-tied-to-sharply-lower-graduation-rates-in-college-football)

### Inferences
- The NCAA graduation data and my SWAC roster tally point the same way: a fifth of SWAC men's basketball players in 2025-26 had already played for three or more schools, which is the group the NCAA finds graduating 15 to 20 points less often. That is a link by reasoning; no dataset I found measures graduation outcomes for SWAC transfers specifically.
- Men's basketball is an exception a careful brief should acknowledge: one-time transfers graduate at a higher rate (80%) than non-transfers (74%). The academic case is against repeated transfers, not transfers as such.
- SWAC schools face APR exposure from departures (lost retention points when a leaver is below the grade threshold) and take in many multi-time transfers whose graduation odds are lower. That makes the academic argument more pressing for them than for wealthier programs, but it is an inference, and Florida A&M's own account blames internal support systems.
- The NCAA is an interested party: Baker released the data while pressing Congress for legislation. A brief should say so.
