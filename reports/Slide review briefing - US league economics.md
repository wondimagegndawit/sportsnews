## 2. Media rights detail and sources

- NFL: CBS, Fox, NBC, ESPN/ABC, Amazon, 2023–2033 seasons, reported $110B total. Per partner: ESPN $2.7B, Fox $2.2–2.25B, CBS $2.1B, NBC $2.0B, Amazon $1.0–1.2B. YouTube Sunday Ticket ~$2B/yr. Netflix original ~$75M/game; May 2026 five-game extension fee undisclosed. NFL opt-out available after 2029 season; not exercised. Sources: SportsPro/CNBC March 2026; Bloomberg 2021; Sports Media Watch Aug 2026.
- NBA: Disney ~$2.6B, NBC ~$2.5B, Amazon $1.8–1.9B per year; $76B NBA-only or $77B incl. ~$2.2B WNBA allocation. Sources: ESPN July 2024; Sports Media Watch citing WSJ.
- MLB (2026–2028): Fox ~$729M, Turner $470M (AP) or $535M (SBJ), ESPN $550M (now anchored on MLB.TV distribution), NBC/Peacock $200M, Netflix $50M (AP/CNBC) or $35M (Forbes). Turner and Netflix discrepancies unresolved. Sources: AP Nov 2025; CNBC Nov 2025; Ampere Analysis Oct 2026.
- NHL: ESPN ~$400M + Turner $225M through 2027-28 (SBJ estimates). Rogers Canada C$11B / 12 years from 2026-27 (~US$7.7B, escalating). Sources: Sportico March 2025; CBC; FOS quoting Bettman June 2026.
- MLS: Apple original 10-yr $2.5B; restructured Nov 2025 (Season Pass scrapped, ends after 2028-29, $200M for 2026, $107.5M for 2027 sprint, $275M each 2027-28 and 2028-29). Fox and TelevisaUnivision linear fees undisclosed. Single-sourced to Sportico. MLS targeting $400–500M/yr next cycle.
- WNBA: Old cycle AAV $43M (final year ~$60M; ESPN ~$33M in 2025, ION $13M/yr). New cycle from 2026: $200M/yr foundational (CNBC); $281M/yr full portfolio with CBS, ION, USA Sports, NBA TV (FOS May 2026, single source, not league-confirmed). Sources: Sportico July 2024; CNBC June 2025; FOS May 2026.

Universal caveat: no US league or network officially discloses rights fees. Every figure is press-reported from sources inside the deals.

## 3. Spotrac column definitions (for payroll numbers)

- Total Cash: full cash outlay for the season. Use this for "what teams paid players."
- Active: cash to players currently on the active roster only. Undercounts (injured players still get paid).
- Dead: guaranteed money to waived/released players.
- Retained (NBA): cash already paid to players later traded away. Retained (MLB/NHL): ongoing salary obligations kept in a trade.
- Total Cash is NOT Active + Dead. Gap: NFL 18%, NHL 18%, MLB 42% (injured list, mid-season trades, 40-man players), NBA and WNBA ~1%.
- Total Cap Allocations (cap tracker, not cash tracker): active cap hits + dead cap + reserve lists. Cap accounting, not cash.
- MLS tab is cap allocation, not cash; MLSPA guide ($631M guaranteed comp, spring 2026) is the closer cash analogue.

## 4. Season choice (which year each league uses and why)

- NFL 2025: 2026 season in progress; same media cycle either year. 2025 understates current pay since 2026 cap jumped to $301.2M.
- NBA 2025-26: completed; year one of new media deal.
- MLB: 2025 is in the workbook (complete). 2026 is preferable (year one of new ESPN/NBC/Netflix deals, last year of CBA) if the table is pulled.
- NHL 2025-26: completed; US deals unchanged through 2027-28.
- MLS 2026: year one of restructured Apple deal; cap allocation nearly locked.
- WNBA 2026: new media deal, new CBA ($7.0M cap), 15 teams. 2025 shown for comparison.

## 5. Cap structure quick reference

- Hard caps: NFL ($301.2M, 2026; minimum cash spend 90% over multi-year periods), NHL ($95.5M 2025-26; $104M 2026-27; $113.5M 2027-28), NLL, ECHL.
- Soft caps: NBA (Bird rights, exceptions, luxury tax 1.0x–4.75x, aprons), MLS (DPs, GAM, TAM, off-budget supplemental roster), Major League Volleyball (cap + luxury tax, amounts undisclosed).
- No cap: MLB (Competitive Balance Tax, $244M base 2026 with surcharges to $304M; rates 20–110%). CBA expires Dec 1, 2026; owners proposed $245.3M hard cap / $171.2M floor; lockout widely expected.
- WNBA: $7.0M (2026), 85% floor, $1.4M max; exceptions allow some teams above cap. NWSL: $3.7M (2026) with cap-exempt minimum deals and a $1M High Impact Player exception under grievance.

## 6. League revenue sources and reliability

- Audited/CBA-defined: NBA BRI ($11.68B, 2025-26). NHL HRR (final 2025-26 pending). NFL all-revenue via Packers annual report (national share $453.2M/club FY2026).
- League-stated: NFL $23B+ (CFO to owners, FY2025); MLB $12.1B (2024); NHL $7.5–8B projected (Bettman).
- Estimated: Sportico and Forbes annual valuations (MLS ~$2.5B, WNBA ~$410M for 2025; MLB ~$12.4B Forbes-implied or ~$13.2B Sportico-implied for 2025).
- Avoid as primary: Statista, DocSports, Wikipedia tables.

## 7. College football comparison (if on the slide)

- Cash-basis by nature: no signing-bonus proration, no dead money.
- House settlement cap: $20.5M per school (2025-26), $21.3M (2026-27), ~4%/yr growth; football ~75% share (reported, not official), ~$14–15M per Power 4 team.
- Third-party NIL uncapped on top; deals over $600 cleared by College Sports Commission.
- 2025-26 all-sports revenue sharing: ~$1.77B (NCAA/CSC). Opendorse football-only projection: ~$1.9B for 2025 (revenue share $1.4B + collectives $213M + commercial NIL $290M). Do not mix these two figures.
- Top rosters (e.g., Ohio State) reported ~$50M all-in.

## 8. Common slide errors to check

- Mixing cap-basis (MLS, cap × teams) with cash-basis (Spotrac Total Cash) in one chart.
- Labeling NBA/NHL seasons as a single year (2025-26 ≠ 2025).
- Presenting $77B NBA deal as NBA-only (includes WNBA allocation).
- Using $281M WNBA AAV without noting it is single-sourced; $200M is the corroborated figure.
- Using $12B NHL-style "all national" figures without separating US vs Canada.
- Citing NFL $14.5B as total revenue (it is the national pool only).
- Comparing old WNBA AAV ($43M) to new without noting the $60M final-year figure.
- Treating MLB's 2025 revenue as confirmed (it is not, as of Oct 2026).
