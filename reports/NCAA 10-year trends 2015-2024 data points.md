Page numbers below refer to the PDF. Fiscal years run July 1 to June 30, so "2024" means FY2023-24.

## 1. Scope and definitions (pp. 2-4)

- Data come from the NCAA Membership Financial Reporting System for fiscal years 2014-15 through 2023-24; **352 active Division I institutions** (public and private) provided data (p. 2).
- Quartiles are based on each year's total expenses within each subdivision (p. 2).
- **Generated revenue sources** (p. 4): ticket sales; NCAA and conference distributions; contributions from alumni and others; and "Other" (guarantees and options, third-party support, concessions, broadcast rights, royalties/advertising/sponsorships, sports camps, endowment/investment income).
- **Allocated revenue sources** (p. 4), labeled "Allocated support": student activity fees; direct government support; direct institutional support; indirect institutional support.
- **Net generated revenue** (the "Net Operating Results Excluding Allocated Support" chart measure, pp. 33-34) = generated revenue minus total expenses. Allocated revenue is excluded from the revenue side only; no expenses are removed.
- **Self-sufficiency** = generated revenue divided by total expenses (p. 39 footnote).
- Subdivisions: FBS Autonomy (the 69 schools in the autonomy conferences, p. 20), FBS Nonautonomy, FCS, and "Basketball Subdivision" (Division I schools without football).

## 3. Where the money comes from, FY2024 (pp. 12-19)

- "Division I institutions reported total revenue of almost **$20.5 billion** on athletics in 2024, a **7% increase** compared with the previous year" (p. 12).
- "Sixty-three percent of total Division I revenue (**$13 billion**) was generated by the athletics departments, and 37% (**$7.4 billion**) was allocated revenue from institution/government support and student fees" (p. 12).
- "While FBS autonomy schools accounted for about **55%** of the Division I total revenue ($20.5 billion), they accounted for almost **77%** of the total generated revenue" (p. 12).
- Share of total D-I revenue by subdivision (p. 13): FBS Autonomy 55%; FCS 18%; FBS Nonautonomy 15%; Basketball Subdivision 12%.
- All-Division I revenue mix (p. 14): institution and government support **30%**; media rights/NCAA-conference distributions/bowl revenue **20%**; donor contributions and endowments **20%**; ticket sales 11%; student fees 7%; royalties/licensing 6%; other revenue 5%; guarantees 1%.
- Generated revenue as a share of total revenue (p. 15): **89%** for FBS Autonomy, about **44%** for FBS Nonautonomy, **30%** for FCS, **24%** for Basketball Subdivision.
- "For FBS autonomy schools, the largest revenue categories were media rights, NCAA/conference distributions and bowl-related revenue, accounting for about 31%" (p. 15).
- "For the other three groups, institution and government support was the largest revenue category: **41%** for FBS nonautonomy schools, **58%** for FCS and **64%** for the Basketball Subdivision schools" (p. 15).
- FBS Autonomy revenue mix (p. 16): media/NCAA-conference/bowl 31%; donors and endowments 26%; ticket sales 17%; institution and government support 10%; royalties/licensing 9%; other 6%; student fees 1%; guarantees under 1%.
- FBS Nonautonomy revenue mix (p. 17): institution and government support 41%; student fees 15%; donors 13%; media/NCAA-conference/bowl 11%; ticket sales 7%; royalties 5%; guarantees 4%; other 4%.
- FCS revenue mix (p. 18): institution and government support 58%; donors 13%; student fees 12%; media/NCAA-conference/bowl 5%; ticket sales 4%; guarantees 3%; other 3%; royalties 2%.
- Basketball Subdivision revenue mix (p. 19): institution and government support 64%; student fees 12%; donors 9%; media/NCAA-conference/bowl 5%; ticket sales 4% (remaining slices of 1-3% are not legible in the text layer).

## 4. Where the money goes, FY2024 (pp. 20-27)

- "Division I institutions reported spending over **$20.8 billion** on athletics in 2024, **9% more** than the previous year" (p. 20). Spending growth (9%) outpaced revenue growth (7%).
- "The **69 FBS autonomy schools** accounted for 55% of total Division I expenses" (p. 20).
- "Overall, Division I schools spent almost **$3.3 billion** on financial aid for student-athletes, accounting for 16% of total expenses" (p. 20).
- "Over **$3.9 billion** were spent on coach compensation, the largest spending category at 19% of total expenses" (p. 20).
- Share of total D-I expenses by subdivision (p. 21): FBS Autonomy 55%; FCS 17%; FBS Nonautonomy 16%; Basketball Subdivision 12%.
- All-Division I expense mix (p. 22): coach compensation **19%**; facilities **18%**; administrative compensation **17%**; athletics aid **16%**; other expenses 14%; game and travel 12%; recruiting 2%; medical 1%; guarantees 1%.
- "FBS autonomy schools spent over **$1.2 billion** on student athletics aid in 2024, accounting for approximately **11%** of total athletics spending" (p. 23). Aid was 17% of spending at FBS Nonautonomy, about 25% at FCS and 26% at Basketball Subdivision schools (p. 23).
- "For both FBS autonomy and nonautonomy schools, coach compensation was the largest spending category, accounting for almost 19% of total expenses" (p. 23).
- FBS Autonomy expense mix (p. 24): coach compensation 19%; administrative compensation 19%; facilities 19%; other 16%; game and travel 12%; athletics aid 11%; recruiting 2%; medical 2%; guarantees 1%.
- FBS Nonautonomy expense mix (p. 25): coach compensation 19%; athletics aid 17%; administrative compensation 16%; facilities 16%; other 14%; game and travel 13%; recruiting 2%; medical 2%; guarantees 1%.
- FCS expense mix (p. 26): athletics aid 25%; coach compensation 19%; facilities 16%; administrative compensation 13%; other 12%; game and travel 12%; recruiting, medical, guarantees 1% each.
- Basketball Subdivision expense mix (p. 27): athletics aid 26%; coach compensation 19%; facilities 16%; administrative compensation 14%; game and travel 12%; other 11%; recruiting, medical, guarantees 1% each.

## 5. Ten-year trends, FY2015 to FY2024 (pp. 29-37)

Text-stated findings:

- "Over the 10-year period, median generated revenue grew by **56%** among FBS autonomy schools and **35%** among FBS nonautonomy schools. Median total expenses grew by **81%** for autonomy schools and **51%** for nonautonomy schools" (p. 36).
- "In 2015, the median expense gap between the autonomy and nonautonomy groups was **$60 million**. It is now **$118 million**" (p. 36).
- "The median net operating deficit for FBS autonomy schools exceeded **$15 million** in 2024, an increase of **$6.5 million** compared to the previous year" (p. 36).
- "The median deficit for nonautonomy schools was **$27 million** in 2024, which is less than a $1 million increase from the previous year" (p. 36).
- FCS: median generated revenue grew **47%** and median total expenses **57%** over ten years; the median net operating deficit went from **$11.9 million (2015) to $16.8 million (2024)**, a 41% change (p. 37).
- Basketball Subdivision: median generated revenue grew **29%** while median total expenses grew **51%**; the median deficit widened about 49% (p. 37).

- Median total revenue, FBS Autonomy (p. 29): roughly $90M in 2015, rising to ~$120M by 2019, dipping to ~$100M in 2021, then ~$140M in 2022 and ~$150M in 2024 (the FY2024 median is stated in the text as $149.3M). FBS Nonautonomy: roughly $30M in 2015 to ~$45M in 2024 (text: $47.0M).
- Median generated revenue, FBS Autonomy (p. 30): roughly $85M in 2015, ~$110M in 2019, a COVID drop to slightly under $80M in 2021, then ~$115M in 2022 and ~$135M in 2024 (text: $133.8M). FBS Nonautonomy: roughly $13M in 2015 to ~$17M in 2024 (text: $17.2M), essentially flat.
- Median total expenses, FBS Autonomy (p. 31): roughly $90M in 2015, ~$120M in 2019, ~$105M in 2021, ~$130M in 2022, ~$145M in 2023 and ~$165M in 2024 (text: $166.8M). FBS Nonautonomy: roughly $30M in 2015 to ~$50M in 2024 (text: $48.7M).
- Median net operating result excluding allocated support, all FBS (p. 33): roughly −$12M in 2015, widening to ~−$19M in 2019 and 2020, ~−$23M in 2021, ~−$19M in 2022, ~−$22M in 2023 and ~−$25M in 2024 (text: −$25.13M).
- Same measure by FBS group (p. 34): Autonomy within a few million of break-even (roughly −$3M to −$4M) from 2015 through 2018, ~−$7M to −$8M in 2019 and 2020, ~−$18M in 2021, ~−$3M in 2022, ~−$9M in 2023 and ~−$15M in 2024 (text: −$15.2M). Nonautonomy roughly −$19M in 2015, widening almost every year to ~−$27M in 2024 (text: −$27.1M).
- Reading of those two charts: the autonomy median sat close to break-even from 2015 through 2018 and again in 2022, and the FY2024 autonomy deficit is the worst non-COVID year in the series. The nonautonomy deficit widened in every year except 2022.

## 6. Dashboard indicators (pp. 39-47)

- Median revenue self-sufficiency, FY2024 (p. 45-47): FBS Autonomy **89%** ("the lowest except for 2021 with COVID-19 impact"); FBS Nonautonomy **38%** ("consistent with the trend in prepandemic years"); FCS **25%** (27% the prior year); Basketball Subdivision **18%**.
- Self-sufficiency chart readings (p. 39, approximate, no data labels): FBS Autonomy slightly under 100% from 2015 to 2018, ~95% in 2019, low 90s in 2020, low 80s in 2021, slightly under 100% in 2022, mid 90s in 2023 and 89% in 2024 (the 2024 figure is stated in the text). FBS Nonautonomy ~40% from 2015 to 2019, mid 30s in 2020, ~30% in 2021, then high 30s (text: 38% in 2024).
- "Over the 10-year period, coach compensation as a proportion of total expenses has been relatively consistent in the range of **18%-20%** for both autonomy and nonautonomy schools, except during the pandemic years" (p. 45). Total compensation ranged **32%-36%** of expenses (p. 45).
- "The median ratio of athletics expenses to institutional expenses has been stable for FBS schools in the **6%-7%** range" (p. 45). FCS: **9%**, "the highest among the three subdivisions" (p. 46). Basketball Subdivision: 7% (p. 47).
- "In 2024, the median expense growth gap between athletics spending and institutional spending is **−0.2%** for autonomy schools and **2.5%** for nonautonomy FBS schools. These numbers seem to suggest the COVID-19 impact (adjustment) in athletics spending is over" (p. 45). FCS gap 0.9% (p. 46); Basketball Subdivision 0.9% (p. 47).
- FCS: coach compensation steady at 19% of expenses; total compensation 32% (p. 46).

## 9. How these numbers connect to the four questions

- Pre-NIL baseline: the PNR table (p. 35) shows 20 to 29 FBS schools covering their expenses in every pre-NIL year 2015-2020, so the deficit predates NIL; FY2021 (9 schools) is the COVID year.
- Revenue vs. cost: generated revenue at autonomy schools grew 56% over ten years, but expenses grew 81% (p. 36). Revenue growth is real; spending outran it.
- Concentration: 69 autonomy schools take 77% of generated revenue and 55% of all spending (pp. 12, 20); the autonomy/nonautonomy median expense gap doubled from $60M to $118M (p. 36).
- Who fills the gap: $7.4B of the $20.5B is allocated support (p. 12); it is 41% of revenue at nonautonomy FBS, 58% at FCS, 64% at non-football D-I (p. 15), and direct institutional support is the single largest revenue line for bottom-half FBS, all of FCS and all of the Basketball Subdivision (pp. 56, 68, 80).
- Cost-cutting room: coach compensation (19%), administrative compensation (17%), facilities (18%) and aid (16%) together are 70% of all D-I spending (p. 22). Coach and total compensation shares have been flat for a decade (p. 45), so cuts would have to come from headcount or facilities commitments, not from a trend reversal that is already under way.
- Caveat on timing: this report ends at FY2024, before the House settlement's revenue-sharing payments (which begin in FY2026) and before the FY2025 data that Knight-Newhouse has already published for public FBS schools.
