**Basis for the numbers below**

- Every figure here is published by the source linked under it; nothing is computed by us. The only exception is the sports count, where the per-school figures come from the NCAA's membership directory (listed in the appendix) and the average is simple arithmetic on that list.
- The latest full-sample NCAA report (all 69 autonomy schools, public and private) covers FY2024. Every FY2025 Power Four figure, from any source, is public schools only, because the 15 private members (Boston College, Duke, Miami, Notre Dame, SMU, Stanford, Syracuse, Wake Forest, Northwestern, USC, Baylor, BYU, TCU, Vanderbilt) plus Pittsburgh (exempt under Pennsylvania law) do not release NCAA financial reports.
  - Source links: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf); [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf)
- The FY2025 sources are: the Knight-Newhouse College Athletics Database (Knight Commission and Syracuse's Newhouse School; 53 public Power Four schools), as reported in its own FY2025 summary and in the Senate Commerce Committee and Cardinal News analyses of it; the nil-ncaa.com compilation of 52 public Power Four NCAA reports, which the Drake Group adopted in its August 2026 appendix to Congress; and the schools' own filings and local press for named examples. Sportico also maintains a public database of these filings that is still being filled in for FY2025.
  - Source links: [Knight-Newhouse database overview](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/knightnewhouse_webinar_2026.pdf); [Sen. Cantwell report, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf); [Cardinal News, Sept 2026](https://cardinalnews.org/2026/09/04/college-sports-are-awash-in-more-money-than-ever-yet-a-growing-number-of-schools-are-losing-money-on-intercollegiate-athletics/); [nil-ncaa.com](https://nil-ncaa.com/); [Drake Group appendix, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf); [Sportico College Sports Finances Database](https://d3data.sportico.com/NCAADatabase/index.html)
- Three definitions of "burn" are in use and they give very different answers. (1) Bottom line: all revenue, including university support and student fees, minus expenses. (2) NCAA "net generated revenue": revenue excluding university support, state money and student fees, minus expenses; donations still count. (3) Operating basis: donations and endowment income are stripped out too. Each bullet says which one it is using.
  - Source link: [NCAA definitions, Dec 2025, p. 4](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- FY2025 (ended June 30, 2025) is the last year before House settlement revenue sharing, which started July 1, 2025. Only $52.9M of early athlete revenue share appears across all 110 public FBS schools in FY2025, and FY2026 reports are not due until January 15, 2027.
  - Source links: [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf); [Drake Group, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf)

**On average, how much did Power 4 athletic departments burn last year (FY2025)?**

- On the operating basis (before donations, endowment income, university support and student fees), the average public Power Four department lost $62.9M in FY2025. By conference: SEC −$78.1M, ACC −$71.6M, Big 12 −$57.4M, Big Ten −$48.2M. The hole was filled by booster contributions averaging $44.4M per school, university support and student fees averaging $14.8M, and endowment income averaging $3.9M, leaving an average bottom line of +$172,235.
  - Source links: [nil-ncaa.com (52 public Power Four schools, FY2025)](https://nil-ncaa.com/); [Drake Group appendix to Congress, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf)
- On the NCAA basis (university support and student fees excluded, donations included), only 14 of the 53 public Power Four departments in the Knight-Newhouse database covered their expenses in FY2025. Another 20 showed a surplus only because of university support or mandatory student fees, and the remaining 19 lost money outright. Of the 14 self-sufficient schools, 7 are Big Ten, 5 SEC, 2 Big 12 and none ACC. Ten years earlier only seven Power Four schools were losing money.
  - Source links: [Sen. Cantwell report, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf); [Cardinal News analysis of Knight-Newhouse, Sept 2026](https://cardinalnews.org/2026/09/04/college-sports-are-awash-in-more-money-than-ever-yet-a-growing-number-of-schools-are-losing-money-on-intercollegiate-athletics/)
- On the bottom line, conference averages in FY2025 were close to zero: SEC +$343,475, Big Ten +$647,246, Big 12 +$1,703,445, ACC −$3,362,495 per school. Three of the four conferences lost money in aggregate in FY2025; only the Big 12 was profitable as a group, and the ACC has lost money in five straight non-COVID years.
  - Source links: [nil-ncaa.com conference tables](https://nil-ncaa.com/sec/); [Cardinal News, Sept 2026](https://cardinalnews.org/2026/09/04/college-sports-are-awash-in-more-money-than-ever-yet-a-growing-number-of-schools-are-losing-money-on-intercollegiate-athletics/)
- The full-sample NCAA benchmark (all 69 autonomy schools including privates, FY2024, NCAA basis): median net generated revenue −$15.2M, $6.5M worse than the year before; range −$90.5M to +$43.8M; the median school was 89% self-sufficient. GAO, on the same data: 49 of 69 Power schools spent more than they generated, the median gap grew from $2.7M to $15.2M over ten years, and the worst 20% ran gaps of $42.7M to $90.5M.
  - Source links: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, pp. 7, 36 and 45](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf); [GAO-26-108640, July 2026](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf)
- What the subsidy looks like. Across all 110 public FBS programs in FY2025, university funding, government support and student fees were $2.5B of $12.8B in revenue. University support to the 53 public Power Four programs rose $519.9M between 2015 and 2025; the largest FY2025 amounts were Cincinnati $51.7M, Arizona State $48.0M, South Carolina $43.7M, Cal $42.7M and Colorado $41.8M. On FY2024 data, GAO found university contributions to Power programs nearly tripled from $445M to $1.2B in a decade, with the median Power school receiving $18.1M and 48% of Power schools charging student athletics fees (median $125 per undergraduate).
  - Source links: [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf); [Sen. Cantwell report appendix, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf); [GAO-26-108640, July 2026](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf)

- About a quarter. The NCAA's full-sample figure (all 69 autonomy schools, FY2024) is 26% of total revenue for "donor contributions and endowments," the second-largest source after media rights and conference distributions at 31% and ahead of ticket sales at 17%. Division I-wide the share is 20%.
  - Source link: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, pp. 14 and 16](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)

By conference the contribution averages were SEC $65.8M, ACC $38.1M, Big Ten $36.0M and Big 12 $34.8M. Knight-Newhouse's FY2025 median for the 16 public Big Ten schools is $34.1M.
  - Source links: [nil-ncaa.com (52 public Power Four schools, FY2025)](https://nil-ncaa.com/); [Drake Group appendix, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf); [CBS Sports citing Knight-Newhouse, Sept 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/ucla-money-problems-donors-martin-jarmond-firing-athletic-director/)

**On average, how many sports do Power 4 athletic departments sponsor?**

- About 22. The NCAA membership directory lists each school's sponsored sports for 2026-27; across the 68 Power Four schools (full list in the appendix) the mean is 21.7 and the median 21, ranging from 16 (Kansas State, UCF, Mississippi State) to 37 (Stanford, which its own site counts as 36). By conference: Big Ten mean 23.9, median 23; ACC 23.6, 22; SEC 20.1, 21; Big 12 18.8, 18.
  - Source links: [NCAA membership directory](https://web3.ncaa.org/directory/); [Stanford Facts](https://facts.stanford.edu/campus-life/cardinal-athletics)
- Counting basis matters. The NCAA counts men's and women's cross country, indoor track and outdoor track as six separate sports. The federal EADA database lets schools report them as one line, and most do, so EADA team counts run three to four below the NCAA count. Use the NCAA count when comparing to the NCAA minimum.
  - Source link: [EADA data files](https://ope.ed.gov/athletics/#/datafile/list)
- The floor is 16. FBS members must sponsor at least 16 varsity sports including football, with at least six men's or mixed and eight women's sports, and the bylaw allows no waivers. Seventeen Power Four schools sit at 16 to 18 sports, with little room to cut.
  - Source link: [NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27, Bylaws 20.9.9.1 and 20.9.9.5](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- For context, the average Division I school sponsored 19.2 teams and had 577 athletes in 2024-25, and GAO counts 40,486 athletes at the 69 Power schools on FY2024 data, or about 587 per school.
  - Source links: [NCAA Sports Sponsorship and Participation Rates Report, Sept 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/sportpart/2025RES_SportsSponsorshipParticipationRatesReport.pdf); [GAO-26-108640, Table 1](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf)
- Only two of those sports pay for themselves. The NCAA's last sport-level medians (FY2016, autonomy schools) showed football +$19.0M and men's basketball +$2.8M on generated revenue, and every other sport negative: women's basketball −$3.2M, baseball −$1.5M, rowing −$1.5M, volleyball −$1.4M, track −$1.3M, softball −$1.3M. The NCAA has not published sport-level medians since.
  - Source link: [NCAA Revenues and Expenses 2004-2016, Table 4.9](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2017D1RES_D1RevExpReportFinal.pdf)
- House changed roster math, not sport counts. Scholarship caps became roster limits on July 1, 2025 (football 105, basketball 15, baseball 34, softball 25, soccer 28, volleyball 18). No Power Four school has dropped a sport citing the settlement; the recent changes are Utah dropping beach volleyball (April 2025), Iowa State dropping gymnastics and adding women's wrestling (2026), and Nebraska adding flag football.
  - Source links: [NCAA Proposal 2025-11, roster limits](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108635); [Utah Athletics, Apr 2025](https://utahutes.com/news/2025/4/29/utah-athletics-to-discontinue-beach-volleyball-program-at-conclusion-of-academic-year); [Iowa State Athletics, Apr 2026](https://cyclones.com/news/2026/4/16/athletics-iowa-state-adds-womens-wrestling-as-18th-varsity-program); [Huskers.com, Jan 2026](https://huskers.com/news/2026/01/16/nebraska-announces-addition-of-womens-flag-football)
