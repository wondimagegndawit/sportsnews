**Basis for the numbers below**

- The FY2025 figures (fiscal year ended June 30, 2025) are computed by us from the NCAA Membership Financial Reporting System (MFRS) reports of 52 of the 53 public Power Four athletic departments, the same filings behind the Knight-Newhouse database. 48 rows come from the College Sports Finance compilation of those filings, Indiana from its primary report, and Auburn, Florida and Rutgers from newspaper coverage of their filings. 13 of the rows were checked to the dollar against the school's own PDF or independent press. Georgia Tech's FY2025 report is not public. The full school-level table is in the appendix.
  - Source links: [College Sports Finance SEC](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/sec); [Big Ten](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-ten); [Big 12](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-12); [ACC](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/acc); [Utah FY25 NCAA report](https://utahutes.com/documents/download/2026/1/21/FY25_NCAA_Revenue_and_Expense_Report.pdf); [Tennessee FY25 NCAA report](https://utsports.com/documents/download/2026/1/15/FY25_NCAA_AUP.pdf); [Indiana FY25 NCAA report](https://npr.brightspotcdn.com/f8/cd/6f6273e141998c6ff6b63178b250/2025-ncaa-frs-report.pdf)
- The 15 private Power Four schools (Boston College, Duke, Miami, Notre Dame, SMU, Stanford, Syracuse, Wake Forest, Northwestern, USC, Baylor, BYU, TCU, Vanderbilt) plus Pittsburgh (exempt under Pennsylvania law) do not publish NCAA reports, so every FY2025 Power Four aggregate from any source is public schools only. The NCAA's own report, which covers all 69 autonomy schools including privates, runs only through FY2024 and is used as the full-sample benchmark.
  - Source links: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf); [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf)
- Three definitions of "burn" are used in this market and they give very different answers. (1) Bottom line: all revenue, including university support and student fees, minus expenses. (2) NCAA "net generated revenue": revenue excluding university support, state money and student fees, minus expenses. Donations still count as revenue here. (3) Operating basis: also strips out donations and endowment income. The bullets say which one is being used.
  - Source link: [NCAA definitions, Dec 2025, p. 4](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- FY2025 is the last year before House settlement revenue sharing, which started July 1, 2025. Only $52.9M of early athlete revenue share shows up across all 110 public FBS schools in FY2025. FY2026 filings are not due until January 15, 2027.
  - Source links: [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf); [Drake Group, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf)

**On average, how much did Power 4 athletic departments burn last year (FY2025)?**

- On the reported bottom line, the typical public Power Four department roughly broke even. Median net result +$0.9M, mean −$0.8M, across 52 schools; 19 of 52 reported a deficit. That bottom line already includes an average of $16.0M (median $10.4M) per school of university support, state money and student fees.
  - Source link: [Computed from 52 FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/sec)
- Strip out that support and the picture flips. On the NCAA's net generated revenue basis, the median public Power Four department burned $10.6M and the mean burned $17.2M in FY2025; 36 of 50 were in the red. Knight-Newhouse, using the same filings for 53 schools, found only 14 covered their expenses without university support or student fees; 20 more were profitable only because of that support; the other 19 lost money outright.
  - Source links: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-ten); [Sen. Cantwell report, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf); [Cardinal News, Sept 2026](https://cardinalnews.org/2026/09/04/college-sports-are-awash-in-more-money-than-ever-yet-a-growing-number-of-schools-are-losing-money-on-intercollegiate-athletics/)
- Strip out donations as well and the average operating loss is about $63M per school. On that basis (operating revenue only, before contributions, endowment income, university support and fees) the Power Four averaged a $62.9M loss in FY2025: SEC −$78.1M, ACC −$71.6M, Big 12 −$57.4M, Big Ten −$48.2M. Donations ($44.4M average), university support and fees ($14.8M) and endowment income ($3.9M) filled the hole.
  - Source links: [nil-ncaa.com FY2025 tables](https://nil-ncaa.com/); [Drake Group appendix to Congress, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf)
- The full-sample NCAA benchmark (all 69 autonomy schools including privates, FY2024): median net generated revenue −$15.2M, worse by $6.5M than the year before; range −$90.5M to +$43.8M. GAO, on the same data: 49 of 69 Power schools spent more than they generated, and the median gap grew from $2.7M to $15.2M over ten years.
  - Source links: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, pp. 7 and 36](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf); [GAO-26-108640, July 2026](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf)
- By conference, FY2025 (median bottom line / median net generated): SEC +$1.3M / −$3.7M (15 schools); Big Ten +$2.4M / −$6.1M (16); Big 12 +$0.2M / −$13.9M (13); ACC −$3.0M / −$22.9M (8). The ACC and Big 12 lean hardest on campus money: median allocated support $22.1M and $14.8M per school versus $9.1M in the Big Ten and $3.9M in the SEC.
  - Source link: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-12)
- Nine public schools report zero allocated support (Texas, Penn State, LSU, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Mississippi State, Purdue, Kansas State). The biggest subsidies are Arizona State $59.7M, Cincinnati $51.7M, South Carolina $43.7M, Colorado $43.5M, Cal $43.1M, Florida State $42.5M and Houston $41.2M.
  - Source links: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/acc); [Sen. Cantwell report appendix, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf)
- Largest FY2025 bottom-line deficits: Rutgers −$47.2M on the ledger (−$78M before $30.8M of university, state and fee support, a record), Oklahoma −$43.7M (first SEC year on a partial $12.5M distribution), Cal −$24.3M, Texas −$23.3M, UCLA −$21.6M (only because the university put in $31.1M), North Carolina −$15.0M. Largest surpluses: Alabama +$32.7M, Florida +$20.0M, Tennessee +$15.9M, Ohio State +$15.7M, Michigan +$14.3M.
  - Source links: [NJ.com, Jan 2026](https://www.nj.com/rutgers/2026/01/rutgers-athletics-deficit-hit-record-78-million-in-2024-25-new-ad-says-its-got-to-get-better.html); [247Sports on Oklahoma, Jan 2026](https://247sports.com/college/oklahoma/article/oklahoma-ncaa-financial-report-for-2025-fiscal-year-sooners-first-in-sec-272740382/); [CBS Sports on UCLA, Sept 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/ucla-money-problems-donors-martin-jarmond-firing-athletic-director/); [AL.com SEC roundup, Apr 2026](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/alabama-athletics-revenue-spending-stacks-101116089.html)
- FY2026 will be worse because the $20.5M revenue share plus roughly $6M of added scholarships lands on these same budgets. Early reported FY2026 results and budgets: LSU −$44M actual (−$26M projected FY2027), Colorado −$27M, Washington −$19M cash flow covered with loans, Kansas −$15.8M actual, Louisville −$12.5M, Minnesota −$9M, and Michigan balanced only after $15M from the university. The Drake Group's accountant expects "many" Power schools to show operating losses above $100M for FY2026.
  - Source links: [FootballScoop on LSU, Sept 2026](https://www.footballscoop.com/2026/09/24/lsu-athletics-projects-26-million-deficit-after-44-million-deficit-last-year-president-says-program-must-stop-that-bleeding); [USA Today on Colorado](https://www.aol.com/articles/colorado-financial-report-shows-limits-095241132.html); [Seattle Times on Washington, June 2025](https://www.union-bulletin.com/sports/college_sports/university_of_washington/here-s-what-uw-s-athletic-department-budget-looks-like-for-2026/article_c658300c-4ee2-575c-abb0-60128bd81a12.html); [Kansas City Star, Aug 2026](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/ku-athletics-board-approves-13-103000239.html); [WDRB on Louisville](https://www.wdrb.com/sports/louisville-athletics-projects-a-12-5-million-deficit-in-1st-budget-of-revenue-sharing-era/article_071eb72d-f599-45c0-90fe-26af47608af9.html); [Star Tribune on Minnesota](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/gophers-athletics-forecasts-another-9-150200514.html); [MGoBlue, June 2025](https://mgoblue.com/news/2025/6/12/general-michigan-athletics-projects-balanced-budget-for-fiscal-year-2026); [Drake Group, Aug 2026](https://www.thedrakegroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FINL-APPENDIX-B-08.21.26.pdf)

**On average, what percentage of Power 4 athletic department revenue is donations?**

- Roughly a quarter. Across the 52 public Power Four departments in FY2025, cash contributions were 23.0% of total revenue on average and 22.5% at the median; in aggregate, $2.27B of $9.55B, or 23.7%. The average department took in $43.6M of contributions (median $39.2M).
  - Source link: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/sec)
- The NCAA's full-sample figure (all 69 autonomy schools, FY2024) is 26% of total revenue for "donor contributions and endowments," the second-largest source after media rights and conference distributions at 31%, ahead of ticket sales at 17%. The NCAA figure includes endowment income; ours does not, which explains most of the gap.
  - Source link: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, p. 16](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- GAO, on the same FY2024 data: the median Power school received $30.8M in athletics donations and the top 20% received $56.9M or more. Knight-Newhouse's FY2025 Big Ten public median is $34.1M.
  - Source links: [GAO-26-108640, July 2026](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf); [CBS Sports citing Knight-Newhouse, Sept 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/ucla-money-problems-donors-martin-jarmond-firing-athletic-director/)
- By conference, FY2025 (median share / aggregate share): SEC 25.6% / 28.3%; Big 12 24.4% / 25.5%; ACC 23.4% / 22.6%; Big Ten 17.9% / 18.2%. The Big Ten is the least donor-dependent because its media money is the largest.
  - Source link: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-ten)
- The range is wide. Oklahoma State is 50.8% donor-funded ($76.5M), Texas 47.6% ($167.8M, the most in the country), Oklahoma 45.2% ($92.9M, a school record), Tennessee 36.4% ($110.6M), Mississippi State 35.9%. At the other end: Rutgers 5.5% ($8.1M), South Carolina 8.6%, UCLA 8.8%, Maryland 9.1%.
  - Source links: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-12); [247Sports on Oklahoma, Jan 2026](https://247sports.com/college/oklahoma/article/oklahoma-ncaa-financial-report-for-2025-fiscal-year-sooners-first-in-sec-272740382/)
- Donations are still growing, not being cannibalized by NIL collectives: Ohio State a record $68.3M (from $52.5M), Oklahoma a record $92.9M, Auburn $56.4M (up $11.1M), UCF $27.9M (from $15.4M).
  - Source links: [Ohio State News, Jan 2026](https://news.osu.edu/ohio-state-athletics-sets-record-with-336m-in-fy25-revenue-after-national-championship-season/); [247Sports on Oklahoma](https://247sports.com/college/oklahoma/article/oklahoma-ncaa-financial-report-for-2025-fiscal-year-sooners-first-in-sec-272740382/); [AL.com on Auburn, Jan 2026](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/much-money-did-auburn-athletics-222336441.html); [Orlando Sentinel on UCF](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/ucf-athletics-revenue-expenses-grow-100226725.html)
- Two definitional notes. NCAA "contributions" include priority-seating and seat-license payments tied to tickets, so a portion is quasi-ticket revenue. And the 14 public Power Four schools that cover expenses without university support still, in the Senate report's words, "depend significantly on donor money to stay afloat."
  - Source link: [Sen. Cantwell report, Sept 2026](https://www.cantwell.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/spiraling_college_sports_costs_snapshot2.pdf)

**On average, what revenue do Power 4 athletic departments have?**

- FY2025, 52 public Power Four departments: mean total revenue $183.6M, median $178.6M, range $92.8M (Houston) to $352.5M (Texas); combined $9.55B. Expenses: mean $184.4M, median $180.7M, combined $9.59B.
  - Source link: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/sec)
- By conference, FY2025 (mean / median revenue): SEC $224.0M / $205.9M (15 publics); Big Ten $194.9M / $181.7M (16); ACC $167.1M / $158.1M (8); Big 12 $133.4M / $140.3M (13). The SEC average is about $90M above the Big 12 average.
  - Source link: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/big-ten)
- Top of the table: Texas $352.5M, Ohio State $336.1M, Tennessee $304.1M, Michigan $275.8M, Alabama $267.4M, Penn State $254.9M, Texas A&M $235.5M, Georgia $233.5M, LSU $223.5M, Florida $221.0M. Fifteen public schools cleared $200M in FY2025.
  - Source links: [Computed from FY2025 MFRS reports (appendix)](https://college-sports-finance.com/conference/sec); [Ohio State News, Jan 2026](https://news.osu.edu/ohio-state-athletics-sets-record-with-336m-in-fy25-revenue-after-national-championship-season/)
- The NCAA's full-sample benchmark (all 69 autonomy schools including privates, FY2024): median total revenue $149.3M, median generated revenue $133.8M, median expenses $166.8M. The FY2025 public-only median is about $30M higher for two reasons: the privates that are missing are mostly smaller programs (Wake Forest, Boston College, Vanderbilt), and FY2025 was the first full year of the new Big Ten and SEC television contracts.
  - Source link: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, p. 7](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- Where the revenue comes from (NCAA FY2024, autonomy schools): media rights, NCAA and conference distributions and bowl revenue 31%; donor contributions and endowments 26%; ticket sales 17%; university and government support 10%; royalties and licensing 9%; other 6%; student fees 1%.
  - Source link: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, p. 16](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf)
- Conference distributions per full-share school, FY2025: Big Ten $76.1M, SEC $72.4M, ACC about $47M, Big 12 average $39.5M. That single line explains most of the gap between the SEC and Big Ten on one hand and the ACC and Big 12 on the other.
  - Source links: [CBS Sports on Big Ten, May 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/big-ten-1-37-billion-in-revenue-to-its-18-members-for-2025-fiscal-year/); [SEC release, Feb 2026](https://www.secsports.com/news/2026/02/sec-announces-2024-25-revenue-distribution); [Wilner, Bay Area News Group, May 2026](https://www.pressdemocrat.com/2026/05/25/big-12-spring-meetings-financial-reports-revenue-deficit/)
- Scale check: across all 110 public FBS programs in FY2025, revenue was $12.8B against $12.8B of spending, with $11.4B of athletics debt outstanding. The 52 public Power Four schools alone account for $9.55B of that $12.8B.
  - Source link: [Knight-Newhouse FY2025 sheet](https://www.knightcommission.org/wp-content/uploads/KNAD_database_information_FY2025.pdf)
- Revenue growth has not outrun spending growth. Over ten years the median Power school's generated revenue grew 56% while its expenses grew 81%.
  - Source links: [NCAA 10-year trends, Dec 2025, p. 36](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2025D1RES_DI-RevExpReport_Final.pdf); [GAO-26-108640, July 2026](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf)

**On average, how many sports do Power 4 athletic departments sponsor?**

- About 22. Per the NCAA membership directory's "sponsored sports" listing for 2026-27, the 68 Power Four schools sponsor a mean of 21.7 and a median of 21 varsity sports, ranging from 16 (Kansas State, UCF, Mississippi State) to 37 (Stanford, which its own site counts as 36). We pulled all 68 school pages on Oct 27, 2026.
  - Source links: [NCAA membership directory](https://web3.ncaa.org/directory/); [Stanford directory page](https://web3.ncaa.org/directory/orgDetail?id=674); [Kansas State directory page](https://web3.ncaa.org/directory/orgDetail?id=327)
- By conference (mean / median): Big Ten 23.9 / 23 (range 20 to 35, Ohio State); ACC 23.6 / 22 (17 to 37); SEC 20.1 / 21 (16 to 23); Big 12 18.8 / 18 (16 to 26). The two richest conferences are not the broadest: the SEC's biggest brands (Texas, Alabama, Georgia, LSU, Florida, Oklahoma) each sponsor 21.
  - Source link: [NCAA membership directory](https://web3.ncaa.org/directory/)
- Counting basis matters. The NCAA counts men's and women's cross country, indoor track and outdoor track as six separate sports. The federal EADA database lets schools report them as one line, and 67 of 68 Power Four schools do, so EADA shows a mean of 18.1 and median of 17 for 2024-25. Use the NCAA count when comparing to the NCAA minimum.
  - Source link: [EADA data files, 2024-25](https://ope.ed.gov/athletics/#/datafile/list)
- The floor is 16. FBS members must sponsor at least 16 varsity sports including football, with at least six men's or mixed and eight women's sports, and the bylaw allows no waivers. Seventeen Power Four schools sit at 16 to 18 sports, with little room to cut.
  - Source link: [NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27, Bylaw 20.9.9.1 and 20.9.9.5](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008)
- Roster size: the average Power Four department had 589 unduplicated athletes in 2024-25 (median 560); GAO counts 587 per Power school on FY2024 NCAA data. The all-Division I average is 19.2 teams and 577 athletes.
  - Source links: [EADA data files, 2024-25](https://ope.ed.gov/athletics/#/datafile/list); [GAO-26-108640, Table 1](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108640.pdf); [NCAA Sports Sponsorship and Participation Rates Report, Sept 2025](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/sportpart/2025RES_SportsSponsorshipParticipationRatesReport.pdf)
- Only two of those sports pay for themselves. The NCAA's last sport-level medians (FY2016, autonomy schools) showed football +$19.0M and men's basketball +$2.8M net of generated revenue, and every other sport negative: women's basketball −$3.2M, baseball −$1.5M, rowing −$1.5M, volleyball −$1.4M, track −$1.3M, softball −$1.3M. The NCAA has not published sport-level medians since.
  - Source link: [NCAA Revenues and Expenses 2004-2016, Table 4.9](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/Finances/2017D1RES_D1RevExpReportFinal.pdf)
- House changed roster math, not sport counts. Scholarship caps became roster limits on July 1, 2025 (football 105, basketball 15, baseball 34, softball 25, soccer 28, volleyball 18). No Power Four school has dropped a sport citing the settlement; the only recent changes are Utah dropping beach volleyball (April 2025), Iowa State dropping gymnastics and adding women's wrestling (2026), and Nebraska adding flag football.
  - Source links: [NCAA Proposal 2025-11, roster limits](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108635); [Utah Athletics, Apr 2025](https://utahutes.com/news/2025/4/29/utah-athletics-to-discontinue-beach-volleyball-program-at-conclusion-of-academic-year); [Iowa State Athletics, Apr 2026](https://cyclones.com/news/2026/4/16/athletics-iowa-state-adds-womens-wrestling-as-18th-varsity-program); [Huskers.com, Jan 2026](https://huskers.com/news/2026/01/16/nebraska-announces-addition-of-womens-flag-football)
