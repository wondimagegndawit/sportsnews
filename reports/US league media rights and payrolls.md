# Big Four Leagues Capture $21B Media Pie

Across roughly thirty men's and women's professional team leagues in the United States, national media rights are worth about **$21 billion a year**, and the NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL take more than 95 cents of every dollar: the NFL's deals are reported at **~$10 billion a year for its five core partners and ~$12 billion with YouTube's Sunday Ticket and Netflix added**, the NBA's new cycle at **~$6.9–7.0 billion**, MLB's at **~$2.0–2.1 billion**, and the NHL's U.S. deals at **~$625 million** (about $1.27 billion once the new Rogers Canadian deal starts in 2026-27). Everything else combined is worth well under $1 billion: MLS's Apple deal (~$250 million headline, restructured to $200–275 million a year), the WNBA's new portfolio (**~$281 million**, the only women's league with a nine-figure rights value), the NWSL (~$60 million) and Unrivaled (~$17 million), with every other league's fee undisclosed and in most cases closer to a distribution arrangement than a rights payment. Player pay follows the same shape: the NFL's cash payroll was **~$9.65 billion in 2025** (and the league's own 2026 "player costs" figure is $12.1 billion including benefits), NBA salaries and benefits reached **$6.27 billion in 2025-26**, MLB's Opening Day 2026 payroll implies **~$5.0 billion** (or ~$6.1 billion on a luxury-tax basis), the NHL's players' 50% share of revenue puts pay near **$3.0–3.4 billion**, MLS paid **$631 million** in guaranteed compensation in spring 2026, the WNBA roughly **$99 million** under its new $7 million cap, and the PWHL **$11.1 million**. Cap structures split cleanly: the NFL and NHL run hard caps ($301.2 million and $95.5–104 million per team), the NBA and MLS run soft caps that teams blow through via Bird rights, exceptions, Designated Players and allocation money, MLB has no cap at all (a $244 million Competitive Balance Tax threshold instead, with owners demanding a hard cap in a CBA fight that expires December 1, 2026), and the WNBA and NWSL use caps that are hard in principle but carry enumerated exceptions. One interpretive note up front: the request's reference to the "WSL" was read as the **NWSL** (National Women's Soccer League), since the Women's Super League is an English competition; no English league figures are included. A second caveat applies throughout: not one of the big-league media figures below is official. Leagues and networks do not disclose rights fees, so every AAV is a press-reported estimate (Bloomberg, CNBC, WSJ, SBJ, Sportico, Front Office Sports), and total payroll is published by a league or union only in a handful of cases (MLS, PWHL, the NBA's league-accounted figure); elsewhere this report sums third-party trackers or computes a proxy and shows the arithmetic.

## Four leagues take 95 cents of every national media dollar

Summing the best-sourced figures, the NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL generate roughly **$19.5–21.5 billion a year** in national rights, against well under $1 billion for all other U.S. leagues combined. None of these numbers is official; each is a press estimate, and because rights fees typically escalate over a contract's term, an "AAV" is an average rather than the amount paid in the current season.

### NFL: ~$10 billion for the core five, ~$12 billion all-in, with the 2029 opt-out unexercised

The NFL's March 2021 agreements with CBS, Fox, NBC, ESPN/ABC and Amazon run from the 2023 season through 2033 and are reported at a combined ~$110 billion, which SportsPro, citing CNBC in March 2026, still describes as "11-year deals with Amazon, CBS, ESPN, Fox and NBC [that] total US$110 billion" ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/broadcast-ott/nfl-cbs-paramount-media-rights-price-march-2026/)). The per-partner figures that sum to that total are ESPN/ABC ~$2.7 billion, Fox ~$2.2–2.25 billion, CBS $2.1 billion, NBC ~$2.0 billion and Amazon ~$1.0–1.2 billion a year ([Daily Maverick/Bloomberg](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2021-03-18-nfl-signs-105-billion-tv-deal-with-amazon-taking-thursdays/); [Sports Media Watch](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2026/08/murdoch-fox-nfl-renegotiate-closer-2029-opt-out/); [NPR via HPPR](https://www.hppr.org/npr-news/2021-03-19/new-113-billion-nfl-media-rights-deal-gives-fans-more-options-to-watch-games)). The 2021 estimates ranged from Bloomberg's "more than $105 billion" to CNBC's $113 billion, a discrepancy that reflects differing Amazon and escalator assumptions; $110 billion has become the consensus figure used by S&P Kagan and most 2026 coverage ([TheDesk.net/SNL Kagan](https://thedesk.net/2025/08/snl-kagan-tv-rights-study-football-nfl/)). Outside that package, YouTube pays a reported ~$2 billion a year for Sunday Ticket (WSJ; Bloomberg said ~$2.5 billion) under a seven-year deal from 2023 ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/youtube-secures-rights-to-nfl-sunday-package); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/nfl-sunday-ticket-youtube-tv-primetime-channels-2023)), and Netflix paid a reported ~$75 million per game for its original Christmas package ([ProFootballTalk](https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/report-netflix-will-pay-about-75-million-each-for-christmas-games)) before signing an undisclosed four-year extension in May 2026 that expands to five games a season through 2029 ([Sports Media Watch](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2026/05/netflix-nfl-deal-expanded-christmas-thanksgiving-eve-first-last-weeks-season/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/netflix-deepens-its-nfl-ties-with-expanded-five-game-package/)). Adding the pieces (ESPN $2.7B + Fox $2.2B + CBS $2.1B + NBC $2.0B + Amazon $1.0B = $10.0B; plus YouTube ~$2.0B and Netflix ~$0.15B) yields a **best-sourced all-national AAV of roughly $12.0–12.3 billion**, a figure consistent with the Packers' FY2026 national revenue share of $453.2 million per club, which implies ~$14.5 billion in league-wide national revenue once sponsorship and licensing are included ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/packers-earnings-nfl-revenue-media/)). The ESPN–NFL Network transaction that closed January 31, 2026 gave the NFL a 10% equity stake in ESPN (analyst estimates $2.2–3.0 billion) but is not a rights fee ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/media/espn-closes-deal-nfl-network-rights-redzone-giving-nfl-stake-network-rcna256906)). The operative figures have not changed in 2026: the NFL sought roughly $1 billion a year more from Paramount for the CBS package ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/broadcast-ott/nfl-cbs-paramount-media-rights-price-march-2026/)), Fox's Lachlan Murdoch said in August the network "will not be making any amendments to our existing contractual relationship, which extends to the completion of the 2029 season" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/fox-nfl-media-rights-renegotiations-opt-out.html)), and Roger Goodell said in September the league is still "evaluating" whether to repackage its games before the post-2029 opt-out ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/nfl-roger-goodell-tv-packages-deal.html)).

On payroll, the NFL publishes no league-wide total, so the best figures come from trackers and the league's per-club cap announcement. Spotrac's 2025 cash tracker totals **$9,652,450,676** across 1,686 contracts (about $7.31 billion active and $623 million dead money, led by Jacksonville at $351.3 million), though the page returned access errors and the total is taken from a search snapshot ([Spotrac 2025 cash tracker](https://www.spotrac.com/nfl/cash/_/year/2025/sort/cash_total)). Over The Cap's in-progress 2026 table sums to $10.58 billion (this report's addition of 32 team rows; Cowboys $391.4 million highest, Dolphins $246.4 million lowest), a running figure that will change ([Over The Cap](https://overthecap.com/cash-spending)). The league's own number is per club: for 2026 the cap is **$301.2 million plus $77.6 million in benefits, or $378.8 million in total player costs per team**, announced February 27, 2026 ([NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-2026-salary-cap-set-at-301-2-million-per-team)); multiplied by 32 that is **$12.12 billion** of 2026 player costs including benefits, versus a 2025 cap aggregate of 32 × $279.2 million = $8.93 billion. Cash exceeds cap because the cap counts prorated signing bonuses while cash counts them when paid, which is why the Packers' player costs rose $131.7 million in one fiscal year ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/packers-earnings-nfl-revenue-media/)). The cap is unambiguously **hard**: CBA Article 12 Section 9 also imposes a minimum cash spending floor of 90% of the caps over the 2024–26 and 2027–30 periods, with shortfalls paid to players ([Over The Cap CBA text](https://overthecap.com/collective-bargaining-agreement/article/12/section/9)). Players receive a base 48% of All Revenue under the 2020 CBA with a "media kicker" to 48.5–48.8% ([NFL.com CBA guide](https://www.nfl.com/news/guide-to-understanding-proposed-cba-ahead-of-voting-deadline-0ap3000001105649)); which tier is currently in effect is not published. Labor peace runs through March 2031.

### NBA: ~$6.9–7.0 billion a year, no opt-out, and a soft cap teams routinely exceed

The NBA's 11-year deals with Disney, NBCUniversal and Amazon began in 2025-26 and were reported at ~$76 billion by ESPN (Disney ~$2.6 billion, NBC ~$2.5 billion, Amazon ~$1.8 billion a year; official terms not released) ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/40535771/reports-nba-agrees-terms-76-billion-media-rights-deal)). The Wall Street Journal's breakdown, relayed by Sports Media Watch, puts Amazon at $1.9 billion for a $77 billion total, of which ~$75 billion is the NBA and ~$2.2 billion over the term is allocated to the WNBA; unlike the NFL, the NBA deals carry no league opt-out ([Sports Media Watch](https://www.sportsmediawatch.com/2024/07/nba-media-rights-breakdown-who-gets-what-espn-nbc-amazon/)). The two figures bracket the AAV at **$6.9–7.0 billion**, roughly $6.7–6.8 billion attributable to the NBA proper, a ~165% increase on the prior cycle ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/analyst-comment/nba-nets-more-media-value-with-new-domestic-rights/)).

The NBA is the one big league where a league-accounted total compensation figure surfaces, via its escrow reconciliation. Sportico reported that for 2025-26 basketball-related income was **$11.68 billion and total player salaries and benefits were $6.27 billion**, above the players' 51% designated share, so the ~$580 million escrow pool split 54.8% to owners and 45.2% to players, a 5.5% haircut on negotiated salaries ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2026/nba-escrow-players-salaries-owners-million-1234937798/)). For 2024-25, BRI was $10.25 billion and players forfeited more than $480 million of escrow ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-player-salaries-cut-escrow-system-1234858905/)), implying a guaranteed share of about $5.23 billion at 51%. Both Sportico pieces are paywalled and the figures come from search snapshots. Spotrac, HoopsHype and Basketball-Reference roster sums for 2025-26 could not be retrieved, so no independent roster-sum total is available; benefits typically make up several hundred million of the $6.27 billion, so pure salary is likely in the ~$5.7–5.9 billion range (inference, not reported). The structure is a textbook **soft cap**: the official 2025-26 cap is **$154.647 million**, with a tax level of $187.895 million, first apron $195.945 million, second apron $207.824 million and minimum team salary $139.182 million ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-salary-cap-set-2025-26-season)); the 2026-27 cap is $164,961,000 ([Basketball-Reference](https://www.basketball-reference.com/contracts/)). Tax rates run $1.00/$1.25/$3.50/$4.75 per dollar across successive brackets (repeater rates $3.00–$6.75), with proceeds split between the league and non-taxpayers ([Hoops Rumors glossary](https://hoopsrumors.com/2024/11/hoops-rumors-glossary-luxury-tax-penalties-4.html)). The soft-cap character shows in the arithmetic: 30 × $154.647 million is only $4.64 billion and even 30 × the tax line is $5.64 billion, yet the league-accounted $6.27 billion sits above both, and ten teams paid $461.2 million in luxury tax for 2024-25 ([Hoops Rumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/denver-nuggets/page/10)), with a projected seven to eight taxpayers owing ~$221–229 million for 2025-26 ([Sports Business Classroom](https://sportsbusinessclassroom.com/nba-2025-26-luxury-tax-tracker/); [SalarySwish](https://www.salaryswish.com/luxury-tax/2026)). The 2023 CBA runs through June 30, 2030 with a mutual opt-out after 2028-29 ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-and-nbpa-reach-tentative-deal-on-new-collective-bargaining-agreement)).

### MLB: ~$2 billion nationally, ~$5–6 billion in pay, no cap and a lockout looming

MLB's 2026–28 national package has five components. AP reported that the three new three-year deals announced November 19, 2025 "will average nearly $800 million per year" with "ESPN [paying] $550 million while the NBC deal is worth $200 million and Netflix $50 million," alongside legacy deals in which "MLB receives an average of $729 million from Fox and $470 million from Turner Sports per year" through 2028 ([AP via World Baseball Network](https://worldbaseball.com/mlbs-new-media-deals-with-espn-nbc-and-netflix-total-800m-annually-2/)). Two of those numbers are contested: SBJ reported Turner at "around $535 million a year" ([SportBusiness](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/mlb-finalizes-seven-year-rights-extension-with-turner/)), and Forbes put Netflix "in the $35 million a year range" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maurybrown/2025/11/19/mlb-announces-3-year-media-rights-deals-with-espn-nbcuniversal-and-netflix/)). Summing the reported components gives **$1.98–2.06 billion a year**, which matches Ampere Analysis's independent estimate that national deals are worth $2.1 billion in 2026 on top of $1.6 billion in regional rights, for $3.7 billion in total broadcast revenue ([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/business/mlbs-regional-broadcast-rights-worth-usd1-6b-despite-rsn-disruption)). The ESPN headline is not like-for-like with its old $550 million: the new package is anchored on MLB.TV distribution, so CNBC framed it as a ~$300 million haircut on the game rights alone ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/19/mlb-media-rights-deals-nbc-espn-netflix.html)).

No official league-wide payroll total exists. The AP's Opening Day 2026 study found a record average of **$5,335,966 across 942 players** on rosters and injured lists, with the Mets at $352.2 million and Cleveland lowest at $62.3 million, but did not print a total ([AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/sports/2026/04/ap-study-mlb-average-salary-hits-a-record-5-34m-as-the-mets-lead-spending-again/)); 942 × $5,335,966 ≈ **$5.03 billion** is this report's arithmetic and the best "cash on Opening Day" figure. The Baseball Cube's compilation shows $6,130,926,880 for 2026 under a broader methodology that tracks closer to luxury-tax payrolls (its Dodgers figure is $415.2 million versus the AP's $316.6 million) ([The Baseball Cube](https://www.thebaseballcube.com/content/payroll_year/2026/)), so a defensible range for 2026 total compensation is ~$5.0 billion (cash) to ~$6.1 billion (CBT-style). For the completed 2025 season, the commissioner's office's final accounting had nine clubs paying a record $402.6 million in luxury tax, $169.4 million of it by the Dodgers, whose payroll was $345.3 million ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/47360963/dodgers-hit-record-169m-luxury-tax-winning-2nd-straight-ws)). MLB has **no salary cap**; the 2026 Competitive Balance Tax threshold is $244 million with base rates of 20%, 30% and 50% for first, second and third-plus consecutive years over, plus surcharges at $20 million, $40 million and $60 million above the line that take the top marginal rate to 110% ([MLB.com glossary](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/transactions/competitive-balance-tax); [TrueBlueLA](https://www.truebluela.com/dodgers-payroll/108038/dodgers-payroll-competitive-balance-tax-2025)). That may not last: the CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, 2026, owners formally proposed a $245.3 million hard cap and $171.2 million floor for 2027, the union countered with a $300 million CBT threshold and called a cap "institutionalized collusion," and MLB sent clubs a lockout-procedures memo on October 7 ([CBS Sports timeline](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-cba-negotiations-timeline-2026/); [MLB Trade Rumors](https://www.mlbtraderumors.com/2026/10/mlb-sends-memo-to-teams-outlining-lockout-procedures.html)).

### NHL: $625 million in the U.S., doubling with Canada, and a hard cap that just crossed $100 million

The NHL's seven-year U.S. deals (2021-22 through 2027-28) are reported by SBJ at roughly $400 million a year for ESPN/ABC/Hulu and $225 million for Turner ($1.575 billion over seven years), or "more than $625 million per year" combined ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/nhl-inks-seven-year-broadcast-deal-with-turner-sports)); Sportico rounds the pair to "roughly $600 million" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/report-nhl-rogers-reach-12-year-canadian-television-deal)). The league's national revenue steps up sharply in 2026-27 when Rogers' new 12-year Canadian deal, officially C$11 billion and about US$7.7 billion, begins ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/sports/hockey/nhl/rogers-retain-canadian-nhl-rights-april-2-1.7499786); [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/hockey/2025/nhl-rogers-new-media-deal-billion-1234845631/)). On a straight-line basis that is ~US$640 million a year, lifting all-national AAV to roughly **US$1.27 billion**, though the Rogers deal escalates and pays less early.

The NHL publishes no payroll total, but its economics are formulaic: players receive 50% of hockey-related revenue. HRR was about US$6.5 billion in 2024-25 ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/insights/nhl-ice-hockey-business-revenue-sponsorship-media-rights-data/)), Gary Bettman projected $6.8 billion for 2025-26 in December 2025 ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nhl/story/_/id/47258975/nhl-projects-season-revenue-68-billion)) and then "$7.5 billion to $8 billion in mixed U.S. and Canadian currency" in June 2026 ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/nhl-projects-record-8b-in-revenue-sees-bigger-growth-ahead)). Half of those figures gives a players' share of **~$3.25 billion (2024-25) to ~$3.4 billion (2025-26)**, with the mixed-currency figure overstating USD totals. A cap-arithmetic check agrees: 32 teams × the $95.5 million 2025-26 upper limit = $3.06 billion maximum of cap hits, and most clubs sit near the ceiling. Spotrac, PuckPedia and CapWages were inaccessible, so no published 32-team cap-hit sum was captured. The cap is **hard, with a floor**: the NHL and NHLPA jointly announced upper/lower limits of **$95.5M/$70.6M for 2025-26, $104M/$76.9M for 2026-27 and $113.5M/$83.9M for 2027-28** ([NHL.com](https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-nhlpa-announce-team-payroll-ranges-for-next-3-seasons-through-2027-28)), and ratified a four-year CBA extension running from 2026-27 through 2029-30 that adds an 84-game season and a playoff salary cap ([The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/nhl-and-nhlpa-ratify-four-year-cba)).

## MLS's $631 million payroll dwarfs a $6.4 million "cap," and second-tier men's leagues run on minimums

Below the Big Four, MLS is the only men's league with both a reported nine-figure media deal and a published payroll. The rest publish neither: their media arrangements are distribution deals with undisclosed or zero fees, and their pay is set by CBA minimums or small hard caps, so every payroll figure below is a proxy.

### MLS: Apple's restructured $200–275 million, $631 million in guaranteed pay, soft cap

Apple's 10-year, $2.5 billion deal (headline AAV ~$250 million) was recalibrated in November 2025: Season Pass was folded into the standard Apple TV subscription from 2026, the deal now ends after the 2028-29 season instead of 2032, Apple waived its post-2027 opt-out, and Sportico reported payments of **$200 million for 2026, $107.5 million for the shortened 2027 "sprint" season and $275 million each for 2027-28 and 2028-29**, about $50 million more than MLS would have been paid through June 2029 under the old terms ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/soccer/2025/apple-mls-streaming-contract-change-1234876902/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/mls-folding-its-season-pass-shifting-all-games-to-apple-tv-in-2026/); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/11/14/mls-apple-tv-deal-ends-in-2029-years-ahead-of-schedule)). The revised schedule averages ~$214 million over the four payment periods but $275 million on a full-season basis; Fox and TelevisaUnivision's 2023–26 linear fees were never disclosed and no renewal has been reported ([MLSsoccer.com](https://www.mlssoccer.com/news/mls-on-linear-tv-fox-sports-televisaunivision-tsn-rds-reach-multi-year-deals)). MLS is reportedly seeking $400–500 million a year for its next deal from 2029-30 ([Yahoo/Awful Announcing](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/mls-eyes-400-million-tv-160004334.html)).

MLS payroll is the best-documented in this group because the MLSPA publishes player-level data twice a year. The Spring 2026 guide (data as of April 16, 2026) sums to **$630,955,755 in annualized guaranteed compensation** across 30 clubs plus a $6.2 million league pool, an average of $688,816 and median of $352,104, with 133 players at $1 million or more and Inter Miami alone at $54.6 million ([KC Soccer Journal](https://kcsoccerjournal.com/05/12/2026/2026-mls-salaries-by-player-and-team/); [MLSPA](https://mlsplayers.org/resources/salary-guide)). The total is a third-party sum (the MLSPA does not print one), implies ~916 players ($630.96M / $688,816), and the fall 2026 guide had not been released as of October 21. Against that spend, the 2026 Salary Budget is just **$6,425,000** with a maximum budget charge of $803,125, covering only the 20-man senior roster; Designated Players count only $803,125 regardless of salary, clubs receive $3.28 million in General Allocation Money (up to $2 million more under the U22 model) and $2.125 million in Targeted Allocation Money to buy down charges, and 11 supplemental slots sit entirely off budget ([MLSsoccer.com Roster Rules](https://www.mlssoccer.com/about/roster-rules-and-regulations)). Thirty clubs × $6.425 million = $192.75 million of on-budget spend, under a third of actual compensation, which is why the MLS cap is best classified as **soft** even though it has no luxury tax: the binding constraints are slot counts and the max charge, not a payroll ceiling.

### USL Championship, UFL and the football/soccer tier

The USL Championship's U.S. rights sit with CBS Sports through 2027 (around 100 matches a year across CBS, CBS Sports Network, Golazo Network and Paramount+) alongside ESPN platforms and TUDN, with no fee disclosed ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/soccer/news/cbs-sports-reaches-multi-year-rights-agreement-to-air-usl-championship-league-one-matches-through-2027-season)). There is **no salary cap**; the five-year CBA ratified September 4, 2026 sets a $42,000 minimum (including housing) for 2026 with annual increases and a $67,500 minimum for the planned Division One USL Premier ([USLSoccer.com](https://www.uslsoccer.com/news_article/show/1366196)). No club or league payroll is published; a floor-only proxy of 25 clubs × 25 players × $42,000 = **~$26 million** understates actual spend by an unknown multiple. The UFL has no rights fee at all because Fox and ESPN are among its owners, with every game on Fox/FS1 or ABC/ESPN ([theUFL.com](https://www.theufl.com/about)); its CBA pays active players a flat **$64,000 for 2026** ($6,400 a week, housing stipend folded in) across eight teams and 43-man rosters ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/united-football-league/story/_/id/45007604/ufl-ratifies-new-cba-deal-runs-2026-season)), so 8 × 43 × $64,000 = **~$22.0 million** before off-roster pay and quarterback premiums; the CBA expires after 2026 with no successor reported. Indoor football is smaller still: the 14-team IFL streamed 64 games free on Yahoo Sports after a FanDuel Sports Network deal collapsed ([IFL](https://goifl.com/sports/fball/2026/releases/20260225kpli4z)), pays $250–500 per game ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_Football_League)), and a 14 × 25 × $375 × 16-game proxy yields ~$2.1 million; Arena Football One renewed with VICE TV with no pay data available ([theAF1.com](https://www.theaf1.com/news/arena-football-one-kicks-off-2026-season-with-acti)).

### Lacrosse, rugby and cricket: small hard caps, undisclosed ESPN and Willow deals

ESPN renewed with the Premier Lacrosse League for five years from 2026 and took a ~3% equity stake, fee undisclosed ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/25/espn-premier-lacrosse-league-media-deal-equity-stake.html)). The PLL does not publish its current cap; the last public figure is a $735,000 per-team pool (2021-22) of which 98% must be spent ([UB Law Sports Forum](https://ublawsportsforum.com/2021/12/15/premier-lacrosse-league-takes-shape/)), and the league said the cap rises 11% and average compensation 15% for 2026 ([Sticks In Lacrosse](https://www.sticksinlacrosse.com/post/pll-to-open-2026-on-may-9th-with-expanded-summer-schedule-player-compensation-set-to-increase)); if the pool had stayed at $735,000, an 11% rise implies ~$816,000 × 8 teams = **~$6.5 million**, a low-confidence proxy. The National Lacrosse League's October 2025 CBA is explicit: a **hard cap of $810,000 in 2025-26 rising to $885,000 in 2029-30**, a floor of $530,000 to $590,000 and a $42,000 veteran maximum ([USA Lacrosse Magazine](https://www.usalacrosse.com/magazine/professional/nll/nll-nllpa-agree-new-cba-through-2029-30-season)), so 14 teams × $810,000 = **$11.34 million maximum**; its ESPN and TSN deals expired after 2025-26 and Octagon is selling the next cycle ([NLL.com](https://www.nll.com/news/national-lacrosse-league-announces-partnership-with-octagon/)). Major League Rugby shrank to six teams in 2026 and five for 2027, extended with ESPN for one season with no fee ([MajorLeague.Rugby](https://www.majorleague.rugby/news/major-league-rugby-and-espn-extend-broadcast-agreement)), and carries a ~$500,000 per-team hard cap that is only secondary-sourced ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salary_cap)), giving 6 × $500,000 = **~$3.0 million**. Major League Cricket's Willow TV deal (five years from 2023) is undisclosed ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/media/willow-tv-secures-domestic-rights-to-mlc-in-five-year-deal/)); Forbes reported its $1.1 million per-team pool has not changed since launch ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tristanlavalette/2025/06/12/salary-cap-for-americas-major-league-cricket-tipped-to-rise-in-coming-years/)), so 6 × $1.1 million = **$6.6 million**.

### Development leagues: AHL, ECHL, G League and MiLB

None of the four affiliated development leagues has a separable media value. AHL games stream on FloHockey under an undisclosed multi-year extension ([FloSports](https://www.flosports.tv/2024/09/12/american-hockey-league-and-flosports-announce-significant-expansion-of-multi-faceted-media-partnership/)); the AHL has **no cap and no maximum salary**, its 2024-25 minimum was $52,725 ([Pension Plan Puppets](https://www.pensionplanpuppets.com/nhl-contracts-ahl-contracts-echl-contracts-cba-nhlpa-phpa-how-do-nhl-contracts-work-waivers-two-way/)), and the five-year CBA ratified January 21, 2026 raised minimums without publishing them ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/ahl-phpa-agree-to-terms-on-new-cba)); because so many AHL players are paid under NHL two-way contracts, no payroll proxy is meaningful. The ECHL's new CBA, ratified after a brief December 2025 strike, raised the cap 19.8% immediately ([The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/echl-phpa-ratify-new-collective-bargaining-agreement)); the league's FAQ lists a **weekly hard cap of $18,610 for the first 30 days and $18,000 thereafter, a $13,680 floor and $610/$660 weekly minimums** for 2026-27 across 30 teams ([ECHL FAQ](https://echl.com/about/faq)), which at an assumed 26-week season gives 30 × $18,000 × 26 = **~$14.0 million** at the cap. The G League's first multi-year CBA sets a flat **$45,000 standard salary for 2025-26** plus a $5,000 enhanced minimum, rising 3% a year, while two-way players earn $636,435 (half the NBA rookie minimum) on NBA books ([Hoops Rumors](https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2025/10/nba-g-league-ngbpu-announce-collective-bargaining-agreement.html); [NBA G League](https://gleague.nba.com/nba-g-league-101-two-way-contracts)); its games are carried inside the NBA's ESPN/ABC and Prime Video packages ([NBA Communications](https://pr.nba.com/nba-walt-disney-company-nbcuniversal-amazon-prime-video-media-agreements/)), so standalone AAV is effectively zero and a ~31-team × ~10-player × $45–50k proxy gives **~$14–15.5 million** (team and roster counts assumed). Minor League Baseball's 2026 weekly minimums are $870 (Low-A), $1,020 (Double-A) and $1,225 (Triple-A) ([Legion Report](https://legionreport.com/minor-league-baseball-salaries/)); MiLB games are bundled into MLB.TV, now distributed by ESPN, and MiLB's own rights were described as "low seven figures" a year when it hired Octagon ([Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/mlb/minor-league-baseball-hires-octagon-to-sell-its-media-rights.html)). A minimum-only proxy across 120 full-season clubs and 28-man rosters (AAA 30 × 28 × $1,225 × 26 weeks = $26.8M; AA 30 × 28 × $1,020 × 24 = $20.6M; High-A and Low-A at $870 × 60 × 28 × 24 = $35.1M) totals **~$82 million**, a floor that excludes 40-man players and above-minimum deals.

## The WNBA's new CBA quintupled the cap to $7 million on a $281 million media base; the NWSL remains a $60 million league

The two established women's leagues now sit far apart. The WNBA's 2026 media portfolio and seven-year CBA moved it into a different tier, while the NWSL's rights and cap are a fraction of the size and its cap rules are in active dispute. (Again, "WSL" in the original request was interpreted as the NWSL.)

### WNBA: $281 million a year, ~$99 million payroll, a $7 million cap with exceptions

The foundational July 2024 deal with Disney, NBCUniversal and Amazon was valued at about $200 million a year, or $2.2 billion over 11 years ([CNBC via NBC 7](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/wnba-renews-media-rights-deal-with-scripps/3847464/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/wnba-media-deals-top-3b/)). In May 2026 Front Office Sports, citing a source familiar with the deals, reported the full portfolio with Paramount/CBS, Scripps/ION, USA Sports and NBA TV added is worth **$3.1 billion over 11 years, an AAV of $281 million**, about 6.5 times the previous $43 million, with revenue-sharing provisions that can lift the figure and a reset after the 2028 season ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/wnba-media-deals-top-3b/); [Just Women's Sports](https://justwomenssports.com/reads/wnba-media-rights-deal-3-billion-2026/)). That number is single-sourced and unconfirmed by the league, no per-partner values exist for the additions, and the only reported component is ION's prior ~$13 million a year ([Coast Reporter/AP](https://www.coastreporter.net/basketball/surge-in-viewers-leads-ion-tv-to-extend-current-3-year-broadcasting-deal-with-wnba-10807568)). No evidence of a Netflix deal was found.

Payroll has to be summed from a secondary compilation. Her Hoop Stats' cap database, as published by DIRECTV with data as of May 10, 2026, lists all 15 team payrolls from the Liberty's $7,348,510 to the Mystics' $5,161,166, with a stated league average of $6.59 million ([DIRECTV Insider](https://www.directv.com/insider/wnba-payrolls/)); the 15 figures sum to **$98,860,754** (this report's addition), about 94% of the $105 million aggregate cap (15 × $7.0 million) and above the 15 × $5.95 million = $89.25 million aggregate floor. The same source's 2025 table (13 teams, cap $1,507,100) sums to $18,837,447. These are cap allocations at a snapshot date, not audited year-end pay. The new CBA, agreed March 18–20 and ratified by players and the Board of Governors the following week, "creates a revenue-based salary cap directly tied to league and team revenue growth," set at **$7.0 million for 2026** and projected above $11 million by 2032, with a $1.4 million maximum, minimums of $270,000–300,000, two off-cap developmental spots, a season-ending injury exception and a pregnancy/childbirth exception ([WNBA/NBA key elements PDF](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/46/2026/03/KEY-ELEMENTS-OF-THE-TENTATIVE-COLLECTIVE-BARGAINING-AGREEMENT-Final-.pdf); [WNBA.com](https://www.wnba.com/news/wnba-wnbpa-tentative-cba-deal-2026)). From 2027 the cap plus benefits is set at roughly 30.25–30.5% of league revenue plus 12–13% of team revenue, moves at most 13% in 2027 and 10% a year thereafter, and the team minimum is 85% of the cap, or $5.95 million in 2026 ([The IX Basketball](https://www.theixsports.com/features/unpacking-the-new-wnba-collective-bargaining-agreement/); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/wnba/story/_/id/48898370/wnba-new-cba-shows-salary-cap-set-20-player-revenue-share)). No official document calls the cap "hard" or "soft." Under the 2020 CBA it was "a hard cap with very little wiggle room" ([Her Hoop Stats](https://herhoopstats.substack.com/p/wnba-cba-and-salary-cap-explained-a92)), and the 2026 version is best described as **hard in form with enumerated exceptions and no luxury tax**: the Liberty's payroll above $7.0 million in the same source that calls it a hard cap shows those exceptions in use. The league projects more than $1 billion in salaries and benefits over the seven years ([NBA PR](https://pr.nba.com/wnba-and-wnbpa-reach-tentative-deal-on-historic-collective-bargaining-agreement)).

### NWSL: ~$60 million a year, no published payroll, a $3.7 million cap under grievance

The NWSL's 2024–27 package with CBS/Paramount+, ESPN, Amazon Prime Video and Scripps/ION "will pay the NWSL about $60 million a year for four years," per a person familiar who spoke to Fortune, up from ~$1.5 million under the prior CBS deal ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/11/09/national-womens-soccer-league-240-million-tv-contract-amazon-cbs-espn-scripps)); SBJ's sources described the same $60 million as including rights fees, production costs and marketing commitments, so the pure rights component is lower ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/media/report-nwsl-rights-deals-with-espn-cbs-amazon-scripps)). The September 2025 mid-cycle expansion (ESPN from 20 to 36 games, CBS to 38, and a new free partner, Victory+, with 57) came with no financial terms ([IMG](https://www.img.com/our-news/nwsl-announces-new-media-partnerships-expanding-agreements-with-cbs-sports-and-espn-and-a-new-partnership-with-victory)), so the best-supported AAV is **~$60 million plus an undisclosed increment**. No league, union or tracker page with an actual league-wide payroll was accessible, so only bounds can be stated: with the 2026 cap at $3.7 million (a $3.5 million base plus revenue share) ([The Equalizer](https://equalizersoccer.com/quick-update/nwsl-announces-3-7m-team-salary-cap-3-5m-base/)), 16 teams × $3.7 million = **$59.2 million** is the cap-counted ceiling, and the CBA's Team Minimum Spend (minimum salary × 22–26 cap-counting players plus revenue share) implies a floor of about 22 × $50,500 + ~$200,000 ≈ $1.31 million per team, or ~$21 million league-wide ([NWSL CBA §8.13](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf)). The CBA (July 30, 2024 to December 31, 2030) schedules base caps of $3.3M (2025), $3.5M (2026), $4.4M (2027), $4.7M (2028), $4.9M (2029) and $5.1M (2030), each raised by a Team Revenue Share equal to 10% of net national media and sponsorship revenue divided by the number of teams, with minimum salaries climbing from $48,500 in 2025 to $82,500 in 2030 and no maximum salary ([NWSL CBA §8.1, §8.14, §8.16](https://www.nwslplayers.com/_files/ugd/84dade_0b675d2ffb2048f8b89257555a35f231.pdf); [Just Women's Sports](https://justwomenssports.com/reads/nwsl-raises-player-salary-cap-pay-ahead-of-2025-season-kick-off/)). The cap has no luxury tax and is **hard in structure**, but teams may carry up to four minimum-salary cap-exempt contracts and roster-relief contracts outside it, and the league's 2025 High Impact Player rule lets each club spend an extra $1 million above the cap on qualifying stars, which the NWSLPA challenged in a grievance filed January 14, 2026 whose outcome has not been reported ([The Equalizer](https://equalizersoccer.com/2026/01/14/nwslpa-files-grievance-over-new-high-impact-player-rule-calls-for-immediate-rescission/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nwsl-union-files-grievance-over-leagues-rodman-rule-for-star-players/)).

## Beyond the WNBA and NWSL, only the PWHL publishes a payroll

The newer women's leagues share three traits: no disclosed rights fees (most arrangements are free streaming, FAST channels or cable distribution with equity or no payment), no caps in the traditional sense, and no published payroll except in the PWHL's case. Summed best estimates for the latest season across PWHL, Unrivaled, LOVB, MLV, AUSL, USL Super League and WPBL come to roughly $45–55 million, less than a tenth of MLS's payroll.

### PWHL: $11.1 million official payroll, an average-salary target instead of a cap, no U.S. rights fee

The PWHLPA's first salary disclosure (May 26, 2026) shows the eight-team league spent **$11,110,938.65 on 2025-26 base salaries across 194 contracts**, from Montréal's $1,446,300 to Vancouver's $1,327,909.50, with Emily Clark's $126,090 the top salary and 17 players at the $37,131.50 minimum ([The IX/The Ice Garden](https://www.theixsports.com/the-ice-garden/pwhl/pwhlpa-releases-2025-26-salary-data-pwhl-womens-hockey/)). The CBA through 2031 has **no salary cap and no maximum salary**; instead each team's average salary must hit a target of $58,349.50 in 2025-26 and $60,099.99 in 2026-27, varying by up to 10% for player movement, with the minimum rising 3% a year to $38,245.45 ([Yahoo Sports/Boston Globe](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/pwhl-players-association-releases-salaries-180106170.html)); launch-era descriptions of a "$1.3 million team cap" ([NESN](https://nesn.com/pwhl/news/how-pwhl-teams-are-built-roster-rules-salary-cap-basics-draft-system/aa6450784cd9a3b306957604)) are superseded by the union's 2026 materials. With 12 teams in 2026-27 and a roster size still undecided as of mid-October ([The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/womens/pwhl/pwhl-has-yet-to-announce-2026-27-roster-size-with-camps-closing-in)), the payroll proxy is 12 × $60,099.99 × 23–25 players = **~$16.6–18.0 million**. The league had no national U.S. TV partner for three seasons, streaming free on YouTube with regional RSN and over-the-air deals ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/heading-into-pwhl-third-season-no-major-us-tv-deal-amy-scheer-streaming)); its first season-long national U.S. package, with Scripps' ION and Scripps Sports Network for 2026-27, was announced September 9, 2026 with no financial terms ([PWHL](https://www.thepwhl.com/en/news/2026/september/09/pwhl-and-scripps-sports-announce-national-us-television-partnership-for-2026-27-season)), and Canadian TSN/Sportsnet/CBC/Prime Video fees are equally undisclosed. Media AAV is therefore not reported and likely minimal.

### Unrivaled, LOVB, Major League Volleyball and AUSL

Unrivaled is the only league in this group with a reported eight-figure rights value: SBJ described its TNT Sports deal as "a guaranteed three-year deal with a three-year WBD option that could take the total six-year value to approximately $100M," with WBD holding equity ([Sportskeeda citing SBJ](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/wnba/what-unrivaled-basketball-league-tv-deal); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/unrivaled-ratings-wbd-tnt-basketball/)), or **~$16.7 million a year** if the option is exercised. Its 2025 average salary was reported at $220,000 (the league itself said $222,222) across 36 players ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/unrivaled-has-signed-nearly-90-of-2026-roster-salaries-to-increase/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/01/15/unrivaled-womens-basketball-wnba-teams-schedule)), so 36 × $220,000–222,222 = **~$7.9–8.0 million**; FOS separately reported "$7 million or more" on salaries against $27 million of first-year revenue ([SI summarizing FOS](https://www.si.com/onsi/womens-fastbreak/news/unrivaled-revenue-injury-insurance-details-from-inaugural-season-revealed-01jr67mtbsfx)). The 2026 season expanded to 48 rostered players plus a six-player pool, every player earning at least six figures and salaries "going up" without a published average ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/wnba/news/2026-unrivaled-preview-rosters-expansion-schedule-pay-season-two/)), implying more than $10.5 million; there is no cap, and the league's valuation reached $650 million in August 2026 with players holding ~30% ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/unrivaled-valued-at-650m-after-latest-funding-round/)). LOVB Pro's ESPN (2024), Versant/USA Network (2025) and Victory+ (2025) deals are all undisclosed ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/09/espn-league-one-volleyball-media-deal.html); [LOVB](https://lovb.com/press/versant-lovb-announce-multiyear-media-rights-agreement); [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/news/lovb-gains-fta-coverage-with-victory-deal/)); a CNBC Sport profile reportedly valued ESPN's rights at $5–10 million and Versant's at $1–2 million a year, but the page could not be read and those figures are unverified ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/20/cnbc-sport-lovb-pro-volleyball-next-breakthrough-sport.html)). LOVB publishes no cap, average or payroll; its $60,000 starting salary ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/other-sports/2025/league-one-volleyball-lovb-teams-expansion-cities-1234823977/)) and 13-player rosters ([WOWT](https://www.wowt.com/2026/07/13/lovb-nebraska-unveils-13-player-roster-2026-27-season/)) give a floor of 6 × 13 × $60,000 = **$4.68 million** for 2026 and $7.8 million at 10 teams in 2026-27. Major League Volleyball, which absorbed the Pro Volleyball Federation in August 2025 at a combined valuation above $325 million ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/mlv-pvf-us-volleyball-merger-leagues-august-2025/)), is the one league here with a formally **soft** structure: its October 5, 2026 Marquee Player Exception lets each team designate two players whose pay sits outside "the standard salary cap, subject to the league's luxury tax," though neither the cap nor the tax rate has been published ([MLV](https://mlvb.com/news/2026/10/major-league-volleyball-introduces-marquee-player-exception)). The league's own pay release says all 14 rostered players earn $60,000 or more, top base salaries reach $175,000 and "total compensation exceeds $1.3 million per team" ([MLV/PVF](https://mlvb.com/news/2024/03/player-compensation-increases-announced-for-2025)), so 8 teams × $1.3 million = **~$10.4 million+** in 2026 (salary-only floor 8 × 14 × $60,000 = $6.72 million), rising to ~$15.6 million at 12 teams in 2027; its CBS Sports Network, Roku, ION and Vice TV distribution carries no disclosed fees ([MLV](https://provolleyball.com/news/2025/12/mlv-announces-extensive-2026-broadcast-schedule)). Athletes Unlimited's AUSL softball league pays an average of $35,000 with a maximum of $80,000 per the league's site, lower than the $45,000 average projected at launch ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/espn/story/_/id/50178712/pro-women-softball-players-unionize-ask-ausl-recognition); [AU Pro Sports](https://auprosports.com/read/the-3-biggest-takeaways-from-the-ausl-launch/)); six teams with 17–22 listed players give **~$3.6–4.6 million** for 2026, with no team cap (salaries are set centrally), undisclosed ESPN/CBS Sports Network/MLB Network terms ([AUSL](https://theausl.com/news/ausl-announces-2026-broadcast-schedule/)), a newly formed players' association seeking a CBA for 2027, and AU's volleyball and basketball properties paused ([AU Pro Sports](https://auprosports.com/read/official-update-au-pro-basketball-volleyball/)).

## Where sources disagree and where no number exists

The discrepancies cluster in the biggest numbers. The NFL's package was reported at $105 billion (Bloomberg), $110 billion (SBJ/consensus) and $113 billion (CNBC/NPR) in 2021, and Fox's fee at $2.0, $2.2 or $2.25 billion depending on the outlet; the Sunday Ticket fee spans $2.0–2.5 billion; and Netflix's 2026 extension has no reported value at all. The NBA's total is $76 or $77 billion depending on whether Amazon pays $1.8 or $1.9 billion and whether the WNBA's ~$2.2 billion allocation is counted. MLB's Turner fee is $470 million (AP) or $535 million (SBJ), and Netflix's is $50 million (AP/CNBC) or $35 million (Forbes), a combined spread of ~$80 million a year. The NWSL's $60 million is variously a rights fee (Fortune) or a bundle including production and marketing (SBJ), and a separate SBJ-attributed line called the deals "low eight figures per year." The WNBA's $281 million rests on a single FOS source. On payroll, the choice of basis matters more than any source conflict: the NFL is $8.9 billion (cap aggregate), $9.65 billion (cash) or $12.1 billion (league "player costs" with benefits); the NBA's $6.27 billion includes benefits while cap aggregates are $4.6–5.6 billion; MLB is $5.0 billion on the AP's Opening Day basis or $6.1 billion on a CBT basis. On caps, the MLS, WNBA and NWSL cases show that "hard" and "soft" are not binary: MLS has no luxury tax yet is functionally soft because most pay sits off-budget, while the WNBA and NWSL caps are binding ceilings that nonetheless carry exceptions the strongest teams use.

The gaps are equally structured. No league or network has officially disclosed a rights fee for any deal covered here. No U.S. league publishes an audited league-wide payroll except through union salary guides (MLS, PWHL) or escrow accounting (NBA); the NFL, MLB, NHL, WNBA and NWSL totals are tracker sums, averages multiplied out, or cap bounds, and Spotrac, Over The Cap (2025), HoopsHype, PuckPedia and CapWages all blocked retrieval. The NHL's final 2025-26 HRR, the NBA's final 2025-26 luxury-tax total, the MLSPA's fall 2026 guide, the PLL's current cap figure, MLV's cap and tax rate, AHL's new minimums, the AUSL salary schedule, WLL and WER pay, and the NWSLPA grievance outcome are all unpublished as of October 21, 2026. Where a proxy is given, it is a floor (USL Championship, LOVB, USL Super League, MiLB) or a cap-maximum (NLL, MLR, MLC, ECHL, WPBL), and the true figure sits an unknown distance from it.

## Conclusion

The U.S. sports economy is a four-league economy with a long tail. About $21 billion in national media money and roughly $25 billion in player pay concentrate in the NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL, and the ratio between the two is remarkably stable across them: players collect something close to half of revenue in each league with a cap (NFL 48%, NBA 51%, NHL 50%), which is exactly the arrangement MLB owners are now trying to impose by proposing a $245.3 million hard cap before the December 1 deadline. If they succeed, every big U.S. league will tie payroll to a revenue formula, and the only remaining "no cap" leagues will be the ones too small to need one.

The more consequential shift is below the Big Four. The WNBA's $281 million media portfolio and revenue-indexed $7 million cap mean its payroll jumped roughly fivefold in one year, from ~$19 million to ~$99 million, making it the first women's league whose economics resemble a scaled-down NBA rather than a start-up. The NWSL, at $60 million in media and a $3.7 million cap, is now the league under pressure, and its High Impact Player fight is a preview of how caps in growing leagues get stretched. Everywhere else, the honest answer to "what is the media AAV" is that there isn't one: the UFL, G League, MiLB and WLL are bundled into or owned by their broadcasters, and the PWHL, WER, WPBL and USL Super League are paying for reach rather than being paid for it. For those leagues, the salary floor in the CBA, not the cap, is the number that determines what players actually earn.
